The power's out in half the house, the fridge has gone quiet, and the safety switch has dropped again. It's annoying, but it's the switch doing its job: it cuts the power when electricity is leaking somewhere it shouldn't. Most of the time the cause is one appliance, and you can find it yourself in about 10 minutes.

**If you've had a shock or tingle from anything, or you can smell burning, stop here.** Don't use the appliance or reset the switch. Call a licensed electrician, or your electricity distributor if there's danger.

## How to find what's tripping it

You only need to touch the switch and your plugs. Don't take any covers off.

1. **Check your hands are dry and the floor is dry.** If the switchboard is wet, or water is anywhere near it, don't touch it. Call an electrician.
2. **See which switch has dropped.** It's usually marked "RCD", "safety switch" or "test", with a small test button.
3. **Turn off and unplug everything on that circuit.** If you don't know what's on it, work through the part of the house that's lost power, including the fridge, the washing machine and anything outside.
4. **Flick the safety switch back on.**
5. **If it stays on,** plug appliances back in and turn them on, one at a time, waiting a little after each one.
6. **When it trips again,** the last thing you plugged in is most likely the fault. Leave it unplugged and don't use it until it's been repaired by a professional or replaced.

Some faults only show up when an appliance is working hard, such as a washing machine on its heating cycle or a fridge when its compressor starts. If it trips hours later, try leaving the suspect out for a full day.

**If it trips with everything unplugged, or it won't reset at all,** the fault is likely in the fixed wiring or something wired in, such as an oven, a hot water system or outdoor lights. That's a job for a licensed electrician. Keep it switched off.

## The usual suspects

These are the common culprits, roughly in order of where to look first:

- **Anything old with a heating element:** kettles, toasters, heaters, irons and dishwashers.
- **Anything that lives near water:** washing machines, fridges, and appliances in laundries and bathrooms.
- **Outdoor gear and power points:** extension leads left out in the rain, pool pumps, garden lights and outdoor power points. Tripping that starts after heavy rain often points here.
- **Too many appliances at once:** an overloaded circuit often trips a circuit breaker rather than the safety switch, but regular tripping of either one is worth having checked.
- **A one-off:** a storm or a temporary fault can trip it once. If it resets and stays on, it may never happen again.

## Safety switch or circuit breaker?

They sit side by side and do different jobs:

| Device | What it protects | What a trip usually means |
|---|---|---|
| Safety switch (RCD) | People, from electric shock | Electricity is leaking, often through a faulty appliance or wiring |
| Circuit breaker | Wiring, from overheating | Too much load on the circuit, or a short circuit |

A safety switch that trips is treated as the more serious of the two, because it points to a possible leakage fault. If you're not sure which one tripped, take a photo of the switchboard before you touch anything. The down switch tells your electrician a lot.

## What you must never do

- **Don't tape, hold or bypass a safety switch** so it stays on. It's the thing stopping someone getting a fatal shock.
- **Don't keep resetting it** to get through the day if it keeps tripping. Find the cause, or call someone.
- **Don't open the switchboard or a power point.** In Australia, you can generally reset switches and change light globes yourself. Other electrical work must be done by a licensed electrician.

## When to call a licensed electrician

- It won't reset, or trips with everything unplugged.
- It keeps tripping and you can't find a cause.
- You've felt a shock or tingle from anything in the house.
- You can smell burning, hear buzzing, or see scorch marks at the switchboard or a power point.
- The test button doesn't turn it off.
- Your old switchboard has no safety switch on some circuits, and you'd like them covered.

## Test yours while you're there

Test each safety switch regularly, because one that no longer works gives no warning. Tell everyone at home first so nobody loses their work, then press the test button. The switch should drop and the power to that circuit should go off. Turn it back on. If it doesn't drop, it's faulty, and a licensed electrician needs to replace it.

Queensland and Victoria recommend testing every three months, and NSW guidance says every six months. Three months is the simple one to follow, and it's on our [home maintenance checklist](/guides/home-maintenance/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my safety switch trip for no reason?**
There's usually a reason, but it can be hard to see: an appliance that only faults when it heats up, damp in an outdoor power point after rain, or a one-off from a storm. If it keeps happening and you can't find the cause, call a licensed electrician.

**Why did my safety switch trip when it rained?**
Water getting into an outdoor power point, light or extension lead is a common cause. Unplug what's outside and try again. If it still trips, have an electrician check it, and keep away from anything wet.

**Can a faulty fridge trip a safety switch?**
Yes. It may only trip when the compressor kicks in, so it can take a while to show up. Use the one-at-a-time test, and give anything perishable a thought while it's off.

**Can a safety switch go faulty?**
Yes. A safety switch that won't reset, or that doesn't drop when you press the test button, may be faulty. It needs replacing by a licensed electrician.

**Can I replace a safety switch myself?**
No. Work inside a switchboard must be done by a licensed electrician in Australia.
