## Where the dirty water goes matters

Outside drains, including the grate at the bottom of your driveway and the gutter in the street, are **stormwater, not sewer**. South Australia's EPA explains that stormwater **flows untreated** into creeks, rivers, wetlands and the sea.

In SA, the Environment Protection (Water Quality) Policy 2015 says certain pollutants **must not reach the stormwater system from your property**. The list includes:

- **high-pressure water blasting waste**
- **cleaning agents and detergents**
- **soil, clay, gravel or sand**
- green waste like leaves and lawn clippings

Breaking the rules can mean a **$300 fine**, an Environment Protection Order or prosecution. Other states and councils have similar rules. In Queensland, for example, Ipswich City Council's local law says you can't let waste water run onto a neighbour's land or the road so that it causes a nuisance.

## How to do it

1. **Sweep and weed first.** Get leaves, dirt and loose sand up and into the green bin, so it isn't washed into the drain.
2. **Plan the run-off.** Work so the water flows onto the lawn or a garden bed. If it can only run to the driveway grate or the street, keep it to plain water and as little of it as you can, or ask your council what it allows.
3. **Try plain water first.** Most grime lifts with water alone. If you need a cleaner, choose one for pavers, follow the label, and keep it and its rinse water away from drains.
4. **Test a corner.** Start with a wide fan nozzle, held well back, and move in until it cleans without marking the surface.
5. **Keep the wand moving** at a low angle and a steady distance. Holding it still or too close can etch soft pavers and blast the sand out of the joints.
6. **Rinse the grime one way,** toward the lawn, not back over the clean section.
7. **Top up the joint sand** once the pavers are completely dry, sweeping it into the joints. Our [paver base guide](/guides/materials/paver-base-road-base-and-sand/) covers the sand.
8. **Seal if you want to,** a day or two later, following the sealer's instructions.

## Watch out for

- **Sealed or old pavers:** the sealer can peel under high pressure. Test first.
- **Joints with weeds:** pull them before washing, or the roots stay and they grow straight back.
- **Windows and doors:** spray away from them, and cover nearby outdoor power points.
- **Slip risk:** wet pavers with algae are slippery. Wear grippy shoes.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I clean pavers without a pressure washer?**
Sweep them, wet them, then scrub a section at a time with a stiff broom and plain water. It takes longer but is gentler on the joint sand. Keep detergents and chemicals out of the run-off: South Australia's EPA lists cleaning agents among the things that must not reach stormwater.

**Can I wash the driveway into the street?**
Not with detergent, sand or blasting waste. In SA the EPA bans those from stormwater, and other states and councils have similar rules. Sweep first and direct the water onto grass.

**Will pressure washing damage my pavers?**
It can if the nozzle is too close or held in one place. Use a wide fan, test a corner, and keep moving.

**When should I re-sand the joints?**
When the pavers are fully dry, usually the next sunny day.
