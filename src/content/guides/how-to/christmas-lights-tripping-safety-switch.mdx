The display looked perfect at 8 pm. At 9 the house went dark, again. A safety switch that keeps dropping when the Christmas lights are on is doing exactly its job: it has found electricity leaking somewhere it shouldn't. The trick is finding where.

## Why Christmas lights trip safety switches

A safety switch (an RCD) cuts the power when it detects current leaking away, for example through water or damaged insulation. Outdoor Christmas lights give it plenty of chances:

- **Water in a connection.** A plug, power board or join sitting in the garden or a gutter fills up after rain or the sprinklers. Tripping that starts after rain points here.
- **An indoor-only transformer outside.** Queensland's Electrical Safety Office says some outdoor light sets need their transformer kept indoors, away from the weather.
- **Indoor lights used outside.** The ESO says indoor lights aren't safe outdoors, even on a veranda, because they're not weatherproof.
- **Damage.** A lead crushed in a door or window, chewed by a pet, or cut by a sharp gutter bracket.
- **Old sets.** Lights that have spent a few years in a shed can crack and let moisture in.

A circuit breaker tripping (rather than the safety switch) more often means **too much load**. The ESO warns that multiple high-powered lamps can overload a circuit.

## Find the culprit, one at a time

1. **Turn the display off** and unplug every set, lead and power board at the wall.
2. **Reset the safety switch** at the switchboard.
3. **Plug in one set at a time**, waiting a minute or two between each.
4. **When it trips,** the last thing you plugged in is your suspect. Swap the lead it's on to rule out the lead.
5. **Check the suspect in daylight:** look for cracks, scorch marks, water in the plug or transformer, and damage along the wire.

If it trips with **everything unplugged**, the fault isn't the lights. Stop there and call a licensed electrician. Our [safety switch guide](/guides/how-to/why-does-my-safety-switch-keep-tripping/) covers that side.

## Fixes that work

- **Throw out damaged sets.** Don't tape or repair them.
- **Weatherproof every outdoor connection.** The ESO says outdoor connections must be weatherproof, and you can buy weatherproof covers for plugs and boards.
- **Get plugs and boards off the ground** and out of gutters and garden beds.
- **Bring indoor-only transformers inside** and run the low-voltage lead out through a gap that won't crush it.
- **Use outdoor-rated factory-made leads** and unwind them fully.
- **Spread high-powered lights** across circuits, or swap them for LED.
- **Turn the display off when it rains**, as the ESO recommends.

## What never to do

- **Never tape, hold or bypass the safety switch** to keep the lights on. It's the thing stopping someone getting a shock from a wet lead.
- **Don't keep resetting it** in the rain. Turn the display off until it's dry and you've found the cause.
- **Don't open up the switchboard or a power point.** That's licensed electrical work.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do my Christmas lights trip the power when it rains?**
Usually water has got into an outdoor plug, power board, transformer or join. Weatherproof the connections, get them off the ground, and turn the display off in rain.

**Can I just use a different power point?**
If the fault is in the lights, they'll trip that circuit too. Find the faulty set first.

**Is it the safety switch or the circuit breaker?**
A safety switch trips on current leakage, often water or damage. A circuit breaker trips on overload or a short circuit, such as too many high-powered lamps on one circuit.

**When should I call an electrician?**
If it trips with everything unplugged, won't reset, or you've felt a tingle or smelled burning.
