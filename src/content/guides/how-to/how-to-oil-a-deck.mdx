## How often does a deck need oiling?

WoodSolutions, the Australian timber industry's technical resource, says **every deck needs a finish**. Left bare, timber fades to **silver grey** and can **distort, split and develop surface checks**.

For decking oils and stains, it says **reapplication is generally needed every two to five years**, depending on the product and how exposed the deck is. A deck in full sun and rain needs it sooner than one under a roof.

**Signs it's due:** water soaks in rather than beading, the colour has gone grey or patchy, and the surface feels dry or furry.

## Choosing the oil

WoodSolutions says decking oils and stains are popular because they give a **natural, semi-transparent finish**. Both oil-based and water-based versions are available, often with mould inhibitors, and it recommends **lighter pigments** because they absorb less heat. If the deck already has a finish, stay with the same type, or check the new product is compatible.

## What you'll need

- Broom, and a scraper or putty knife for the gaps
- Deck cleaner and a stiff brush
- Sandpaper or a sander (for rough spots)
- Decking oil, enough for the coats on the label
- An applicator pad, brush or roller, and a small brush for edges
- Drop sheet and painter's tape for the house wall

## Step by step

1. **Clear and sweep.** Move everything off and sweep thoroughly. WoodSolutions says decks should be **thoroughly cleaned and debris removed from between boards** before recoating.
2. **Clean.** Scrub with a deck cleaner, following its instructions, and rinse. Grey timber may need a timber brightener.
3. **Let it dry.** Give it a couple of dry days so the timber can take the oil.
4. **Sand where needed.** WoodSolutions notes some finishes need sanding before recoating. At least sand any rough, furry or splintered areas, then sweep the dust off.
5. **Check the boards.** Punch down any proud nails and tighten loose screws. Replace any soft or split boards.
6. **Pick the weather.** A mild, dry day, with no rain forecast until it's dry. Avoid hot midday sun, which can dry the oil too fast and leave lap marks.
7. **Oil two or three boards at a time,** along their full length, so the edges stay wet. Work toward your exit.
8. **Keep coats thin** and wipe up any puddles before they go sticky.
9. **Second coat** after the drying time on the label.
10. **Stay off it** until the label says it's ready for feet, then furniture.

## Keep it looking good for longer

WoodSolutions suggests:

- **sweep rather than hose** to clean it
- keep pot plants **on trays**, so they don't overflow onto the boards
- avoid shrubs that **permanently shade the deck**, and creepers on the railings
- let it **dry quickly** after rain or watering

## Frequently asked questions

**How many coats of deck oil do I need?**
Usually two thin coats, but follow the tin. Thin coats soak in and dry properly. A thick coat stays tacky and wears unevenly.

**How long does deck oil need before rain?**
Check the tin for its drying time, and only start when the forecast is dry for at least that long. Rain on fresh oil can leave it patchy.

**How often should I oil my deck?**
WoodSolutions says decking oils and stains generally need reapplying every two to five years, depending on the product and the exposure.

**Do I have to sand before re-oiling?**
Not always. WoodSolutions says some finishes need sanding before recoating. Clean it first, then sand any rough or grey patches.

**Can I oil a deck in summer?**
Yes, but early in the day or on a mild one. Hot midday sun can dry the oil too fast.
