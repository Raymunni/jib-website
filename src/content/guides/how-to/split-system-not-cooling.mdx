It's the first properly hot day, the aircon's on, air is coming out, and the room still feels like an oven. Before you book anyone, these checks fix a surprising number of "broken" split systems, and they take about 15 minutes.

## 1. Check the mode on the remote

The most common cause is the simplest one. Look for the **snowflake** (Cool).

- **Fan** only moves air. It doesn't cool.
- **Dry** takes moisture out of the air. Mitsubishi notes it stops removing moisture once the room is within about 2°C of the set point, so it won't keep cooling a hot room.
- **Heat** blows warm air. Easy to leave on after winter.
- **Auto** chooses heating or cooling for you, depending on the temperatures.

Then check the **set temperature** is well below the room temperature, and that no timer, sleep or eco setting is limiting it. If the remote's batteries are low, it may not be sending what the screen shows.

## 2. Give it time (it may be working normally)

Inverter units, which most modern splits are, run hard to reach the set temperature, then slow down to hold it. Mitsubishi says that's normal. If the room got close to the set point and the airflow eased off, it's doing its job.

Close doors and windows to the room you're cooling and draw the blinds on sunny windows. A unit sized for a bedroom won't cool an open-plan living area on a 40°C day. Mitsubishi says incorrect sizing leads to temperature control problems.

## 3. Clean the filters

Blocked filters choke the airflow. Daikin says to clean them every **two weeks**, more often in dusty homes, and Mitsubishi says every **two to four weeks** with regular use. Daikin puts the energy saving from a clean filter at **5% to 15%**.

1. Turn the unit off and isolate the power.
2. Lift the front panel and slide out the filters.
3. Vacuum them, or wash in lukewarm water.
4. Let them dry fully in the shade, then refit.

Never spray water into the unit itself. Mitsubishi is clear that water must not go on any of the inner workings. Our [split system cleaning guide](/guides/home-maintenance/how-to-clean-a-split-system-air-conditioner/) has the full step by step.

## 4. Check the outdoor unit

The outdoor unit gets rid of the heat from inside. If it can't breathe, the indoor unit can't cool.

- Clear plants, weeds, bins, covers and stored stuff from around it.
- Check the fan is spinning when the system is running in Cool.
- Look for leaves or dust packed against the fins at the back.

Mitsubishi lists "no barriers erected around the outdoor unit" among the first things to check.

## 5. Reset it

Turn it off at the remote, then switch off the isolation switch (usually on the wall near the outdoor unit) or the circuit at the switchboard. Wait a few minutes, turn it back on, and set it to Cool again.

## When to call a technician

Mitsubishi lists these as signs a service is due:

- It struggles in hot weather even with clean filters
- It isn't blowing enough air
- A whistling noise, or an odour
- Dust building up on the louvres and vents
- It's been a year or more since it was installed or last serviced

Also call someone if the indoor unit shows an error code or flashing light, water drips from the indoor unit, or the outdoor fan won't run.

**Gas is a licensed job.** Under federal rules, anyone who installs, services or repairs an air conditioner must hold a Refrigerant Handling Licence (the ARCtick). If the system is low on refrigerant, there's a leak to find and fix, not just a top-up to buy.

When you book, give the technician the brand and model (on a sticker on the side of the indoor unit), what you've already checked, any error code, and photos of both units. It saves a visit.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is my aircon blowing but not cooling?**
Usually the wrong mode (Fan, Dry, Heat or Auto), blocked filters, or a blocked outdoor unit. If those are fine, it needs a technician.

**How often should I clean my split system filters?**
Every two weeks according to Daikin, or every two to four weeks with regular use according to Mitsubishi. More often in dusty homes.

**Can I re-gas my own air conditioner?**
No. Handling refrigerant requires an ARCtick Refrigerant Handling Licence.

**How often should a split system be serviced?**
Mitsubishi suggests a professional service if it's been a year or more since installation, along with regular filter cleaning.
