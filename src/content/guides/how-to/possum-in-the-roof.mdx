Thumping across the ceiling at 2 am, hissing, a smell in the roof space. In most Australian suburbs that's a brushtail possum, and it has picked your roof because it's warm, dry and easy to get into. You can get it out. You just can't do whatever you like, because possums are protected wildlife in every state.

## The rules everywhere

- **Don't harm it.** Possums are protected native animals.
- **Don't relocate it.** Possums are territorial. NSW, Victoria and SA all say relocated possums usually die, fighting resident possums for food and shelter. Moving one to the bush is illegal as well as cruel.
- **Don't seal one in.** Block entry points only once you're sure it has gone out for the night.

## State by state

| State | Can an owner trap it? | Release rules |
|---|---|---|
| NSW | Not without a licence. Hire a licensed catch and release operator, or apply for a landholder's licence once other options have failed | At dusk, no more than 150 m from capture, near a tree or tall fence |
| Victoria | Yes, owners and occupiers can trap a brushtail possum living in their building. Ringtails need a permit | Same property, within 50 m, after sunset on the day of capture |
| SA | Only with a free trap and release permit, or a permitted possum controller. Only possums living in a building | Same property, within 50 m, after dusk and within 24 hours |
| Queensland | Use a licensed pest controller | Within 25 m of capture |
| Tasmania | Only with a permit, as a last resort | Trapping and relocation generally doesn't work |

In **Victoria**, traps must not injure the possum (mesh under 19 mm square is ideal), must be checked within two hours of sunrise, and the possum kept covered in a quiet place until release. If you can't release it on your property, it must go to a vet within 24 hours. Breaches can bring penalties of up to **$9,246** under the Wildlife Act, and up to **$44,380** under the animal cruelty rules.

In **SA**, the permit is free and processing can take up to 4 weeks. Apply before you borrow or hire a trap.

## The fix that works: rehouse, then block

Removing the possum alone doesn't solve anything. Another one moves into the empty space. The steps NSW and Queensland both recommend:

1. **Find the way in.** Watch at dusk, when it leaves to feed. Queensland suggests stuffing loose wads of paper into likely gaps during the day; the one pushed out overnight is the entry. NSW suggests flour near the gap to check footprints, in case it's actually rats.
2. **Put up a nest box** in a tree in your yard. NSW says at least 4 m off the ground; Victoria says at least 3 m. If you can safely reach the possum's nest in the roof, put some of the nesting material in the new box.
3. **Encourage the move.** NSW suggests a light left on in the roof space for 3 days and nights and quassia chips sprinkled in the cavity, with fruit near the new box.
4. **Block the entry at night**, once the possum has gone out. Queensland suggests between 8 and 10 pm. A one-way flap hinged at the top lets it out but not back in.
5. **Kill the scent.** Queensland suggests washing the old entry with a strong disinfectant so it doesn't try to get back to its den.
6. **Cut off the highways.** Victoria suggests pruning branches back at least 1.5 m from the roof, and sheet metal collars on tree trunks.

If it comes back, there's another way in. Find it and repeat.

## Watch for babies

Brushtail possums have young in autumn or spring. Victoria notes a joey spends about six months in the pouch, then one to two months riding on its mother's back. If you see young, wait before blocking the entry, and contact a local wildlife rescue group if you find a joey on its own.

## When to get help

- You can't find the entry, or there are several possums
- The entry is high or on a two-storey roof
- There's damage to the ceiling, wiring or insulation
- You're in NSW or SA and trapping is the only option left

Ask any possum remover for their licence or permit, and get them to fix the entry points as part of the job. That's what stops it coming back.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it illegal to remove a possum from your roof?**
No, but how you do it is regulated. You can't harm or relocate a possum, and trapping needs a licence or permit in most states.

**Can I trap a possum myself in Victoria?**
Yes, owners and occupiers can trap a brushtail possum living in their building, but it must be released on the same property, within 50 m, after sunset on the day of capture.

**How far away can you release a possum?**
50 m in Victoria and SA, 150 m in NSW, and 25 m in Queensland, always on or near the property where it was caught.

**How do I stop a possum coming back?**
Give it a nest box in a nearby tree, block the entry once it has gone out at night, remove the scent, and cut back branches touching the roof.
