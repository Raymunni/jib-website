Not every house has air con, and the ones that do don't need to run it all day. Most of what keeps a house cool is stopping the heat getting in. These are the steps from the Australian Government's energy advice, cheapest first.

## During the day: shut the heat out

energy.gov.au's first tip: **close windows, curtains, blinds and doors** during the hottest part of the day so sunlight doesn't get in. The sunniest windows matter most, usually west in the afternoon.

- **Close internal doors** to the rooms you're not using.
- **Use a door snake** to stop hot air getting in under external doors.
- **Roll up the rugs.** energy.gov.au says they insulate the floor and hold heat. Bare tiles and floorboards stay cooler.
- **Avoid the oven, dishwasher, washing machine and dryer** in the afternoon. They add heat and humidity. Dry clothes on the line instead.
- **Switch to LED globes.** energy.gov.au says they produce less heat than halogen and incandescent bulbs.

## In the evening: let it out

When it's cooler outside than in, **open windows or doors on opposite sides** of the room or house to get a breeze through. Close up again in the morning before it heats up.

## Fans

energy.gov.au says to try **fans first**. They have to blow on you to feel cooler, but a portable fan costs **about 2 to 3 cents an hour** to run. **Ceiling fans** are the most efficient. Set them to spin the right way for summer with our [fan direction guide](/guides/how-to/ceiling-fan-direction-summer/).

For an instant cool-down, energy.gov.au suggests a mist of water or a damp towel around your neck.

## Small upgrades that make a big difference

- **Seal the gaps.** Weather strips or sealant around windows and doors, and draught stoppers under doors. energy.gov.au says hot air gets in through gaps and cracks, while cool air escapes.
- **Shade the windows.** Awnings, shade sails, or thermal-backed curtains and honeycomb or roman blinds. **Plant trees and shrubs** outside sunny windows and walls. Deciduous plants shade in summer and let the sun through in winter.
- **Top up the insulation.** energy.gov.au says insulation can cut heating and cooling costs by **up to 45%**, and that old insulation deteriorates. Use a certified installer.

## Look after yourself in a heatwave

Spend the hottest part of the day somewhere cool if you can, like a library or shopping centre, and sleep in the coolest room. Know the signs of heat stroke and dehydration, and check on older neighbours. Healthdirect has advice on heat-related illness.

Keep pets inside or in shade with water, and walk dogs early or late: if the ground is too hot for your feet, it's too hot for theirs.

## Frequently asked questions

**How can I cool my house down fast without air con?**
Close blinds and curtains in the heat of the day, run fans, and open windows on opposite sides once it's cooler outside to get a cross breeze.

**Should I keep windows open or closed on a hot day?**
Closed while it's hotter outside than in. Open them once the outside temperature drops below the inside.

**Do fans actually cool a room?**
Not the room itself. They cool you while they're blowing on you, which is why they're so cheap to run.

**What's the best long-term fix for a hot house?**
External shade on sunny windows, sealing gaps, and topping up the ceiling insulation.
