Warm weather brings snakes out. The Queensland Government says they become active and are often seen **from around September**, the start of their breeding and feeding season, and that you should **take extra care in summer**. You can't make a yard completely snake-free, but you can make yours a lot less interesting to them.

## Why snakes come into yards

The Queensland Government says snakes visit yards and houses when people **unknowingly provide food and shelter**:

- **Rats and mice** draw brown snakes and taipans to sheds.
- **Chickens and aviary birds** draw pythons and brown tree snakes.
- **Rats and possums in the roof** draw pythons into ceilings.
- **Frogs** draw tree snakes around ponds and gardens.
- **Timber piles and sheets of corrugated iron** give all sorts of snakes shelter.

## Make the yard less inviting

1. **Keep the lawn short** and well maintained.
2. **Move garden beds and shrubs away** from the house.
3. **Stack timber neatly.** Our [firewood guide](/guides/home-maintenance/how-to-store-firewood/) covers stacking it off the ground.
4. **Clear rubbish,** old sheets of iron and anything lying flat on the ground.
5. **Block holes** in rock walls and other spots where rats and frogs shelter.
6. **Keep rats and mice down:** food scraps in a closed compost bin, bird and pet food in rodent-proof containers.
7. **Snake-proof aviaries and chook pens** with mesh small enough to keep snakes out.
8. **Keep the shed tidy.** The government suggests the big tidy-ups happen in the cooler months, when snakes are less active.

## Seal the house

- **Screens on doors and windows,** and repair any holes.
- **Block gaps** around the house, **including between the roof and the ceiling**.
- **Seal gaps under doors** with a door seal or brush strip.
- Deal with **rats or possums in the roof**, which pythons follow. See our [possum in the roof guide](/guides/how-to/possum-in-the-roof/).

## If you see a snake

**In the yard:** don't panic. Back away to a safe distance and let it move off. The Queensland Government says snakes usually prefer to retreat, and **most bites happen to people who try to catch or kill them**.

**Inside the house:**
1. Keep everyone, including pets, well away from it.
2. **Close the internal doors** and block the gaps under them with rolled-up towels.
3. **Open the external doors and windows**, and put chairs or boxes under windows so it can climb out.
4. If it doesn't leave, **call a licensed snake catcher**. It's worth having a local number saved before summer.

**Never try to kill or catch it.** Snakes are protected under Queensland's Nature Conservation Act 1992, and other states protect native snakes too.

## When gardening

Wear gloves, long pants and covered shoes, and lift things like pots and sheets so they face away from you. If someone is bitten, call 000.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I keep snakes away from my dog?**
Keep the dog's area mown, clear of stacked junk, and free of spilled food that draws rats. If your dog finds a snake, call it away and keep it away. If you think it has been bitten, keep it still and get it to a vet straight away.

**Do snake repellents work?**
The Queensland Government's advice doesn't mention repellents. It focuses on removing food and shelter and sealing the house.

**Can I tell a dangerous snake by its colour?**
No. The government says colour alone isn't a reliable guide, and you should treat every snake with caution.

**Will removing a snake solve the problem?**
Often not for long. Another snake living nearby can take its place, so snake-proofing your house and yard is the better fix.
