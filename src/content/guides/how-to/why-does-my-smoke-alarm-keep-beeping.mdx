It's always 3 am, and it's always the one on the highest ceiling. A smoke alarm that chirps every minute is almost never an emergency, but it's telling you something, and tearing it off the ceiling is the wrong fix. Here's how to work out what it wants.

**First: is it a chirp or a full alarm?** A short chirp every minute or so is a fault or battery warning. **A loud, continuous alarm means treat it as a real fire until you've checked.** Get everyone out if you see or smell smoke, and call Triple Zero (000).

## What the beeping means

| What you hear | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| One chirp about every minute | Low battery | Replace the battery |
| Still chirping after a new battery | Error not cleared, or a failing alarm | Reset it, then replace the alarm if it continues |
| Chirps and the alarm is over 10 years old | End of its life | Replace the alarm |
| Full alarm with no smoke, often from cooking or steam | Nuisance alarm | Air the room, clean the alarm, consider moving it |
| Several linked alarms sound at once | Interconnected alarms | Check every room, then find the one that started it |

Patterns vary a little by brand, and some have a separate "end of life" beep. Your manual has the exact code, and the model is usually printed on the back.

## How to stop the chirp

1. **Use a sturdy ladder**, and get someone to hold it. Falls are a far more likely way to get hurt here than the alarm itself.
2. **Twist the alarm off its base.** Most turn anticlockwise.
3. **Check the date on the back.** If it's past its replace-by date, or more than 10 years old, skip the battery and replace the whole alarm.
4. **Fit a new battery**, of the type your manual lists. Don't use an old one from a drawer.
5. **Refit the alarm and press the test button.** It should give a loud alarm.

**Sealed 10-year alarms** don't have a replaceable battery. When they chirp at the end of their life, the whole alarm gets replaced.

## If it's still beeping after a new battery

The alarm may not have cleared the error. Quell, for example, suggests removing the battery, holding down the test button for at least 15 seconds, and refitting the battery. Check your own manual, as the steps differ.

Next, clean it. Dust and small insects inside the sensor chamber are a classic cause of false alarms and chirps. Fire and Rescue NSW recommends vacuuming your alarms every six months, using the soft brush attachment around the vents.

If it's still beeping after that, replace it. It's a cheap part doing a critical job, and it isn't worth the argument.

## Hardwired alarms have batteries too

Many homes have 240V alarms wired into the ceiling. They usually have a backup battery, so they can chirp too, and it's the same fix: change the backup battery and test.

**The wiring is different.** A hardwired smoke alarm must be replaced by a licensed electrician. Don't disconnect one to stop it beeping, and don't swap a hardwired alarm for a battery one if your home's rules require hardwired alarms. ## Why does it go off when I'm cooking?

Toast and steam from the shower are the usual suspects, especially when an alarm sits close to the kitchen or bathroom. Don't remove the battery to deal with this. Instead:

- Open windows and use the rangehood, with an exhaust that vents outside if you have one.
- Use the hush button if your alarm has one.
- Clean the alarm, as dust makes false alarms more likely.
- Queensland Fire notes that moving an alarm is an option if it keeps going off while cooking. Hardwired alarms need an electrician to move them.

## How often to replace a smoke alarm

Replace every alarm at least every **10 years**. Most have a date printed on them, usually on the back. Fire services agree on this, and in Queensland it's also required if the alarm fails when tested. Queensland is phasing in stricter rules for all homes by 2027, covered in our guide to the [Queensland smoke alarm laws](/guides/home-maintenance/queensland-smoke-alarm-laws-2027/).

## Monthly and yearly checks

- **Monthly:** press the test button on each alarm.
- **Every six months:** vacuum around the vents.
- **Once a year:** change the battery, unless it's a sealed 10-year type. Many people do it when daylight saving changes.
- **Every 10 years:** replace the alarm.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is my smoke alarm beeping every 30 seconds or every minute?**
Usually a low battery. Replace it and test the alarm. If it continues, try a reset, clean it, and replace the alarm if the beeping carries on.

**Why does my smoke alarm beep when it's hot or at night?**
Temperature changes at night can bring out a weak battery, which is why the chirp so often starts at 3 am. Change the battery and test it.

**How do I turn off a beeping smoke alarm?**
Fix the cause rather than switching it off: change the battery, clean it and reset it. If you take it down, replace it the same day, because an alarm that isn't installed can't protect anyone.

**Can I just take the battery out?**
Only to change it. An alarm without a battery won't wake you in a fire.

**Is it illegal not to have a working smoke alarm in Australia?**
Every Australian state and territory requires working smoke alarms in homes, though the details differ. Check your fire service's rules for your state.
