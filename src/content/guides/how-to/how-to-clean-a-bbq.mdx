## First: the gas check

Energy Safe Victoria says fire crews attend dangerous LPG barbecue fires **every summer**, and some become house fires. Its "know the drill before you grill" check:

1. **Inspect the hose** for cracks or perishing. If it's damaged, don't use the BBQ until the hose is replaced.
2. **Check the connection** is tight and the **O ring** isn't cracked or split.
3. **Squirt the cylinder connection with soapy water** (dishwashing liquid and water) and turn the gas on at the bottle. **Bubbles mean gas is escaping.**

If you see bubbles: **turn the gas off**, re-check the hose, connection and O ring, and test again. If it still bubbles, the hose or cylinder may need replacing, or call a licensed gasfitter. Energy Safe Victoria says to do this check **every time you connect a cylinder**, and ideally every time you barbecue.

Also check the bottle's test date. A cylinder can't be refilled if it's **more than 10 years** since it was tested. Our [gas bottle guide](/guides/home-maintenance/gas-bottle-expiry-date/) shows where to find it.

## Then: the clean

### Hotplate and grill

1. **Heat it for 10 to 15 minutes** with the hood up to soften the grease.
2. **Turn the gas off at the bottle** and let it cool until it's warm, not hot.
3. **Scrape the hotplate** with a flat scraper, pushing the grease toward the fat drain.
4. **Scrub the grill bars** with a BBQ brush. Lift them out and soak in hot soapy water if they're caked.
5. **Wipe over with a little cooking oil** when dry, so cast iron doesn't rust.

### Burners

With the plates out, brush the burners gently with a soft wire brush and clear any blocked holes with a toothpick or paperclip. Spiders and wasps love burner tubes over winter. If a burner is cracked or rusted through, replace it.

### Hood and body

Wash the hood and body with warm soapy water and a soft cloth. On stainless steel, wipe with the grain.

### Fat tray

Empty it into the bin (never down a drain), scrub it, and line it with foil or fresh fat absorber. Fire Rescue Victoria says to **remove the excess fat after each use**, because it's what feeds barbecue fires.

## Where the BBQ goes

Fire Rescue Victoria says to:

- use it **outside, in a clear space**, never indoors or in a confined area
- keep **one metre** between the barbecue and walls, fences and other property
- **never leave it unattended**
- avoid using it **in wind**, which can blow the burners out and leak gas

If a barbecue catches fire, call **000**, and if it's safe, turn off the gas at the cylinder.

## Frequently asked questions

**What's the best way to clean a BBQ hotplate?**
Heat it for 10 minutes to soften the grease, turn the gas off, and scrape it while it's warm. Then wash it with warm soapy water, rinse and dry it. A light wipe of cooking oil stops cast iron rusting.

**How do I clean the BBQ burners?**
With the gas off and the burners cool, lift them out and brush the holes clear with a wire brush. Check for cracks or holes rusted through before you refit them. A burner rusted through needs replacing.

**How often should I do the soapy water test?**
Every time you connect a cylinder, and Energy Safe Victoria suggests every time you barbecue.

**Can I hose the grease off onto the lawn or driveway?**
Put the fat in the bin, and wash parts in a tub. Grease and detergent shouldn't go down outside drains.

**How often should I deep clean it?**
Clean the plates and fat tray after each use, and do the full clean, burners and all, at the start of summer.
