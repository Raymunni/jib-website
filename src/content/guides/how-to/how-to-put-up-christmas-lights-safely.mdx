## 1. Check the lights are made for outside

Queensland's Electrical Safety Office is clear: **indoor lights stay indoors**, even on a veranda. Outdoor lights meet extra safety standards and carry an **IP rating**, such as IPX3, IP23 or IP44. The higher the numbers, the more weatherproof the light. The ESO says outdoor equipment needs a rating of **at least IP23**.

Some outdoor sets need their **transformer (the plug end) kept indoors**, out of the weather. Some are only for temporary outdoor use. Read the box.

The safest options, the ESO says, are **solar, LED and extra-low voltage (12 V or 24 V)** lights.

### Buying new lights

Lights sold in Australia must meet safety standards and carry an approval number (for example Q12345 or N12345) or the **Regulatory Compliance Mark**, usually on the box, a tag near the plug or the transformer. The ESO warns against buying lights online from overseas, where the standards are different. You can search registered products at eess.gov.au.

## 2. Check last year's lights

Before you hang anything:

1. **Unravel the set fully** and look at the plug, the leads and every lamp holder.
2. Look for **exposed wires, cracks, melted or broken parts**.
3. **Plug them in on the ground** and check they all work before you climb up.
4. Check the plug has **insulated pins**. The ESO says lights sold after 2006 must have them. If yours don't, they don't meet current requirements.
5. Check they haven't been **recalled**.

Throw out anything damaged. Second-hand lights should be checked by a licensed electrician.

## 3. Plan the power

- **Test your safety switch** first. If you don't have one, use a portable safety switch.
- Use **power boards with overload protection**, not double adaptors or piggyback plugs.
- Use **factory-made extension leads** rated for outdoors, and **unwind them fully** so they don't overheat.
- Make every outdoor connection **weatherproof**. Hardware stores sell covers for this.
- Don't run leads **across paths or driveways**, or through doors and windows where they can be crushed.
- Be careful with lots of high-powered lamps and floodlights: they can **overload a circuit**, and halogen and flood lights get hot, so keep them clear of anything that can burn.

If the display keeps cutting out, our guide to [Christmas lights tripping the safety switch](/guides/how-to/christmas-lights-tripping-safety-switch/) walks through the usual causes.

## 4. Getting them up safely

- **Switch off at the wall** while you hang lights or change bulbs.
- Use **plastic gutter clips or hooks**. The ESO says not to wrap lights around sharp metal, which can cut the wires, and to secure them so wind can't tear them down.
- **Set the ladder on firm, level ground** and have someone hold it. Don't overreach: climb down and move the ladder instead.
- **Stay off the roof.** If the display needs the roof or a second storey, hire someone with the right gear.
- **Keep lights away from the pool.** The ESO says no lighting around or above swimming pools, and no leads near water.

## 5. Running the display

- Use a **timer** and turn the display **off before bed** and when you go out.
- **Turn outdoor lights off in rain and storms.**
- Keep lights **out of reach of children**.
- If you have a real tree inside, **unplug its lights before you water it**.

## Frequently asked questions

**What IP rating do outdoor Christmas lights need?**
At least IP23, according to Queensland's Electrical Safety Office. Higher numbers mean better weatherproofing.

**Can I use indoor Christmas lights outside under a veranda?**
No. The ESO says indoor lights aren't safe outdoors, even on a veranda, because they don't have the weatherproof rating.

**Is it safe to leave Christmas lights on overnight?**
The ESO says to turn decorative lighting off before going to bed or leaving the house. A timer makes that easy.

**Are solar Christmas lights safer?**
The ESO lists solar, LED and extra-low voltage lights as the safest options to prevent shocks, overheating and fires.
