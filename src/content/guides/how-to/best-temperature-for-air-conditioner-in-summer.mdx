It's 38°C outside and the temptation is to set the remote to 18 and wait. The room won't cool any faster, and the bill will be bigger. Here's the setting the government's energy advice recommends, and how to make it feel cooler than it is.

## The setting: 25 to 27°C

energy.gov.au says to use the air conditioner only when you need it and set it **between 25 and 27°C** in summer. Every degree higher saves **5 to 10%** on energy costs.

With most units, a lower number doesn't cool the room much faster. It mainly keeps the unit running longer to reach a colder target.

## Make 26°C feel cooler

- **Close internal doors** and cool only the rooms you're using. energy.gov.au suggests a door snake to stop cool air escaping.
- **Shut blinds, curtains and windows** in the hottest part of the day, and open up on opposite sides of the house when it cools down outside.
- **Run a fan as well.** Fans have to blow on you to feel cooler, but they're far cheaper to run: energy.gov.au puts a portable fan at about 2 to 3 cents an hour. Set ceiling fans to summer mode with our [fan direction guide](/guides/how-to/ceiling-fan-direction-summer/).
- **Dress for it.** Light, loose clothes and cotton or linen sheets.
- **Avoid heat-making jobs** in the afternoon. Running the oven, dishwasher or washing machine adds heat and humidity, so the air conditioner works harder.

## Keep it running efficiently

- **Clean the filters.** energy.gov.au says cleaning the dust filters improves efficiency and is a job you can do yourself. Our [split system cleaning guide](/guides/home-maintenance/how-to-clean-a-split-system-air-conditioner/) shows how.
- **Keep the outdoor unit clear,** with space above it for airflow. Shade helps it avoid overheating.
- **Book a service** with a licensed technician, as energy.gov.au recommends, rather than DIY on anything inside the unit.
- **Use the timer or programmable settings** so it's not cooling an empty house.

If it's blowing but not cooling, our [split system troubleshooting guide](/guides/how-to/split-system-not-cooling/) runs through the checks.

## Buying a new one?

energy.gov.au suggests looking for the **Zoned Energy Rating Label**, which rates efficiency for three climate zones across Australia, and using the Energy Rating Calculator to compare running costs.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best temperature for an air conditioner in summer in Australia?**
Between 25 and 27°C, according to energy.gov.au.

**How much does each degree save?**
Each degree higher saves between 5 and 10% on cooling energy costs.

**Does setting the aircon lower cool the room faster?**
Generally not by much. It mostly runs longer to reach a colder target, which costs more.

**Is it cheaper to run a fan or the air conditioner?**
A fan, by a long way. energy.gov.au puts a portable fan at about 2 to 3 cents an hour, though it only cools you while it's blowing on you.
