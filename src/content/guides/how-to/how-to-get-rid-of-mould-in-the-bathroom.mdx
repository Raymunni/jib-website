Black spots on the ceiling, grey creeping along the grout, pink-black stains in the silicone: bathroom mould is one of the most-searched home problems in Australia, especially through humid summers and damp winters. The good news is that most of it is a cleaning job. The catch is that it always comes back unless you fix the moisture that caused it.

## Before you start: protect yourself

Mould releases spores when it's disturbed. Health authorities recommend:

- **Rubber gloves, eye protection, and a P1 or P2 mask.** People with heart or lung conditions should check with a doctor before wearing a P2 mask.
- **Ventilation:** open the window and door, and run the exhaust fan.
- **No dry brushing.** A dry brush flicks spores into the air you're breathing. Always clean damp.
- **Never mix bleach with vinegar, ammonia or other cleaners.** It can release toxic chlorine gas.

People with asthma, allergies, breathing conditions or weakened immune systems are more sensitive to mould. If that's you, get someone else to do the clean-up.

## Step by step: removing bathroom mould

### 1. Start with detergent or vinegar

For most surface mould on tiles, grout, glass and painted ceilings, **mild detergent in water, or white vinegar diluted 4 parts vinegar to 1 part water**, is enough (NSW Health). Spray or wipe it on, give it a few minutes to work, then wipe it off with a damp microfibre cloth. Rinse the cloth often, or use several.

### 2. Stubborn mould: diluted bleach

If it doesn't come off, NSW Health suggests a **diluted bleach solution: 250 mL of bleach in 4 litres of water.** Wear gloves and eye protection, keep the room well ventilated, and test on a small area first (bleach can discolour coloured grout). **Don't use bleach and vinegar together**, or one straight after the other without rinsing.

### 3. Dry everything

Mould grows back on anything that stays damp. After cleaning, dry the surfaces with a clean towel, leave the fan running and the window open for an hour.

### 4. Deal with the silicone

If mould is *inside* the silicone (black or pink spots that don't wipe off), cleaning won't fix it; the mould has grown into the sealant. **Replace it:** cut out the old silicone with a sharp blade, clean and dry the joint completely, and run a new bead of mould-resistant bathroom silicone. Let it cure fully (usually 24 hours) before showering.

### 5. Ceilings and painted walls

Wipe (don't scrub) with the detergent or vinegar solution, dry, and once it's fully dry, repaint with a primer and a bathroom paint that contains a mould inhibitor. Painting over live mould without cleaning it first just hides it for a few weeks.

## Why it keeps coming back (and how to stop it)

Mould only grows where it's damp and poorly ventilated, so the long-term fix is moisture:

- **Run the exhaust fan every shower, and for 15–20 minutes afterwards.** NSW Health recommends exhaust fans when bathing and showering. If your fan doesn't clear the mirror, it may be too small, blocked, or venting into the ceiling instead of outside.
- **Open a window** when you can, even a little.
- **Squeegee the shower** walls and glass after each use.
- **Fix leaks.** Dripping taps, leaking shower roses and cracked grout all feed mould. So does a blocked gutter or leaking roof above the bathroom ceiling.
- **Keep it warm and dry.** Cold ceilings attract condensation. Wash and fully dry bath mats and towels regularly.

**Want a better exhaust fan?** Installing, replacing or moving a hardwired exhaust fan is licensed electrical work in every Australian state, so that step needs an electrician. Cleaning the fan's grille and checking it runs is DIY.

## When it's more than a clean-up

Call a professional if:

- **The mould covers a large area**, or keeps returning within weeks of cleaning. Better Health Channel recommends a mould remediation professional for large areas of regrowth.
- **There's a smell of damp or soft, stained plasterboard**, which can mean water is getting into the wall or ceiling.
- **The tiles are drummy, grout keeps cracking, or the room next door has damp skirting.** That can mean the shower's waterproofing has failed. Read [can I waterproof my own bathroom?](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-waterproof-my-own-bathroom/) before you decide who fixes it.
- **Mouldy plasterboard is crumbling.** Cut it out and replace it (see [how to patch plasterboard](/guides/how-to/how-to-patch-a-hole-in-plasterboard/)), and use wet-area board in a bathroom.

## Keep it gone: a simple routine

| How often | Job |
|---|---|
| Every shower | Exhaust fan on; squeegee glass and walls |
| Weekly | Wipe shower and grout with detergent; wash bath mats |
| Every 3 months | Clean the exhaust fan grille; check silicone for spots |
| Every 6 months | Check silicone and grout in all wet areas (part of the [home maintenance checklist](/guides/home-maintenance/)) |

## Frequently asked questions

**Is vinegar or bleach better for mould?** Health authorities suggest starting with detergent or diluted white vinegar, and using diluted bleach for stubborn mould. Never mix them.

**Is bathroom mould dangerous?** For most healthy people it causes irritation, like a runny or blocked nose, itchy eyes or skin, and sometimes wheezing. People with asthma, allergies or weakened immune systems are more at risk. See a doctor if you're worried.

**Why is there mould on my bathroom ceiling?** Steam condenses on the cooler ceiling and stays damp. It's usually a ventilation problem: the fan isn't used, isn't strong enough, or doesn't vent outside.

**Can I paint over mould?** Not without cleaning it first. Clean, dry, prime, then use a paint with a mould inhibitor.

**Is it a renter's or landlord's problem?** Cleaning everyday mould is usually the tenant's job; mould from leaks, poor ventilation or building defects is usually the landlord's. Check your state's tenancy authority.
