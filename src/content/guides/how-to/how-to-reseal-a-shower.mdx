Black, peeling silicone in the shower corners is one of the most common bathroom jobs in Australia, and one of the cheapest to fix properly. A tube of wet area silicone and an afternoon will do it. The trick is doing the prep right, and knowing when the problem is bigger than the silicone. This guide covers the method in detail. For the legal side and regrouting, see [can I regrout tiles or replace shower silicone myself?](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-regrout-tiles-or-replace-shower-silicone-myself/)

## Is it a reseal, or has the waterproofing failed?

It's a reseal if the problem is the silicone itself: mouldy, cracked, peeling or shrinking away from the tiles. It's a waterproofing problem if water is getting past the tiles. Check before you buy anything.

| What you see | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Black spots in the silicone, but it's still stuck down | Mould has grown into the silicone | <span class="v ok">Reseal</span> |
| Silicone cracked, peeling or pulling away from the tiles | Worn sealant | <span class="v ok">Reseal</span> |
| Crumbling or missing grout | Worn grout | <span class="v ok">Regrout and reseal</span> |
| Loose or hollow-sounding tiles | Water may be behind the tiles | <span class="v maybe">Get it checked</span> |
| Damp patches that get worse after showers, peeling paint or stains on the other side of the wall | Water is getting through | <span class="v no">Licensed waterproofer</span> |
| A musty smell or mould in the next room, or white chalky residue on surfaces | Moisture moving through the wall or floor | <span class="v no">Licensed waterproofer</span> |

The last three come straight from the warning signs Queensland's building regulator, the QBCC, lists for failed waterproofing. A quick check from Selleys: if a shower leaves the bathroom floor wetter than the shower recess, you probably have a leak.

## What you'll need

You need a wet area (bathroom) silicone, not a general gap filler, plus a few tools to get the old stuff out.

- **Wet area or bathroom silicone**, in a cartridge. Look for one that says it's for showers or wet areas and contains a mould inhibitor or fungicide.
- **A caulking gun** to fit the cartridge
- **A silicone removal tool or sharp blade**
- **Sealant remover** (a gel that softens old silicone)
- **Methylated spirits** and a clean cloth
- **A mould killer**, or the mould mix in step 3
- **Painter's tape**
- **A spatula or profile tool**, and a cup of water with a few drops of detergent
- **Gloves and safety glasses**

**Check the cure time before you buy.** Bathroom silicones vary a lot: Selleys' acetic cure No Mould is shower ready in about an hour, while its Wet Area Silicone needs 24 hours before water touches it. If the family has only one shower, that difference decides your day.

## How to remove the old silicone

All of the old silicone has to come out. Selleys doesn't recommend putting new silicone over an old bead, because it may not stick to it well. That's also why the "quick fix" over the top tends to peel within months.

1. **Turn off the shower and let the bathroom dry out.** Ideally, start after a day without showering in it.
2. **Cut along both edges of the old bead** with the removal tool or blade, then peel and scrape it out. Hold the blade flat so you don't scratch the tiles, the bath or the screen.
3. **Soften what's left.** Spread sealant remover over the leftover silicone, about 5 mm thick, and leave it as long as the pack says. Selleys says at least 10 minutes in one guide and up to 2 hours in another, so go by your product.
4. **Scrape off the softened residue.**
5. **Wipe the joint with methylated spirits** to lift the last of the film. Selleys says to use methylated spirits here, not water.

## How to kill the mould before resealing

Treat the mould now, while the joint is open, or it will grow straight back through the new silicone.

1. **Ventilate the room.** Open a window and run the exhaust fan.
2. **Put on gloves and safety glasses.** The Better Health Channel also recommends a P1 or P2 mask from the hardware store.
3. **Use a mould killer**, or NSW Health's mix: mild detergent, or vinegar diluted in water (4 parts vinegar to 1 part water). For stubborn mould, NSW Health suggests 250 mL of bleach in 4 litres of water.
4. **Never mix bleach with other cleaners.** The Better Health Channel warns that bleach mixed with ammonia, acids or other cleaners can release hazardous fumes.
5. **Rinse and let it dry completely.** Overnight with the fan running is ideal. Silicone won't stick to a damp joint.

The Better Health Channel says some people shouldn't remove mould or be in the room while it's removed, including pregnant people, children and people with weakened immune systems or chronic lung disease. If that's you, get someone else to do this step.

## How to apply new shower silicone

Work in one steady bead per joint, and smooth it before it skins. Selleys says you have about 5 minutes before the silicone starts to skin, so do one joint at a time.

1. **Check the joint is clean and bone dry.** Wipe it once more with methylated spirits and let it evaporate.
2. **Tape both sides of the joint** with painter's tape, leaving a gap the width you want the bead to be.
3. **Cut the nozzle at 45 degrees**, a little smaller than the gap, and load the cartridge into the gun.
4. **Test on scrap cardboard** until the silicone flows smoothly.
5. **Run one continuous bead**, pushing the silicone into the joint. On vertical corners, work from the top down.
6. **Smooth it straight away** with a spatula or profile tool dipped in detergent water. Rinse the tool between passes.
7. **Pull the tape off immediately**, before the silicone skins, pulling away from the joint.
8. **Clean up any smears** before they skin. Selleys suggests mineral turpentine on a cloth.

Do the joints where walls meet each other, where walls meet the floor or bath, and around the screen and tap flanges. These joints move, which is why they get silicone, not grout.

## How long before you can use the shower?

It depends on the product, so the tube is the final word. Selleys gives very different times for its own products:

| Product type | When water can touch it | Fully cured |
|---|---|---|
| Selleys No Mould (acetic cure) | About 1 hour | At least 48 hours. Selleys also says 72 hours |
| Selleys Wet Area Silicone | 24 hours | Selleys' leaky shower guide says allow 72 hours before using the shower |

Leave the bead alone until it's fully cured, and don't poke it to check. Selleys also says a hair dryer won't speed it up and may make it worse. If you're unsure, give it the longer time: one more day using another shower is cheaper than doing the job twice.

## How to keep the new silicone clean

Keep the bathroom dry and the silicone will last much longer.

- Run the exhaust fan during every shower and for a while after.
- Squeegee or wipe the walls and joints after showering.
- Clean off soap scum regularly. Selleys says scum and dirt encourage mould.
- Avoid harsh or corrosive cleaners. The QBCC lists this as one of the ways to make waterproofing last.
- Expect to redo it every few years. The QBCC advises replacing silicone joints every few years as normal maintenance.

For mould on the ceiling and grout too, see [how to get rid of mould in the bathroom](/guides/how-to/how-to-get-rid-of-mould-in-the-bathroom/).

## When to call a professional

Call a licensed waterproofer or builder if the signs point past the silicone: damp walls or skirting outside the shower, loose or drummy tiles, stains on a ceiling below, or mould that keeps coming back in the next room. Resealing won't stop water that's already getting under the tiles.

- **Take photos and notes first.** If the bathroom was built or renovated recently, the QBCC advises documenting the problem and telling your licensed contractor in writing as soon as possible.
- **Don't wait.** Bathroom repairs get expensive fast. The QBCC says finalised bathroom claims under the Queensland Home Warranty Scheme in 2024-25 averaged almost $25,000 to fix.
- **Strata?** In an apartment, a leak can affect the unit below, so tell your owners corporation or strata manager.
- **Anything involving the waste or pipes** needs a licensed plumber.

If you're thinking of redoing the membrane yourself, read [can I waterproof my own bathroom?](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-waterproof-my-own-bathroom/) first.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I put new silicone over old silicone?**
No. Selleys doesn't recommend it, because new silicone may not stick well to the old bead. Cut it out and start fresh.

**How long does shower silicone take to dry?**
Anywhere from about an hour to 24 hours before water can touch it, and 48 to 72 hours to fully cure, depending on the product. Check the tube.

**Why does mould keep coming back on my shower silicone?**
Usually because mould has grown into the silicone, or the bathroom stays damp. Replace the mouldy silicone rather than scrubbing it, then run the fan and dry the shower after use. If mould keeps returning fast, check for a leak.

**Will resealing fix a leaking shower?**
Only if the leak is through the silicone joints. If water is getting through the grout and tiles into the wall or floor, the membrane underneath has likely failed and needs a licensed waterproofer.

**Can I use any silicone in the shower?**
Use one sold for wet areas, showers or bathrooms, ideally with a mould inhibitor. General purpose sealants and gap fillers aren't made for constant water.

**How often should shower silicone be replaced?**
The QBCC advises every few years, and sooner if it's mouldy, cracked or peeling.
