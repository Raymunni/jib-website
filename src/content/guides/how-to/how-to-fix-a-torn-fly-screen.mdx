Summer nights mean windows open, and one torn screen lets in every mosquito on the street. Fixing it is cheap, and re-meshing a whole window screen takes less than an hour once you've done one.

## Patch or re-mesh?

| The damage | The fix |
| --- | --- |
| A hole smaller than a coin | Patch it |
| A tear a few centimetres long | Patch it, or re-mesh if it's in the middle |
| A big tear, sagging or brittle mesh | Re-mesh the frame |
| A bent or broken frame | Replace the screen |

## The mesh that keeps mosquitoes out

Victoria's Better Health Channel says fly screens should be **no coarser than 1.2 mm** (12 x 12 meshes per 25 mm) to keep mosquitoes out, and finer if you want to stop smaller insects like sandflies. Fibreglass mesh is the easiest to work with. Aluminium, stainless and pet-resistant mesh are tougher but stiffer.

## Patching a small hole

1. **Trim** loose strands around the hole.
2. **Cut a patch** of matching mesh about 2 cm bigger than the hole on every side, or use a self-adhesive fly screen patch.
3. **Fix it on:** press an adhesive patch on firmly from both sides, or stitch a mesh patch on with fishing line around the edge.

## Re-meshing a screen

**You'll need:** new mesh, new spline (take a piece of the old one to match its thickness), a spline roller, a sharp utility knife, and a flat surface like a workbench or a table.

1. **Take the screen out.** Most window screens lift up and out. Door screens may need the frame unscrewed.
2. **Pull out the old spline,** the rubber cord in the channel around the frame, starting at a corner. Remove the old mesh.
3. **Clean the channel** of dirt and bits of old rubber.
4. **Lay the new mesh over the frame,** with about 5 cm extra on every side.
5. **Start on one long side.** Use the convex wheel of the roller to press the mesh into the channel, then the concave wheel to roll the spline in on top.
6. **Do the opposite side next,** pulling the mesh just snug, not drum tight, which can bow the frame.
7. **Finish the short sides,** pushing the spline into the corners with a screwdriver.
8. **Trim the excess mesh** with the knife angled along the outside of the spline.
9. **Refit the screen** and check it seals all round.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I replace the fly screen on a sliding door?**
The same way as a window screen. Lift the door screen off its track (most lift up, then swing out at the bottom), lay it flat, pull out the old spline and mesh, and roll in new ones. Door screens get more wear, so a pet-resistant mesh is worth a look if you have a dog.

**What size spline do I need?**
Match the old one. Take a piece to the hardware store, because frames take different thicknesses.

**Should I DIY or call someone?**
Window screens are an easy DIY job. Security doors and screens with damaged frames are worth a professional quote.

**Will fly screen mesh keep out midges?**
Standard 1.2 mm mesh keeps out mosquitoes. Better Health says to use a finer mesh for sandflies and other small biting insects.
