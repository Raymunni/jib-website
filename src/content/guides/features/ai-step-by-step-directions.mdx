## Why photo-based steps beat a generic guide

A general "how to regrout a shower" article can't see your shower. A photo lets the directions fit your situation:

- **The right method for what's there.** Wall tiles or floor, cement or epoxy grout, a hairline crack or a missing chunk.
- **Your materials.** Timber, brick, fibre cement or plasterboard change the fixings and the tools.
- **The warning you'd miss.** If the photo shows water damage behind the grout, the steps say to fix that first, before regrouting over a problem.
- **Your rules.** Directions reflect the licensing rules where your house is, not another country's.

## Example: re-siliconing a shower

From one photo of a mouldy shower corner, the directions look something like:

1. Check the silicone is the problem: mould that won't wipe off means it's grown into the sealant.
2. Cut out the old silicone with a sharp blade or silicone remover tool; don't gouge the tiles.
3. Clean the joint with methylated spirits and let it dry completely (a few hours; overnight is better).
4. Tape both edges of the joint with masking tape for a clean line.
5. Run a continuous bead of mould-resistant bathroom silicone.
6. Smooth it with a wet finger or tool, then remove the tape straight away.
7. **Wait 24 hours** before using the shower.

Warnings: ventilate while working; if water is getting behind the tiles, the waterproofing may have failed ([what to do](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-waterproof-my-own-bathroom/)). Verdict: DIY safe. ## How reliable are AI directions?

They're a strong, specific starting point, but you're the one holding the tools:

- **Read the whole list before you start**, and check product instructions (curing times vary by brand).
- **If a step doesn't match what you see**, stop and ask the job's AI chat, or get advice.
- **Follow the legal flags.** If a step needs a licensed tradie, it isn't a suggestion. See [what you can legally DIY](/guides/diy-legal/).
