Search "how to build a deck" and you'll get the same steps whether you live in Hobart or Cairns. Your house doesn't work like that. The year it was built, the soil it sits on and the local climate change how a job should be done, and sometimes whether it's safe to do at all.

## Four things about your house that change the job

### 1. Termites

Subterranean termites are common across much of Australia, and especially active in Queensland, northern New South Wales and other warm, humid areas. They travel through soil and attack timber in contact with the ground.

What it changes:

- **Deck and pergola posts** should sit on galvanised steel post stirrups (post anchors) set into concrete footings, so the timber stays off the ground and termites can't climb straight in. Burying timber posts in the soil is asking for trouble, even treated pine.
- **Timber near the ground** needs the right treatment rating. In Australia, H3 is the usual minimum for outdoor timber above ground, and timber in or near the ground generally needs H4 or better. Check the product label.
- **Keep inspection zones clear.** Don't stack timber, garden beds or soil against the house where they hide termite mud leads. If you see mud tubes, call a licensed pest inspector before you start.

### 2. The age of the house

- **Before about 1990**, building materials may contain asbestos (in eaves, wet-area walls, vinyl floors and fences). Don't drill, cut or sand anything you're unsure about. Get it tested first.
- **Older paint** can contain lead. Test before sanding back old paintwork, especially on pre-1970s homes.
- **Older wiring and plumbing** often need a licensed tradie to check before you add load or move fittings.

### 3. The soil

Reactive clay soils swell when wet and shrink when dry. That moves footings, cracks paths and can heave posts set straight into the ground. Deeper footings, good drainage and flexible joints matter more on clay than on sand.

### 4. The climate

- **Humid and coastal areas:** mould, corrosion of fixings (use stainless or hot-dip galvanised near the coast) and faster timber weathering.
- **High-UV and storm areas:** finishes break down faster, and loose items and roof fixings need checking before storm season.
- **Wet seasons** change when to pour concrete, paint or seal, and how long things take to cure.

## Common questions

**Is this a replacement for a building or pest inspection?**
No. It's general guidance to help you plan and ask the right questions. For termites, asbestos, structure and approvals, get a licensed inspector, tradie or your council to confirm.

**Do I need to know my soil type?**
No. Jib estimates it from your location. If you know better (from a soil report or a builder), add it to your house details.

**Does it work outside Australia?**
Yes. Jib uses your location wherever you are. This guide uses Australian examples because termites, clay soils and building rules vary so much here.
