Almost everyone who does DIY has done it: bought a stud finder, a set of hole saws or a second spirit level, got home, and found the first one sitting in the garage. A tool inventory fixes that. Keeping one used to mean a clipboard and a spare afternoon. Now it can be one photo.

This guide covers why a tool inventory is worth having, the old ways to make one, the one-photo way, and a starter checklist of the tools most homes actually need.

## Why keep a tool inventory at all?

- **You stop buying duplicates.** The easiest money you'll ever save on DIY is the tool you didn't buy twice.
- **You plan jobs properly.** Knowing what you've got tells you whether a job is a Saturday morning or a Saturday morning plus a trip to the hardware store.
- **You can lend (and get things back).** A list makes it obvious when the post-hole shovel hasn't come home.
- **It helps if something goes wrong.** Garages and sheds are a common target for theft. A record of what you owned, with photos, makes a contents insurance claim far easier to support. Check what your policy covers, since some cap or exclude tools kept in outbuildings.
- **Moving house gets easier.** You'll know what's in the boxes, and what went missing on the way.

## The old ways (and why they rarely stick)

| Method | Good for | Why people give up |
|---|---|---|
| Notebook or clipboard | Zero setup | Out of date the first time you buy something |
| Spreadsheet | Serial numbers, prices | Tedious to build, never open when you're in the aisle |
| Labelled shelves and shadow boards | Finding things fast | Doesn't tell you what you own when you're not in the garage |
| Photos in your camera roll | Insurance evidence | Impossible to search: "do I own a jigsaw?" means scrolling 400 photos |

All of these work if you're disciplined. The problem is the typing: most people's inventory stalls at the first shelf.

## The one-photo way

Jib's AI Toolbox takes the typing out:

2. **Photograph a wall, a shelf or an open toolbox.** The AI looks at the photo and lists every tool and piece of equipment it can recognise: drills, levels, saws, clamps, ladders, garden tools, safety gear.

## What happens after you've scanned

This is the part a spreadsheet can't do. Your Toolbox becomes part of every job you plan:

- **Tools needed, already ticked off.** When Jib plans a job, it lists the tools it needs and marks the ones in your Toolbox as ones you already have.
- **Missing tools go on the shopping list with one tap.** Anything you don't own shows a **Buy** button, and after a plan Jib offers to add all your missing tools at once.
- **Bought it? It's in your Toolbox.** Tick a tool off on your shopping list and it moves into your Toolbox automatically, so no future job asks you to buy it again.
- **Pick tools by hand, too.** On any job, **Add a tool** opens your Toolbox as a searchable list, so you can attach exactly what you plan to use.

## Tips for a scan that catches everything

- **Good light beats a good camera.** Open the roller door or switch everything on. Shadows hide small tools.
- **One wall or shelf per photo.** A tight shot of a pegboard beats a wide shot of the whole garage.
- **Open the cases.** Drill cases, socket sets and toolboxes photograph best open.
- **Stand things up.** Tools lying flat on a bench are easier to recognise than tools piled in a tub.
- **Use receipts for the small stuff.** Drill bits, blades and fixings are easier to capture from the receipt than the shelf.
- **Rescan after big changes,** like a birthday, a garage clean-out or a big project.

## A starter tool checklist for homeowners

Not sure what a well-set-up home toolbox looks like? Here's a practical baseline for general maintenance and small projects. Tick off what you have, and you've got a gap list.

**Measuring and marking**
- Tape measure (8 m is a good all-rounder)
- Spirit level (600 mm or longer)
- Combination square
- Pencils and a marker
- Stud finder

**Hand tools**
- Claw hammer
- Screwdriver set (flat and Phillips, plus a stubby)
- Adjustable spanner and a basic socket set
- Pliers: combination, long-nose and locking
- Utility knife with spare blades
- Handsaw
- Putty knife and scraper
- Caulking gun

**Power tools**
- Cordless drill/driver, with a drill bit and driver bit set
- A second battery and charger
- Jigsaw or circular saw (for timber projects)
- Orbital sander

**Access**
- Step ladder
- Extension ladder (if you'll ever clean gutters)

**Safety gear**
- Safety glasses
- Hearing protection
- Dust masks (P2 for sanding and dusty work)
- Work gloves

**Garden**
- Spade and shovel
- Rake
- Pruning secateurs and loppers
- Wheelbarrow

## Keep the evidence (insurance)

For higher-value power tools, it's worth keeping more than a name:

- Photograph the tool and its serial number plate.
- Keep receipts, or a photo of them.
- Store copies somewhere other than the garage.
