With kids, time for the house comes in slivers: twenty minutes of nap time, an hour on a Saturday afternoon, the gap between school pick-up and dinner. The jobs don't wait for a free weekend, so the answer is to match the job to the sliver. Here's what you can realistically finish in each.

**Before you start anything:** keep tools, blades and chemicals out of reach, never leave a ladder up with kids around, and save power tools and anything involving fumes for when the kids are out or asleep in another part of the house.

## Bigger jobs, broken into slivers

Some jobs look like a weekend but are really a string of short sessions with drying time in between. That makes them perfect for busy weeks:

- **Patching and painting a wall:** fill (30 min) → dry overnight → sand and second coat (20 min) → dry → prime and paint (45 min).
- **Regrouting a shower:** rake out one wall per session (30–45 min each), then grout and clean in one go when you have a clear hour.
- **Painting a room:** prep and tape one evening, cut in the next, roll the walls on the weekend.
- **Garden beds:** build the frame one afternoon, fill it the next weekend. See [how many sleepers you need](/guides/materials/how-many-sleepers-raised-garden-bed/).

## Not quick, and not DIY

Some jobs *look* like 15 minutes but legally need a licensed tradie in every Australian state, however quick they are:

- Swapping a power point or light switch
- Replacing a light fitting or ceiling fan
- Replacing a whole tap, toilet or hot water system
- Anything involving gas

Batch them into one call-out to save money. See [what you can legally DIY](/guides/diy-legal/), or let Jib's [DIY-or-tradie verdict](/guides/features/diy-or-tradie-verdict/) flag them for you.

## The real secret: capture first, plan later

The reason quick jobs don't get done isn't a lack of time. It's that when the spare half hour arrives, you can't remember what needed doing, you don't have the part, or you'd need to research how. Fix that and the half hours start adding up:

1. **Snap it when you see it.** Don't stop to write a list; just take a photo.
2. **Let the plan happen in the background.** Jib turns each photo into a job with steps, materials, tools and a time estimate.
3. **Shop once.** Materials from every job land on one shopping list, so you pick up the washer, filler and silicone in one trip.
4. **Pick by time.** When you get a sliver, open the list and choose a job that fits.

More on this in [how to keep a home to-do list when you have no time](/guides/features/home-to-do-list-no-time/).

## Frequently asked questions

**What home jobs can I do during nap time?** Quiet ones: filling holes, wiping mould, cleaning filters, tightening hinges, testing smoke alarms (warn the household first, they're loud). Save noisy power tools for when the kids are out.

**What's the best first quick job?** Test your smoke alarms. It takes five minutes and it's the one that matters most.

**How do I find time for home maintenance with kids?** Stop waiting for a free weekend. Keep a list of short jobs, make sure you have the parts, and do one whenever a spare 15–30 minutes turns up.
