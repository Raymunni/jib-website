Most people remember to photograph a finished renovation. Almost nobody photographs the before. Then, when they sell, claim on insurance, or need to show a tradie what the leak looked like last winter, the photo they need doesn't exist.

## Why before and after photos are worth taking

- **Selling:** buyers and agents love a story. "New silicone, fresh paint, serviced gutters, here's the before" builds confidence, and the photos help answer a building inspector's questions. See [renovating before selling](/guides/how-to/renovations-before-selling/).
- **Insurance:** photos of your home and its condition before damage make a claim easier to support. Photos of repairs show the work was done.
- **Tradies and warranties:** a dated photo of the problem before and after a repair helps if the fix doesn't hold.
- **Rentals:** landlords and tenants both benefit from a dated record of condition and repairs.
- **Motivation:** looking back at how far a room has come is the best reason to start the next job.

## How to take good before and after photos

1. **Same spot, same angle.** Stand in the same place and hold the phone at the same height. A mark on the floor or a doorway corner helps.
2. **Same light.** Shoot at a similar time of day, with lights on or blinds open both times.
3. **Wide, then close.** One photo of the whole room or wall, one close-up of the actual problem.
4. **Before you start, not halfway.** Take the before the moment you notice the job, which is also when you'd log it.
5. **Keep them together.** A before in your camera roll and an after in a different album is as good as no before at all.

## Keep the paperwork with the photos

For bigger jobs, store the documents alongside the photos:

- Receipts for materials and appliances
- Tradie invoices and **compliance certificates** (electrical, plumbing, gas)
- Warranty documents and manuals
- Council approvals, where the work needed them

When you sell, that folder answers most of the buyer's questions before they're asked.
