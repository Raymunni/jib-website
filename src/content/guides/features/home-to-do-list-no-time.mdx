Every parent has a list of house jobs in their head. The wobbly towel rail, the scuffed hallway, the gutter that overflows when it rains, the tap that's been dripping since winter. The list keeps growing, and the only thing that doesn't is the time to deal with it.

Most people try a notes app, a whiteboard or a jar of sticky notes. Most people give up within a month. Here's why, and a system that works when you genuinely have no time.

## Why house to-do lists fail

1. **Writing it down takes too long.** "Fix bathroom" is useless later. A useful entry ("regrout the shower, left wall, buy grey grout") takes a minute you don't have when you're carrying a toddler.
2. **The list doesn't tell you what to do.** When you finally get an hour, you spend it researching how, not doing it.
3. **You don't have the parts.** The job needs a washer or a tube of filler, so it waits for the next hardware trip, which never happens.
4. **Everything looks the same size.** A list doesn't tell you which jobs fit into 20 minutes and which need a whole weekend, so you do none of them.
5. **Recurring jobs vanish.** Smoke alarms, gutters and filters don't live on a one-off list, so they get forgotten until something goes wrong.

## The snap-it method

### 1. Capture: photograph it, nothing else

The moment you notice a job, take a photo. Don't type, don't categorise, don't plan. Capturing has to take less than five seconds or it won't happen. A photo also records things you'd never write down: exactly where the crack is, what the tap looks like, the model number on the appliance.

### 2. Plan later, or let it plan itself

Planning happens when you have headspace, not when you notice the job. In Jib, the AI does the planning for you: each photo becomes a named job, filed under the right room, with [steps, materials, tools, a time estimate and a cost range](/guides/features/photo-to-job-plan/).

### 3. Shop once

Put every job's materials on one shopping list, grouped by store. One hardware trip covers five jobs, and you get the tap washer, filler and silicone before you need them. Jib's [AI Toolbox](/guides/features/garage-tool-inventory/) remembers the tools you already own, so the list only shows what you're missing.

### 4. Pick by time, not by guilt

When a spare moment turns up, don't start with the biggest or oldest job. Start with one that fits: 15 minutes for a hinge, an hour for re-siliconing, a Saturday for the gutters. Finishing something small beats half-starting something big. Our list of [quick home jobs by time](/guides/features/quick-home-jobs-by-time/) has ideas for every size of gap.

### 5. Put the repeating jobs on autopilot

Some jobs come back: smoke alarm tests monthly, gutters every six months, termite inspections yearly. Don't keep them on the list; set them to repeat and get reminded. The [home maintenance checklist](/guides/home-maintenance/) shows what to set and how often.

### 6. Keep it everywhere

The list is only useful if it's with you. Jib syncs to your account, so your jobs are on your phone and in the [web app](/web/?demo) on your computer, handy for planning a weekend or ordering materials from your desk.

## What about the jobs you can't do?

Some jobs on the list will legally need a licensed electrician, plumber or gasfitter. Don't let them sit there forever. Flag them, then batch them into one call-out. Jib marks each job [DIY safe or tradie required](/guides/features/diy-or-tradie-verdict/) automatically, so you can see at a glance which ones to hand over.

## Frequently asked questions

**What's the best app for a home to-do list?** Any system you'll actually use. The key features for busy families are fast capture (a photo, not typing), a plan for each job with a time estimate, one combined shopping list, and reminders for recurring jobs. That's what Jib was built to do.

**How do I prioritise home repairs?** Safety first (smoke alarms, electrical faults, trip hazards), then anything that gets worse and more expensive if left (leaks, blocked gutters, failed silicone), then everything else by the time you have.

**How do I stop the list getting overwhelming?** Group jobs by room, hide the finished ones, and pick by the time you have rather than staring at the whole list. Asking "what can I do in 30 minutes?" feels very different to facing a wall of 40 jobs.
