"Do I need council approval?" is really two questions: does the structure fit inside your state's **exempt development** limits, and does anything about your block — heritage, bushfire, flood, easements — cancel that exemption? This guide covers the first question for the five most common backyard projects, and lists what to check for the second.

> Exempt doesn't mean "no rules". Exempt structures still have to meet building standards, be structurally sound and drain properly — you just don't need to apply for approval.

## Garden sheds

| State | No approval needed if… |
|---|---|
| NSW | Up to 20 m² (50 m² in some rural zones), no more than 3 m high, at least 900 mm from boundaries, behind the building line, and not habitable |
| VIC | A freestanding shed under 10 m², within height and siting limits — a planning permit can still apply under overlays |
| QLD | Up to 10 m², and not in a tropical cyclone area; council siting and boundary rules still apply |
| WA | Up to 10 m² and no more than 2.4 m high |
| SA | Check with your council — we couldn't confirm SA's limits from an official source |

## Decks and patios

| State | No approval needed if… |
|---|---|
| NSW | Up to 25 m², floor no more than 1 m above ground, no more than 3 m high, behind the building line and at least 900 mm from boundaries |
| VIC | Councils generally say decks need a building permit — check with yours |
| QLD | Decks aren't on Queensland's list of work that needs no approval — assume you need approval |
| WA | Decks generally need a building permit |
| SA | Check with your council |

[More on decks in NSW →](/guides/council-approval/deck-council-approval-nsw/)

## Fences

| State | No approval needed if… |
|---|---|
| NSW | Side and rear up to 1.8 m (1.2 m if masonry); front and road frontages up to 1.2 m; not on or next to a heritage item |
| VIC | Front fences within 3 m of the street up to 1.5 m; side and rear up to 2 m; within 9 m of a corner, 1 m |
| QLD | Up to 2 m (pool fences excluded) |
| WA | Side and rear, non-masonry, behind the front setback, up to 1.8 m; front and masonry fences need a permit |
| SA | Check with your council |

## Retaining walls

| State | No approval needed if… |
|---|---|
| NSW | Up to 600 mm high, at least 1 m from each boundary and 2 m from other retaining walls, and not redirecting water onto neighbours |
| VIC | Walls on or next to a boundary, or over an easement, need a permit; check other walls with your council |
| QLD | Up to 1 m including the cut or fill, no load (like a driveway or building) nearby, and at least 1.5 m from a building or another wall |
| WA | Check with your council — some require a permit for quite low walls |
| SA | Retaining 1 m or less (and wall plus any fence on top no more than 2.1 m), unless your zone says otherwise |

## Pergolas

| State | No approval needed if… |
|---|---|
| NSW | Same limits as decks: up to 25 m², no more than 3 m high, 900 mm from boundaries, behind the building line. Roofed pergolas attached to the house have extra rules (not above the gutter, roof water to stormwater) |
| VIC | Check with your council — exemptions generally require an unroofed pergola |
| QLD | Pergolas aren't on the no-approval list — assume you need approval |
| WA | Attached, up to 2.4 m high and 20 m², outside cyclone wind regions C and D. A waterproof sail counts as a verandah and needs a permit |
| SA | Check with your council |

## What can cancel an exemption

Check these before relying on any exemption:

- **Heritage** — heritage items and conservation areas (in NSW, fence exemptions don't apply on or next to a heritage item).
- **Bushfire-prone land** — often adds material requirements, such as non-combustible walls.
- **Flood zones** and foreshore areas.
- **Easements** — you usually need consent from the service authority to build over one.
- **Planning overlays** — in Victoria, an overlay can require a planning permit even when no building permit is needed.
- **Pools** — pool barriers are never covered by these general exemptions.

Your council's planning certificate, or a quick call to its duty planner, will tell you which apply to your property.

## Myths that cause problems

1. **"Any shed under 10 m² is exempt everywhere."** NSW allows 20 m², but every state adds height, setback and material conditions, and overlays can remove the exemption entirely.
2. **"Decks under a metre never need approval."** That's the NSW exempt limit — and it still caps area and setbacks. In Victoria, Queensland and WA, decks generally need approval.
3. **"A 1.8 m fence is always fine."** Front fences are usually limited to 1.2–1.5 m, and corner blocks have extra limits.
4. **"Exempt means no rules."** Building standards, drainage, tree-removal rules and your neighbour's rights still apply.

## Dividing fences

Replacing a fence on the boundary is also governed by dividing-fence laws, which are about cost sharing rather than approval. In NSW, neighbours generally share the cost of a "sufficient" fence equally under the Dividing Fences Act 1991; the other states have their own versions. Talk to your neighbour early, and put agreements in writing.
