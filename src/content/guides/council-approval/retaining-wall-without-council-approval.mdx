Retaining walls are the backyard structure most likely to be underestimated. A wall holding back soil carries far more load than it looks, and a failed wall can damage your property — or your neighbour's. That's why the approval limits are low.

## Limits by state

| State | Exempt if… |
|---|---|
| NSW | No more than 600 mm high (and no more than 600 mm of cut or fill); at least 1 m from each boundary; at least 2 m from any other retaining wall; at least 40 m from a natural waterbody; not redirecting water onto neighbours |
| QLD | No more than 1 m high including the retained cut or fill; no surcharge load (like a building or driveway) in the zone of influence; at least 1.5 m from a building or another wall |
| SA | Retaining 1 m or less, and the wall plus any fence on top no more than 2.1 m — unless your zone says otherwise |
| VIC | A permit is needed on or next to a title boundary or over an easement; confirm other walls with your council |
| WA | Check with your council — some councils require a permit for quite low walls |

## Why the limits are strict

Soil pressure increases quickly with height, and water trapped behind a wall adds even more. Common culprits when walls lean or fail are poor drainage behind the wall, footings too small for the height, and extra load near the top — a driveway, a shed or a fence.

## Building an exempt wall properly

Even under the exempt height, build it like it matters:

- **Drainage:** agricultural (slotted) pipe at the base behind the wall, surrounded by drainage gravel and wrapped in geotextile fabric, running to a legal discharge point.
- **Footings:** follow the block or sleeper manufacturer's footing details for your wall height and soil.
- **Materials:** use sleepers and posts rated for in-ground contact, or engineered block systems.
- **Setbacks:** the boundary distances above exist partly so you can maintain the wall — and so it doesn't become your neighbour's problem.

## When you need an engineer

If the wall is over the exempt height, holds back a slope that supports a building or driveway, or is close to a boundary, get engineering advice and approval before you start. It costs far less than rebuilding a failed wall.
