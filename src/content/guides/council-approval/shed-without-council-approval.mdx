"You can build any shed under 10 square metres without approval" is repeated so often it sounds like law. It isn't, the limit is different in every state, and it's never the only condition.

## Limits by state

| State | Max floor area | Other key conditions |
|---|---|---|
| NSW | 20 m² (50 m² in rural zones RU1–4, RU6, R5) | No more than 3 m high; at least 900 mm from boundaries (5 m in those rural zones); behind the building line; at least 1 m from registered easements; not habitable; not a shipping container |
| VIC | Under 10 m² (freestanding) | Height and siting limits apply; a planning permit can still be needed under an overlay |
| QLD | 10 m² | Not in a tropical cyclone area; council siting and boundary-clearance rules still apply |
| WA | 10 m² | No more than 2.4 m high |
| SA | 15 m² | No part over 2.5 m high; no roof span over 3 m; not in front of the house; not in flood or other listed overlays. See [unapproved sheds in SA](/guides/council-approval/unapproved-shed-south-australia/) |

## What still applies to an exempt shed

- **Setbacks and position:** even exempt sheds usually can't sit in front of the house or hard against the boundary.
- **Overlays:** heritage, bushfire, flood and easement restrictions can cancel the exemption.
- **Cyclone areas:** in Queensland, the 10 m² exemption doesn't apply in tropical cyclone areas, where wind loads are the whole point of approval.
- **Structure:** the shed still has to be built properly and tied down to suit your wind region, follow the kit's engineering, including the slab or footing details.
- **Use:** a garden shed can't be lived in. Turning it into a studio or granny flat is a different approval altogether.

## Before you buy the kit

1. **Measure your site** and mark the setbacks from each boundary.
2. **Check your council's planning certificate** or map for overlays.
3. **Check the kit's footprint**: some advertised sizes are roof dimensions, not floor area.
4. **Plan the base:** most kits need a concrete slab or a specific footing system. Check the kit's instructions for your wind region.

For decks, fences, retaining walls and pergolas, see [do you need council approval? The state-by-state guide](/guides/council-approval/).

## Frequently asked questions

**What size shed can I build without council approval in NSW?**
Up to 20 m², or 50 m² in rural zones RU1 to RU4, RU6 and R5. It must be no more than 3 m high, at least 900 mm from boundaries (5 m in those rural zones), behind the building line and at least 1 m from registered easements.

**What size shed can I build without council approval in Queensland?**
Up to 10 m² in plan area, no more than 2.4 m high (mean height 2.1 m), with no side longer than 5 m. The exemption doesn't apply in tropical cyclone areas, and the council's siting and boundary rules still apply. See [how close to the fence a shed can be](/guides/council-approval/how-close-to-fence-can-a-shed-be/).

**How big can a shed be without a permit in Victoria?**
A freestanding shed of 10 m² or less, no more than 3 m high (2.4 m within 1 m of another property), not masonry, and not further forward than existing buildings. A planning permit can still be needed under an overlay.

**How big can a shed be without a permit in WA?**
10 m² or less, freestanding and not masonry, no more than 2.4 m high within 1 m of a boundary (3 m if further away), and no closer to the street than the house.

**How big a shed can I build in SA without approval?**
Up to 15 m², no part over 2.5 m high and no roof span over 3 m, not in front of the house and not in a flood or other listed overlay. See [unapproved sheds in SA](/guides/council-approval/unapproved-shed-south-australia/).

**What happens if I build a shed without council approval?**
Usually nothing straight away. It tends to surface when a neighbour complains, the council inspects or you sell, and then the council can ask you to get it approved, change it or remove it. See [what happens if you build without council approval](/guides/council-approval/what-happens-if-you-build-without-council-approval/).

**Can I live in a shed?**
Not in a garden shed built under these exemptions. They're for sheds that aren't lived in. Turning a shed into a studio, bedroom or granny flat needs a different approval altogether.

**Does a shipping container need council approval?**
In NSW, yes. A shipping container is excluded from the exempt shed rules, so it needs approval even if it's within the size limit.

**Is the size on a shed kit the floor area?**
Not always. Some advertised sizes are roof dimensions, which are bigger than the floor. Check the floor area and the overall height against your state's limits before you buy.
