Tucking the shed hard against the back fence feels like the obvious way to save lawn. Whether you can depends on your state, the shed's height, and whether it needs approval in the first place.

## Setbacks by state

The short answer: Queensland is the most generous about building close to the fence, and NSW is the strictest for sheds built without approval.

| State | How close a shed can be to a side or rear fence |
|---|---|
| NSW | Exempt shed: at least 900 mm (5 m in rural zones and zone R5). Closer needs approval |
| QLD | 1.5 m as standard. Closer, even on the boundary, if no more than 4.5 m high, total length within 1.5 m of the boundary is 9 m or less, and at least 1.5 m from a neighbour's habitable room window |
| VIC | Permit-free shed: within 1 m only if no more than 2.4 m high (3 m elsewhere). A permitted shed can go on the boundary within limits |
| WA | Permit-free shed: within 1 m only if no more than 2.4 m high (3 m elsewhere) |
| SA, TAS, ACT, NT | Check with your council. We couldn't confirm their setbacks from an official source |

Every state also limits sheds in front of the house, so plan for the back or side yard.

## NSW: 900 mm for an exempt shed

In NSW a garden shed needs no approval only if it meets all the exempt development standards, and one of them is distance: at least **900 mm from each lot boundary**, or **5 m** in rural zones and zone R5. Armidale Regional Council's summary of the rules adds that the shed can be up to 20 m² in most zones (50 m² in rural and R5 zones), no more than **3 m high**, and in the rear yard.

If you want the shed closer than 900 mm, it's no longer exempt. You'll need a complying development certificate (available for garden sheds in some zones) or a development application. Your council or a private certifier will tell you which applies and what setback they'll accept.

On bushfire-prone land, an exempt shed must be built of non-combustible materials. Connecting roof water to the stormwater system needs council approval under section 68 of the Local Government Act 1993.

## Queensland: 1.5 m, or on the boundary with conditions

In Queensland the siting rules come from the Queensland Development Code (MP 1.1 for lots under 450 m², MP 1.2 for lots of 450 m² and over). The standard side and rear clearance is **1.5 m** for a building up to 4.5 m high. A class 10a building such as a shed or garage can be closer, with no setback at all, if:

- it's **no more than 4.5 m high**
- the **total length of all buildings within 1.5 m** of that boundary is **no more than 9 m**
- it's **at least 1.5 m from a window in a habitable room** of the neighbour's house

Rockhampton Regional Council's summary of the code adds three practical points:

- A wall less than **750 mm** from a boundary must be maintenance free, such as pre-finished steel sheeting or unpainted masonry, because you can't get in to paint it.
- Anything closer than **300 mm** to the boundary needs a survey of the block, so you don't build over the line.
- Under the Building Code, a shed less than **900 mm** from a side or rear boundary or a house may need to be fire rated.

Setbacks are measured to the outermost part of the shed, usually the gutter, not the wall. From the street, the usual setback is 6 m on lots of 450 m² or more and 3 to 4 m on smaller lots.

A Queensland shed only skips building approval if it's 10 m² or less in plan area, no more than 2.4 m high (mean height 2.1 m), has no side longer than 5 m, and isn't in a tropical cyclone area. Brisbane City Council lists boundary setbacks among the standards every shed must meet, approved or not.

## Victoria: the 2.4 m rule within 1 m

In Victoria a shed needs no building permit only if it's freestanding, **10 m² or less**, **no more than 3 m high**, or **no more than 2.4 m high within 1 m** of another property, not masonry, and not further forward than existing buildings. You also can't build over an easement or within 1 m of an authority asset without the service authority's written consent.

Bigger sheds need a permit, and then the siting rules in the Building Regulations 2018 apply. Frankston City Council's checklist says a shed on a side or rear boundary may be possible with:

- a **3.2 m maximum average wall height**
- no more than **200 mm** from the boundary, to fit the gutter
- a total length of all boundary walls of no more than **10 m plus a quarter of the boundary length beyond 10 m**
- the shed at least **1 m clear of your own habitable room windows**, and at least **900 mm from habitable buildings** to avoid fire rating

If a boundary wall doesn't meet these, the council can still consent to it, but the neighbours get a chance to comment first. Your building surveyor will confirm the setback for anything that isn't on the boundary.

## WA: 2.4 m within 1 m of the boundary

In WA a shed, garage or carport needs no building permit if it's **freestanding**, **10 m² or less**, **no more than 2.4 m high within 1 m of the boundary** (or 3 m high if it's 1 m or more away), not masonry, and no closer to the street than the house. Anything else needs a building permit, and your council's planning rules may set their own setbacks.

## Gutters, roof water and the fence itself

Whatever your state, a few things catch people out:

- **Measure to the gutter.** In Queensland setbacks are measured to the outermost projection. Elsewhere, ask your council how it measures, and allow for the gutter if in doubt.
- **Keep roof water on your side.** Pipe it to your stormwater or a rainwater tank. Water spilling over the fence is a quick way to fall out with the neighbours.
- **Don't use the fence as a wall.** A dividing fence is usually shared with your neighbour, so the shed should stand on its own frame and base.
- **Leave room to build and maintain it.** A shed 300 mm off the fence is very hard to assemble, paint or clear of leaves.

## What you can do yourself, and when to call a builder or certifier

A kit shed within your state's exempt limits is a fair weekend job for most owners: marking out the setbacks, laying the slab or base, building the frame, and fitting walls, roof and anchors to the kit's engineering for your wind region.

Call a certifier, building surveyor or your council first when:

- you want the shed closer to the fence than the exempt setback
- it's bigger or taller than the exempt limits
- it's near a neighbour's window, an easement or a sewer line
- it may need fire rating because it's close to the boundary or your house
- you're on bushfire-prone, flood or heritage land

For the shed size limits in each state, see [how big a shed can you build without council approval](/guides/council-approval/shed-without-council-approval/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I build a shed on the boundary in QLD?**
Yes, if it's no more than 4.5 m high, the total length of buildings within 1.5 m of that boundary stays at 9 m or less, and it's at least 1.5 m from a habitable room window next door. Walls within 750 mm of the boundary must be maintenance free.

**How close to the fence can a shed be in NSW?**
An exempt shed must be at least 900 mm from every boundary, or 5 m in rural zones and R5. To go closer, you need a complying development certificate or development application.

**Do I need my neighbour's permission to build a shed near the fence?**
Not for exempt development in NSW, though NSW Planning recommends telling them. In Victoria, a boundary wall that needs the council's consent goes to the neighbours for comment. Talking to them first saves trouble everywhere.

**Does the 900 mm include the gutter?**
Ask your council how it measures. In Queensland setbacks are measured to the outermost projection, which is usually the gutter, so measuring to the gutter is the safe choice.
