A rainwater tank is one of the few backyard projects that pays you back, and in most of Australia a standard tank beside the house doesn't need a council application. The catch is that "standard" has a definition in each state, and the part people most often get wrong isn't the tank at all. It's the pipe that connects it to the house.

> Exempt doesn't mean "no rules". A tank without approval still has to sit on a sound base, overflow to the right place and be kept free of mosquitoes.

## Does a rainwater tank need council approval?

Usually not, for an above-ground tank on a house block within your state's limits. Here's the picture by state. Where we couldn't confirm a state-wide rule, we've used a council example and said so.

| State | No approval needed if… |
|---|---|
| NSW | Up to 10,000 L in residential zones, behind the building line, at least 450 mm from boundaries if more than 1.8 m above ground, not resting on the house footings, with a screened rain head, first-flush device and mosquito-proof design |
| VIC | Councils such as Boroondara and Monash say a freestanding tank doesn't need a building permit. A tank supported by a building does. A planning permit can apply under a heritage or flood overlay |
| QLD | Outside cyclone areas, state guidance says approval is only needed for a tank over 10 m² in plan area and more than 2.4 m high. Brisbane and Logan set tighter triggers (below) |
| WA | Varies by council. Joondalup, for example, allows a small prefabricated steel or fibreglass tank under 5,000 L, no more than 1.8 m high, on the ground or a stand up to 300 mm |
| SA | Onkaparinga Council says most tanks on existing homes don't need approval, and points to tanks over 10 m² with a height of 4 m including the stand as ones to lodge. Check with yours |
| ACT | Up to 20 kL (20,000 L) and no higher than 3 m above ground. In front of the building line, the whole tank must be buried |
| TAS, NT | Check with your council, we couldn't confirm a rule from an official source |

## NSW: the full list of exempt conditions

NSW has the clearest rules. An above-ground tank is exempt development if it meets all of these:

- **Capacity:** up to 10,000 L on land not zoned rural or environmental (RU1, RU2, RU3, RU4, RU6, R5, E2, E3 or E4).
- **Rural and environmental zones:** the tank must be at least 10 m from every lot boundary.
- **Boundaries elsewhere:** if the tank is more than 1.8 m above existing ground level, it must be at least 450 mm from any lot boundary.
- **Position:** behind the building line of any road frontage. On a heritage item, it must be in the rear yard.
- **Base:** it can't rest on the footings of an existing building, or need more than 1 m of cut or fill.
- **Fittings:** a screened rain head, a first-flush device with an automatic resetting valve, and a design that stops mosquitoes breeding.
- **Overflow:** connected to an existing stormwater system, not pouring onto the neighbours.
- **Signs and noise:** a "rainwater" sign, and any pump in a soundproof enclosure.
- **Drinking water:** it can't interconnect with a system supplying drinking water unless it meets the water authority's requirements.

It's not exempt in a foreshore area or an environmentally sensitive area. Below-ground tanks are only exempt in some rural zones. Sydney Water's summary matches: tanks storing up to 10,000 litres don't normally need council approval, but check local requirements.

## Victoria: permits depend on how the tank is supported

Victorian councils generally say a freestanding tank needs no building permit. Boroondara says one "supported" by a roof or wall does, and a tank inside a building may need one because of its weight. A supported tank less than 500 mm from a boundary also needs report and consent.

Planning permits are separate. Monash says no planning permit is needed for a tank up to 4,500 litres on a residential property, except under a Heritage Overlay. Boroondara says a tank visible from a street or park needs a planning permit in a Heritage Overlay, and so does one over 10,000 litres in a flood overlay. Your council's planning department can confirm which overlays apply to your block.

## Queensland: state limits, with council triggers

The state's guidance says you may need building approval in a tropical cyclone area, which in Queensland is Wind Region C, from Bundaberg north to the Northern Territory border. Elsewhere, approval is only needed for a tank that's more than 10 m² in plan area and more than 2.4 m high.

Councils apply their own triggers. Brisbane says a tank needs building approval if it is:

- within 450 mm of any property boundary
- more than 2.4 m high, including the stand
- more than 5 m long
- more than 1 m below ground, if submerged or semi-submerged
- attached to a building and within 900 mm of a second building.

Logan lists a tank (including its stand) over 10 m² in plan area, with any side longer than 5 m, higher than 2.4 m, or more than 3.6 m in diameter. If you're unsure, a private building certifier can tell you whether approval is needed.

## Connecting the tank to the house needs a licensed plumber

Plumbing a tank into the toilet, the washing machine, the hot water system or the mains supply is licensed plumbing work. This is where most of the rules sit:

- **NSW:** Sydney Water recommends a licensed plumber experienced with tanks, and says the plumber must book and pay for an NSW Fair Trading inspection for tanks connected to a toilet, washing machine or hot water system.
- **Queensland:** Brisbane says internal connections, such as toilet flushing or cold-water washing machine taps, "must be carried out by a licensed plumber". The state's guidance says the plumber lodges a Form 4 (notifiable work) for a tank connected for indoor use in an existing house. A tank for outdoor use only doesn't need a Form 4.
- **Victoria:** Boroondara says the tank must be installed by a licensed plumber and connected to the stormwater system at a legal point of discharge.
- **Western Australia:** since 10 February 2025, plumbing that supplies water from a rainwater tank to a building has been regulated water supply plumbing work, done by a licensed plumber or a holder of the restricted rainwater storage permit.
- **Mains backup:** the national enHealth guidance says water authorities don't allow a direct connection between a rainwater system and the mains. A backflow prevention device or similar is needed, which is part of why this is a plumber's job.

Sydney Water also suggests a fail-safe top-up connection or an automatic switching device so the house still has water when the tank runs dry.

## Mosquito screens and first flush

Every tank needs both, and in NSW they're conditions of the exemption.

- **Mosquito screens:** cover every opening, including the overflow. Logan Council says screens must have a mesh size of 1 mm or less, made of brass, copper, aluminium or stainless steel gauze, or the openings must have flap valves. Victoria's Health department says no coarser than 1 to 1.5 mm.
- **First flush:** the first rain off a roof carries dust, leaves and droppings. enHealth suggests diverting the first 20 to 25 litres from an average roof. The diverter must be empty before the next shower to work, so check it often. Victoria's guidance suggests every three months.
- **Leaf screens and gutters:** a screened rain head at the downpipe keeps the worst out. Clean gutters make everything else work better, see our [gutter cleaning guide](/guides/home-maintenance/how-to-clean-gutters/).

## What you can do yourself, and when to call a licensed plumber

**Usually yours to do:**

- Check your state and council limits, overlays and any strata rules before you buy.
- Choose the tank size and shape to fit the space, inside the limits.
- Prepare a level, compacted base to the manufacturer's specification. Water weighs about a kilogram a litre, so a full 5,000 litre tank weighs around 5 tonnes.
- Fit and maintain the leaf screens, mosquito mesh and first-flush diverter, and keep the gutters clean.
- Inspect the screens and roof every six months and look for sediment in the tank every two to three years, as enHealth recommends.

**Call a licensed plumber for:**

- Connecting the tank to toilets, the laundry, the hot water system or any indoor tap.
- A mains top-up, automatic switching device or backflow prevention.
- Connecting the overflow to stormwater, if your state treats it as licensed drainage work. Ask before doing it yourself.
- A pump wired into the house: the plumber does the water side, and any new power point or hardwired connection needs a licensed electrician.

**Live in a strata building?** In NSW, installing a rainwater tank is a minor renovation that needs the owners corporation's approval at a strata meeting first.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need approval for a 5,000 litre tank?**
In most residential areas, probably not, if it's freestanding and within your state's height and boundary limits. In NSW, up to 10,000 litres can be exempt. Some WA councils draw the line lower, so check yours.

**How close to the fence can a rainwater tank be?**
In NSW, a tank more than 1.8 m above ground must be at least 450 mm from the boundary. In Brisbane, a tank within 450 mm of a boundary needs building approval. Elsewhere, check with your council.

**Can I connect my rainwater tank to the toilet myself?**
No. Connecting a tank to the toilet, laundry or hot water is licensed plumbing work, and in several states the plumber has to notify or arrange an inspection.

**Can I drink water from my rainwater tank?**
The enHealth guidance says the microbial quality of rainwater is not as reliable as high quality mains water, particularly after rain that follows a long dry spell. The risk from a well-maintained roof and tank is very low, but it rises with less maintenance and without a first-flush diverter. If you're in doubt about the quality, enHealth says to disinfect or boil the water before using it.

**Does an underground tank need approval?**
In NSW, below-ground tanks are only exempt in some rural zones. In Brisbane, a tank more than 1 m below ground needs approval. Elsewhere, check with your council.

**What are the rainwater tank requirements in NSW?**
To skip approval, an above-ground tank must be up to 10,000 litres (outside rural and environmental zones), behind the building line, at least 450 mm from boundaries if it's more than 1.8 m above ground, not resting on the house footings, and fitted with a screened rain head, a first-flush device and a mosquito-proof design, with the overflow going to stormwater.

**Do I need a permit for a rainwater tank in Victoria?**
Councils such as Boroondara and Monash say a freestanding tank doesn't need a building permit, but one supported by a roof or wall does. A planning permit can apply under a heritage or flood overlay.

**Does a rainwater tank need council approval in Queensland?**
Outside cyclone areas, state guidance says only if it's over 10 m² in plan area and more than 2.4 m high. Councils set tighter triggers: in Brisbane, approval is needed within 450 mm of a boundary or over 2.4 m high including the stand.
