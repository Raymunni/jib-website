"It's been there more than 7 years, so it's legal now." It gets said at barbecues, by sellers, and sometimes by agents. It isn't true anywhere in Australia, and believing it can cost you real money when you sell.

## Where the 7 years comes from

The number is real, it just doesn't mean what people think. In **NSW**, an owner (or a buyer under contract) can apply to the council through the NSW Planning Portal for a **building information certificate**. Once issued, it stops the council from ordering the building to be repaired, demolished or altered **for 7 years**.

Three things the myth leaves out:

1. **You have to apply for it.** Nothing happens automatically because time has passed.
2. **It doesn't approve the work.** The NSW Planning Portal is clear that it doesn't make unauthorised work lawful, and it can't be used to get an occupation certificate.
3. **It's NSW only.** No other state has the same certificate.

To get one, the council often asks for plans and engineering, and it may ask you to fix defects first.

## Queensland: no time limit on fixing it

In Queensland, the council's usual first step for unapproved building work is a **show cause notice**, giving you **20 business days** to respond. If it isn't satisfied, it can issue an **enforcement notice** to get the work approved, brought up to standard or removed. Councils send these for old work as well as new. The age of the structure doesn't change whether it needed approval.

The good news: many councils close the matter once the owner gets the work approved. In Logan City Council's fact sheet example, a shed was approved after a show cause notice and no further action was taken.

## Victoria: building notices for old work too

In Victoria, the municipal building surveyor can issue a **building notice** to the owner, giving **30 days** to show cause why the work was done without a permit, followed by a **building order** if needed. Again, the clock starts when the notice is issued, not when the shed went up.

## When old unapproved work actually bites

Most unapproved work sits quietly for years. It usually comes up at one of these moments:

- **You sell.** The buyer's building inspector or solicitor asks for approval paperwork. Buyers use the gap to negotiate the price down, and some lenders want it fixed before settlement. This is the most expensive time to deal with it.
- **A neighbour complains.** Often about height, overlooking or water running onto their side.
- **You renovate.** A certifier may not sign off new work while unapproved work sits on the same site.
- **You claim on insurance** for damage involving the structure.

## Bought a house with unapproved work?

You inherit the problem: the obligations sit with the current owner, not whoever built it. If you're buying, ask your conveyancer about any structure that doesn't appear on the council's approval records before you sign. If you already own it, the fix is the same as for any unapproved work.

## How to sort it out

1. **Check whether it ever needed approval.** Plenty of small sheds, low decks and fences never did. Our guides for [sheds](/guides/council-approval/shed-without-council-approval/), [decks](/guides/council-approval/deck-council-approval-nsw/) and [fences](/guides/council-approval/fence-height-without-council-approval/) have the size and height limits.
2. **Gather what you have:** photos, kit engineering, receipts, any plans.
3. **Talk to a private building certifier or your council's building team** before you list the house. They'll tell you whether it needs approval for the existing work, changes, or engineering sign-off.
4. **In NSW**, consider a building information certificate before selling.
5. **Fix real safety problems first:** loose posts, missing tie-downs and decks without balustrades matter more than the paperwork.

The full process, state by state, is in [what happens if you build without council approval](/guides/council-approval/what-happens-if-you-build-without-council-approval/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Does unapproved building work become legal after 7 years?**
No. No Australian state has a rule like that. Unapproved work stays unapproved until it's approved or removed.

**What is the 7 year rule in NSW?**
A building information certificate stops the council ordering repairs, alterations or demolition of a building for 7 years. You have to apply for it, and it doesn't approve the work.

**Can I sell a house with an unapproved shed?**
Yes, but the buyer's inspection will usually find it, and it often costs you in negotiation or a rush to get it certified before settlement.

**Can the council make me remove a shed the previous owner built?**
It can require the current owner to get it approved, fix it or remove it. That's why it's worth checking before you buy.
