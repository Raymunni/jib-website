Fence height rules split into two groups: **front fences** (facing the street), which are kept low for sight lines and streetscape, and **side and rear fences**, which can be taller for privacy.

## Limits by state

| State | Side and rear | Front / street frontage | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| NSW | 1.8 m (1.2 m if masonry) | 1.2 m | Secondary frontages must be partly see-through; not exempt on or next to a heritage item |
| VIC | 2 m | 1.5 m within 3 m of the street | 1 m within 9 m of a corner intersection; masonry front fences over 1.2 m need a permit |
| QLD | 2 m | Check with your council | Pool fences excluded |
| WA | 1.8 m (non-masonry, behind the front setback) | Needs a permit | Masonry and pool fences need a permit; councils add front-fence policies |
| SA | Check with your council | Check with your council | We couldn't confirm SA's limits from an official source |

### NSW corner blocks

In NSW, a fence along a secondary road frontage must be at least 20% see-through above 400 mm, with piers no wider than 350 mm. A solid 1.8 m fence is allowed along the rear half of the secondary frontage. On sloping ground, stepped fences can reach 1.5 m (front) or 2.2 m (side and rear) at the steps.

## Dividing fences: the neighbour side

Approval is only half of it. A fence on the boundary is also covered by dividing-fence laws, which decide who pays. In NSW, neighbours generally share the cost of a "sufficient" dividing fence equally under the Dividing Fences Act 1991, and anyone wanting something better pays the difference. Other states have similar laws with their own notice requirements.

- Talk to your neighbour before you start.
- Agree the height, material and cost split in writing.
- Confirm where the boundary actually is, an old fence isn't always on it.

## Before you build

- Check for heritage, bushfire and easement restrictions.
- Never use a boundary fence as a pool barrier without checking the pool-fencing rules.
- On bushfire-prone land in NSW, exempt fences must be non-combustible or hardwood.

For sheds, decks, retaining walls and pergolas, see [do you need council approval?](/guides/council-approval/)

## Frequently asked questions

**How high can a fence be in NSW without council approval?**
Side and rear fences up to 1.8 m, or 1.2 m if masonry, and front fences up to 1.2 m. Fences on or next to a heritage item aren't exempt.

**How high can a fence be in Queensland?**
Up to 2 m without approval. Pool fences are excluded and have their own rules, and councils can add local requirements, so check yours for front fences.

**How high can a front fence be in Victoria?**
1.5 m within 3 m of the street, and 1 m within 9 m of a corner intersection. A masonry front fence over 1.2 m needs a permit. Side and rear fences can go to 2 m.

**How high can a fence be in WA?**
A non-masonry side or rear fence behind the front setback can be up to 1.8 m without a permit. Front fences, masonry fences and pool fences need a permit, and many councils have their own front-fence policies.

**Who pays for a dividing fence?**
Usually both neighbours. In NSW, the Dividing Fences Act 1991 has neighbours share the cost of a "sufficient" fence equally, and whoever wants something better pays the difference. Other states have similar laws with their own notice steps. Agree the height, material and cost split in writing first.

**What are the fence rules for a corner block in NSW?**
Along a secondary road frontage, the fence must be at least 20% see-through above 400 mm, with piers no wider than 350 mm. A solid 1.8 m fence is allowed along the rear half of that frontage.

**How high can a fence be on sloping ground?**
In NSW, a stepped fence can reach 1.5 m at the steps for a front fence and 2.2 m for a side or rear fence, while staying exempt.

**Can I use my boundary fence as a pool fence?**
Not without checking the pool-fencing rules first. Pool barriers have their own height, gap and climbability requirements, so an ordinary boundary fence often doesn't comply. See [pool fence rules in NSW](/guides/home-maintenance/pool-fence-rules-nsw/) or [Queensland](/guides/home-maintenance/pool-fence-rules-qld/).
