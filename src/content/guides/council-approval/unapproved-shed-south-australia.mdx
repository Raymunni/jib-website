Someone in the street gets a letter from council about their shed, and suddenly everyone's wondering about their own. In South Australia the answer depends on three things: how big the shed is, when it went up, and whether council has already acted.

## First: did it ever need approval?

Plenty of garden sheds never did. Under Schedule 4 of SA's planning regulations, an outbuilding doesn't need development approval if **all** of these are true:

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| Floor area | 15 m² or less |
| Height | No part higher than 2.5 m above natural ground |
| Roof span | No span over 3 m between supporting walls or posts |
| Location | Not in front of the house's building line facing the main street |
| Corner blocks | Not within 900 mm of the side street boundary, and clear of the 6 m corner zone |
| Use | Detached, ancillary to a house on the site, and not lived in |

It also has to be outside flood zones and any other overlay the Planning and Design Code names for this rule, and not on a local heritage place. Look up your address at code.plan.sa.gov.au to check those.

If your shed fits all of that, there's nothing to fix. If it's bigger, taller or in the wrong spot, read on.

## What council can do: enforcement notices

Under **section 213** of the Planning, Development and Infrastructure Act 2016, a council that reasonably believes the Act has been breached can give a written direction to:

- stop the work or use, and
- **make good the breach** within a set time.

If you don't comply, council can do the work itself and recover the cost from you as a debt, which can be charged against the land. You have **14 days** to appeal a notice, and the direction still applies while the appeal runs.

**The time limit:** section 213 says a direction can't be given if the breach happened **more than 12 months** earlier.

## After 12 months: the ERD Court

That doesn't mean an older shed is safe. Under **section 214**, council (or anyone) can apply to the Environment, Resources and Development Court for orders to remedy the breach. The court can order you to stop, to make good the breach in a way and time it sets, to pay compensation, or other action it thinks appropriate. In practice, that's how a shed can be ordered changed or removed.

**The time limit:** proceedings must start within **3 years** of the breach, or later only with the **Attorney-General's authorisation**.

The court can also adjourn a case so the owner can apply for the approval that should have been sought. Councils generally prefer that outcome.

## Fines

Section 215 says a person must not undertake development contrary to the Act. The maximum penalty is **$120,000**, and contravening a section 213 enforcement direction carries a maximum of **$20,000**, plus daily default penalties. Those are maximums, set for serious cases. A garden shed rarely goes near them, but they show why it's worth sorting out rather than ignoring a letter.

## Old sheds: is it legal after a few years?

No. The time limits above stop council **acting** after a while (without the Attorney-General's sign-off), but they don't approve the shed. It stays unapproved. That matters most when you sell: a buyer's inspector or conveyancer can ask for approval paperwork, and buyers use the gap to negotiate.

Whether an unapproved building counts as a single breach on the day it went up, or something ongoing, is a legal question that depends on the facts. If council has written to you about an older shed, get advice before you respond.

The myth that work becomes legal after 7 years is covered in our [7 year rule guide](/guides/council-approval/seven-year-rule-unapproved-building-work/).

## How to sort it out

1. **Measure it.** Floor area, the highest point above natural ground, and the widest roof span. If it's within the Schedule 4 limits, you may not need anything.
2. **Look up your address** in the Planning and Design Code for overlays.
3. **Gather what you have:** the kit's engineering, receipts, photos of the slab and tie-downs.
4. **Lodge a development application on PlanSA** for the existing shed if it needs approval. A building certifier or your council's building team can tell you what drawings and engineering it needs.
5. **If you get a notice,** read the deadline, respond in writing, and don't ignore it. Getting the shed approved is usually the end of it.

## Frequently asked questions

**How big a shed can I build in SA without approval?**
Up to 15 m² of floor area, no part over 2.5 m high, and no roof span over 3 m, behind the front of the house, as long as no overlay or heritage listing applies.

**Can council make me remove a shed in SA?**
Council can direct you to make good a breach within 12 months of it. After that, it needs the ERD Court, and proceedings must start within 3 years unless the Attorney-General authorises later action.

**What is the fine for building a shed without approval in SA?**
Up to $120,000 for undertaking development contrary to the Act, though that maximum is for serious cases.

**Can I get approval for a shed that's already built?**
Yes. Lodge a development application on PlanSA for the existing shed. You may need engineering or changes before it's approved.
