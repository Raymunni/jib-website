Victoria's Better Health Channel puts it simply: you're **likely to be bitten by mosquitoes that breed in your own yard**. They can breed in the tiniest amount of water, so a forgotten bucket after a storm is enough. The fix is a ten-minute walk around the yard once a week.

## The weekly tip-out check

Queensland Health says to check around your home **weekly** and **tip out, wipe out, throw out or dry store** anything that can hold water.

| Where | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Buckets, tubs, plastic containers | Empty, wipe out, store upside down or under cover |
| Kids' toys, wheelbarrows, trailers, boats | Cover, tip over or store under cover |
| Tarps | Pull tight so water can't pool |
| Old tyres | Throw out or store dry |
| Pot plant saucers | Fill with sand |
| Bird baths, pet bowls, vases | Change the water at least weekly, more in hot weather |
| Bromeliads and palm fronds | Reduce bromeliads, clean up fallen fronds |

## Around the house

- **Gutters:** clean them regularly so water runs freely. Our [gutter cleaning guide](/guides/home-maintenance/how-to-clean-gutters/) covers doing it safely.
- **Drain sumps:** clean them and make sure water drains away.
- **Leaking taps:** fix them, so puddles don't form.
- **Pools:** keep them maintained and chlorinated, or securely covered if not in use. Don't let water pool in an empty pool.
- **Fishponds:** keep them tidy, with minimal plants around the edges.
- **Lawns and shrubs:** keep them trimmed, so there are fewer places for mosquitoes to rest.

## Rainwater tanks

Better Health says tanks **must be completely sealed**. Check the lids, covers and inlet pipes for gaps, and fit **removable screen mesh** to the outlet end of the overflow and to all inlets. Queensland Health also says to check the screens and seals. Our [rainwater tank guide](/guides/council-approval/rainwater-tank-without-council-approval/) covers the rules for installing one.

## Keep them out of the house

Better Health says to keep **fly screens on windows, doors, vents and chimneys**, with mesh **no coarser than 1.2 mm** (12 x 12 meshes per 25 mm). Patch or replace torn screens before summer. Indoors, use a knockdown spray on mosquitoes you can see, following the label.

## Can you be fined?

Yes, in Queensland. Queensland Health says that **under the Public Health Act, your local council can fine you** if you're breeding mosquitoes around your home. Other councils run their own mosquito programs, so check with yours.

A torn screen undoes all of that. Here's [how to fix a torn fly screen](/guides/how-to/how-to-fix-a-torn-fly-screen/), by patching it or replacing the mesh.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should I check the yard?**
Weekly, according to Queensland Health, and after rain.

**Why fill pot plant saucers with sand?**
The sand soaks up the water, so it can't pool where mosquitoes lay eggs.

**Do mosquitoes breed in gutters?**
Yes, if leaves block them and water pools. Queensland Health says to clean gutters regularly.

**Do mosquitoes breed in bromeliads?**
They can, because water collects in the centre of the leaves. Queensland Health suggests reducing bromeliads in the yard, and cleaning up fallen palm fronds, which also hold water.

**Do mosquitoes breed in swimming pools?**
Not in a pool that's maintained and chlorinated. Queensland Health says to keep pools maintained, or securely covered if they're not in use, and not to let water pool in an empty pool.

**How do I stop mosquitoes breeding in a bird bath?**
Change the water at least once a week, and more often in hot weather, as Queensland Health advises for bird baths, pet bowls and vases.

**How do I stop mosquitoes breeding in a rainwater tank?**
Keep the tank completely sealed. Better Health says to check the lids, covers and inlet pipes for gaps, and fit removable screen mesh to all inlets and the outlet end of the overflow.

**What mesh size keeps mosquitoes out of the house?**
Better Health says fly screen mesh should be no coarser than 1.2 mm (12 by 12 meshes per 25 mm), on windows, doors, vents and chimneys.
