British homes take a beating from cold, wet winters, so most of the maintenance year revolves around getting ready for them and fixing what they did. Here's what to do and when. The legal bits differ across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, so they're flagged where they apply.

## Every month

- **Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms.** Press the test button on each one. Replace batteries as soon as they chirp.
- **Run water in rooms you don't use**, like a spare bathroom, so traps don't dry out and let in drain smells.
- **Check for damp and mould** in corners, behind wardrobes and around windows, especially in winter. Wipe it off early and improve the ventilation before it spreads.

## Autumn (September to November)

- **Book the boiler service** before the cold sets in, when engineers are less busy. See below.
- **Bleed the radiators.** Cold at the top means trapped air. Turn the heating off, open the bleed valve with a radiator key until water appears, then check the boiler pressure (usually about 1 to 1.5 bar cold).
- **Clear the gutters and downpipes** once most of the leaves have fallen. Blocked gutters overflow down the walls and are a common cause of damp.
- **Lag exposed pipes** in the loft, garage and outside walls, and fit a cover on the outside tap (or isolate and drain it).
- **Find your stopcock** and make sure it turns, so you can shut the water off fast if a pipe bursts.
- **Sweep the chimney** before the first fire of the season (see below).
- **Check draught-proofing** around doors and windows.

## Winter (December to February)

- **Keep the heating ticking over** on a low setting if you go away, so pipes don't freeze.
- **Check the loft** after storms and heavy snow for leaks or daylight through the roof.
- **Keep gullies and drains clear** of leaves so surface water can get away.
- **Watch for condensation** on windows each morning. Wipe it off and open trickle vents to stop mould.

## Spring (March to May)

- **Walk round the roof line** (from the ground, or with binoculars) for slipped or missing tiles and damaged flashing after winter storms.
- **Check fences, gates and sheds** for storm damage and rot. Treat timber on a dry day.
- **Reseal around baths, showers and sinks** where the silicone has gone black or split.
- **Clear the gutters again** if you have trees nearby.
- **Service the lawnmower** and check outdoor taps and hoses for frost damage.

## Summer (June to August)

- **Paint and treat outside woodwork** (windows, doors, fences) while it's dry.
- **Repoint and repair** cracked render or mortar so it's sound before autumn rain.
- **Clean and check windows and seals.**
- **Book jobs that need dry weather** with tradespeople early, as they fill up fast.

## Every year

- **Gas boiler service.** Have it serviced once a year by a **Gas Safe registered** engineer. It isn't a legal requirement for owner-occupiers, but it's the standard advice for safety (carbon monoxide) and efficiency, and many boiler warranties require it. By law, any work on a gas boiler must be done by a Gas Safe registered engineer.
- **Chimney sweep.** HETAS advises sweeping **at least twice a year** if you burn wood or bituminous house coal, and **at least once a year** for smokeless fuels. Best timing is just before the heating season, and after a long period without use.
- **Test your RCDs** (the test buttons on the consumer unit) and check for any that trip often.

## Every few years

- **Electrical check (EICR).** For an owner-occupied home, have an Electrical Installation Condition Report at least **every 10 years**, and when you move in. It's not a legal requirement for homeowners. (Landlords must have one every 5 years.)
- **Replace smoke and carbon monoxide alarms** when they reach the end of their life, usually 7 to 10 years. The date is printed on the back.
- **Repaint exterior woodwork** every 3 to 5 years, depending on exposure.

## Alarms: what the law says

- **Scotland:** since February 2022, every home must have **interlinked** alarms: a smoke alarm in the living room (or the room used most during the day), a smoke alarm in every hallway and landing, and a heat alarm in the kitchen. Where there's a carbon-fuelled appliance (a boiler, fire or stove) or a flue, you also need a carbon monoxide alarm.
- **England, Wales and Northern Ireland:** there's no law requiring owner-occupiers to fit alarms, but it's strongly recommended: at least one working smoke alarm on every floor, and a carbon monoxide alarm in any room with a gas boiler, fire or stove. Landlords have their own legal duties.

## DIY or call someone?

You can do most of this list yourself: bleeding radiators, clearing gutters from a safe ladder, lagging pipes, resealing, testing alarms. Leave gas work to a **Gas Safe registered** engineer, chimneys to a **HETAS approved** sweep, and electrical testing to a registered electrician. For what electrical work you can do yourself in England, see [Part P explained](/guides/diy-legal/uk-part-p-diy-electrical-work/).
