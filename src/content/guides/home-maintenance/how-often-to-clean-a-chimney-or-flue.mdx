Victoria's fire services reported 70 house chimney fires in one year, and more than 530 over five. The advice from fire services every winter is the same: get the chimney or flue cleaned once a year. Autumn is the time to book it.

## The rule: once a year, before winter

- **CFA (Victoria):** chimneys and flues should be cleaned **annually**, professionally.
- **SA Country Fire Service:** have chimneys and flues inspected and cleaned **at least once a year, ideally at the start of winter**, by a qualified tradesperson and as the manufacturer recommends.
- **ACCC Product Safety:** fireplaces, combustion heaters, flues and chimneys should be maintained by a qualified person **at least once a year, at the start of winter**.
- **NSW EPA:** clean the chimney every year to stop creosote building up.

If you burn most nights through a long winter, ask your sweep whether a mid-season clean makes sense too.

## Why it matters: creosote

Every fire leaves residue inside the flue. Over time this builds into **creosote**, which the CFA describes as a black, tar-like residue that is **highly flammable** and can clog the chimney.

The CFA says creosote builds up **faster** when you burn the wrong fuel, such as green wood or treated timber. SA's CFS also says the clean should check the flue is free of corrosion.

## The torch check

The CFA suggests using a torch to check for soot build-up or blockages:

1. Wait until the fire has been out and cold for a day.
2. For an open fireplace, open the damper and shine the torch up the chimney. For a wood heater, look into the firebox and up the flue as far as you can.
3. Look for thick black or shiny, tar-like deposits, flaking, or anything blocking the way, like nests or leaves.

If you see heavy build-up, don't light a fire until it's been cleaned.

## Burn the right wood

The NSW EPA says to burn small logs of **aged, dry hardwood**. Unseasoned wood can be up to 50% water, which means more smoke and more creosote. Never burn rubbish, driftwood, or treated or painted timber.

Keep the fire burning bright. The EPA says a fire should only smoke for a few minutes when you light it and when you add wood, and not to let it smoulder overnight. ## Fire safety around the fireplace

From the CFA, SA CFS and ACCC Product Safety:

- Use a **fire screen** or guard in front of an open fire.
- Keep wood and anything that can burn **at least one metre** from the fire.
- **Put the fire out** before bed or before you leave the house.
- **Dispose of ashes safely.** SA CFS says never to put hot ashes in household or council bins or plastic buckets. Let them go fully cold in a metal bucket first.
- Make sure you have **working smoke alarms**, especially in bedrooms, hallways and living areas.

## Can I clean my own chimney?

Fire services and ACCC Product Safety all say to use a qualified person. A sweep can see damage or corrosion inside the flue that you can't, and clean it without making a mess of the house. Getting on the roof is also a fall risk. Check your heater's manual, as some manufacturers set their own service requirements.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should a chimney be cleaned in Australia?**
Once a year, before winter. The CFA, SA CFS, ACCC Product Safety and the NSW EPA all say annually.

**How do I know if my chimney needs cleaning?**
Shine a torch up the chimney or flue. Thick soot, shiny tar-like deposits or blockages mean it's due. If it's been more than a year, it's due anyway.

**What causes creosote build-up?**
Normal burning, but it builds faster with green wood, treated timber, or a fire left to smoulder.

**When is the best time to clean a chimney?**
Late summer or autumn, before the first fire of winter.
