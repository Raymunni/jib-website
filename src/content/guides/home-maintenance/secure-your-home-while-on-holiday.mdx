The car's packed, the kids are in, and someone asks if the back door's locked. A house left empty over Christmas is an easy target, and most of what keeps it safe takes an hour the weekend before you go.

## What police say works

Queensland Police say most thieves are **opportunistic**: they look for quick, easy ways in and things they can see. Their home security advice comes down to four ideas:

- make it **easy for an offender to be seen**
- make it **hard to get in**
- make it **hard to leave with your things**
- make it **hard to gain anything** from what they take

## The week before you go

### Tell a neighbour

Police call your street your **first line of defence**. Tell a neighbour you trust how long you'll be away and how to reach you.

### Make the house look lived in

Arrange for someone to **bring the bins in, empty the letterbox and water the garden**. Pause paper deliveries. Police suggest a **timer on a light at night and a radio during the day**.

### Tidy the yard

Trim trees and hedges that give someone a place to hide, especially near doors and windows. A mown, tidy yard looks cared for and is easier for neighbours to see into. Police also suggest **motion sensor lights** if you can fit them.

### Hide and mark your valuables

Move anything valuable out of sight of windows. **Mark your property and record serial numbers**, so stolen things are harder to sell and easier to return. Photos of each item help with an insurance claim too.

## The day you leave

1. **Lock every door and window**, including the laundry, the garage and upstairs. Ipswich police say **one third of break-ins** are through unlocked doors and windows.
2. **Keys out of sight,** including spare car keys. In Queensland, police say **one in two cars are stolen using the owner's keys**.
3. **Lights on the timer**, curtains as you'd normally have them.
4. **Cameras on and checked.** Police say CCTV should back up your other security, not replace it, and a system you can watch from your phone lets you act if something happens.

## Inside jobs before you lock up

These aren't police advice, just the jobs that save a nasty surprise when you get home:

- **Washing machine taps off** and the dishwasher empty and dry.
- **Hot water off** if you have a storage tank and you are away for more than a week. Read [should you turn off the hot water on holiday](/guides/home-maintenance/turn-off-hot-water-on-holiday/) first, because the restart needs a wait.
- **Fridge and freezer** cleared of anything that won't last if the power drops in a storm.
- **Unplug** things you won't need, like the TV, computer and the second fridge if it's empty.
- **Gutters cleared** if storms are likely while you're away. Our [storm season checklist](/guides/home-maintenance/storm-season-checklist/) has the rest.
- **Pool gate latched** and the pool chemicals locked away.
- **Pot plants** grouped in the shade with a tray of water, or a neighbour on watering.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I make my house look lived in while I'm away?**
Put a lamp on a timer for the evening and a radio for the day, as police suggest, and leave the curtains as you normally would. Ask someone to bring the bins in, clear the letterbox and water the garden, and pause paper deliveries, so nothing piles up out the front.

**What should I do around the house before going on holiday?**
Outside: trim hedges near doors and windows, mow, and lock the side gates and shed. Inside: lock every door and window, hide keys and valuables, turn the washing machine taps off, clear the fridge and unplug what you won't need. The two lists above cover both, in the order to do them.

**Should I turn off the hot water while I'm away?**
For a storage system and a trip of more than a week, energy.gov.au says it saves money and energy. Allow several hours to heat it back above 60°C before anyone showers when you get home. The [hot water on holiday guide](/guides/home-maintenance/turn-off-hot-water-on-holiday/) has the steps.

**Should I tell people on social media I'm away?**
It's safer to post the photos after you're home.

**Is a camera enough?**
Queensland Police say no: on its own, a camera mostly records the offence. Lock up, light up and get a neighbour involved first.

**What if my home is broken into while I'm away?**
Don't go in if you think someone is still inside. Call 000 in an emergency, or the Police Assistance Line on 131 444 (Policelink in Queensland) to report it.
