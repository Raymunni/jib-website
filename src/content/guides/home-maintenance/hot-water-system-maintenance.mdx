Most people don't think about the hot water system until the morning it fails. Yet a storage tank has one small job that takes a minute every six months, and manufacturers warn that skipping it can lead to the tank failing early. Here's what to check, how often, and which parts need a plumber.

## The schedule at a glance

| Job | How often | DIY? |
|---|---|---|
| Lift the relief valve lever | Every 6 months | Yes |
| Look for leaks, rust and water pooling | Every 6 months | Yes |
| Keep the unit and its vents clear | Every 6 months | Yes |
| Replace the relief valve | About every 5 years | Licensed plumber |
| Inspect or replace the anode | About every 5 years, sooner in harsh water | Licensed plumber |
| Service a gas unit | As your manual says | Licensed gasfitter |

These intervals come from Rheem and Rinnai's Australian manuals. They differ by model: Rinnai's split solar manual suggests a service every three years, for example. Your own manual is the final word, and the model number on the unit's label will find it online.

## The six-monthly relief valve check

The temperature and pressure relief valve lets water out if the tank gets too hot or the pressure climbs too high. It's the small brass valve with a lever, near the top of a storage tank. Mineral deposits can stop it working, which is why it needs moving.

1. **Find the valve and its drain line.** Follow the copper or plastic pipe from the valve to where it ends, so you can see where the water will come out.
2. **Keep clear.** The water is hot enough to burn. Keep your hands, kids and pets away from the end of the drain line.
3. **Gently lift the lever** and hold it for about 10 seconds. Water should flow from the drain line.
4. **Lower the lever gently** so the valve closes and reseats. The flow should stop.
5. **Check again a few minutes later** that it isn't still running.

If no water comes out, or it won't stop running after you've done this, call a licensed plumber. Don't block the drain line or plug the valve, whatever it's doing. It's a safety device.

**A little water is normal.** Rinnai notes that some water from the drain line while the tank heats is expected, because water expands as it heats. A steady or continuous flow can mean the mains pressure is too high or that the valve or thermostat is faulty, so get that checked.

## The five-yearly service

Rheem recommends a major service every five years. It's the part of hot water maintenance that actually adds years to a tank, and it's licensed work:

- **The relief valve gets replaced.** Both Rheem and Rinnai say to replace it at intervals of no more than five years, or sooner where water is scaly.
- **The anode gets inspected or replaced.** This is a metal rod inside the tank that corrodes so the tank doesn't. Once it's used up, the tank itself starts to rust. Rinnai's gas storage guidance is at least every five years, more often in harsh water areas.

Rheem also notes that this work isn't covered by warranty, so expect to pay for it. Ask your plumber to write the date on the unit or send it to you, because nobody remembers what year it was last done.

## What temperature should a hot water system be?

There are two numbers, and they do different jobs:

- **At least 60°C in the tank.** Storage systems must heat water to a minimum of 60°C to stop bacteria such as Legionella growing. Don't turn a storage tank below this to save money.
- **No more than 50°C at bathroom taps.** New systems must have a tempering valve or similar to limit the water at the tap and reduce the risk of scalds. 50°C is plenty for washing, and water at 60°C can burn a child very quickly.

If your hot water suddenly runs far hotter or colder than usual, or you're not sure yours has a tempering valve, ask a plumber to check it. Adjusting or replacing one is licensed work. Here's [how to check your bathroom tap with a thermometer](/guides/home-maintenance/hot-water-temperature-50-degrees/).

**Back from a holiday?** Queensland's Legionnaires' guidance recommends running a system at 60°C or above for a while after a long absence, before using it. Run your taps for a minute or two when you get home as well.

## Checks by system type

**Electric or gas storage tank:** do the valve check above. Look around the base for rust or pooling water.

**Gas continuous flow (instantaneous):** there's no tank and usually no relief valve to lift. Keep the flue and the space around the unit clear of plants and stored items, and have a licensed gasfitter service it as the manual says.

**Heat pump:** keep the fan clear of plants, leaves and stored items so air can move through it, and do the relief valve check if your manual lists one.

**Solar:** check the manual for your model, as service intervals can be shorter. Anything on the roof needs ladder safety, so leave the panels to a technician if you're not confident up there.

## Signs it's time for a plumber

- **Water pooling at the base of the tank.** A leak from the tank body usually can't be repaired, so the tank needs replacing.
- **Rusty or discoloured hot water** when the cold runs clear.
- **Running out of hot water sooner than you used to.**
- **Rumbling or banging** while it heats.
- **A gas unit that won't stay lit**, or any smell of gas. If you smell gas, turn it off at the meter, get outside and call your gas distributor's emergency line.

A storage tank is often said to last around 10 years, but that's a rule of thumb rather than a spec. Water quality, the anode and how well the valve has been kept up all change it. If yours is getting on, it's worth working out what you'd replace it with before it fails, because a tank that dies on a cold morning rarely leaves time to compare. ## Frequently asked questions

**How often should a hot water system be serviced in Australia?**
Rheem recommends a major service every five years for its electric storage units. Other models and solar systems can differ, so check your manual. The six-monthly relief valve check is separate, and you can do that yourself.

**Why is water dripping from my hot water system's overflow pipe?**
A small amount while the tank is heating is normal, because water expands as it heats. A steady or continuous flow can point to high mains pressure or a faulty valve, so call a licensed plumber.

**Can I replace the anode or relief valve myself?**
No. Both are connected to the water supply, so they're licensed plumbing work in Australia. Lifting the lever to test the valve is the part you can do.

**Do I need to drain my hot water system every year?**
Follow your manual. Draining a tank involves shutting off power or gas and handling very hot water, so it's a job to leave to a plumber if the manual calls for it.

**What temperature should I set my hot water system to?**
Keep a storage tank at 60°C or above to control Legionella. The water at bathroom taps should be limited to 50°C by a tempering valve.
