The BBQ comes out for Christmas, the bottle's empty, and the servo won't refill it because it's out of date. LPG bottles don't last forever. Every one carries a test stamp, and checking it takes ten seconds.

## The rule: 10 years from the test date

Queensland's government says the test date stamped on an LPG cylinder is **valid for 10 years**, and that you shouldn't use a cylinder that's out of date. NSW says the same: **to be refilled, a cylinder must have a valid 10-year stamp.** (Very large cylinders, over 100 kg water capacity, can be extended to 15 years in NSW. That's not your BBQ bottle.)

## How to find and read the stamp

1. **Look at the collar,** the metal ring around the valve at the top. Queensland says the stamp is on the **base or neck** of the cylinder.
2. **Find the date.** It's usually stamped as a month and year, or a short code with the year, sometimes alongside the tester's mark.
3. **Add 10 years.** A bottle stamped in 2016 needs testing in 2026.

If you can't find or read a stamp, NSW says the cylinder may not be safe to fill. It will need inspecting and stamping at a certified gas cylinder test station before anyone can fill it.

## Out of date? Your options

- **Swap it.** Exchange outlets take your bottle and hand over a filled, in-date one. Check the one you get: Queensland says to make sure it's in date and has a valve plug or dust cap fitted.
- **Get it tested.** An approved gas cylinder test station can inspect it and restamp it, usually for a fee.
- **Dispose of it.** Queensland and NSW say you can drop old cylinders at approved test stations, some council refuse or recycling centres, and some gas suppliers. Never put one in the household bin.

## Bottle checks before the Christmas BBQ

From the Queensland and NSW guidance:

- **Look for rust, dents and damage.** Don't use a damaged or corroded cylinder.
- **Check the seal.** When you connect it, make sure the rubber seal is intact. Don't connect with a damaged or missing seal.
- **Smell for gas.** Queensland says to check regularly for leaks, which smell like rotten eggs.
- **Store it upright,** away from heat and direct sun, with the valve turned off.
- **Don't repair or remove valves yourself.** Only a trained person at a test station can do that.
- **Turn off the bottle first** when you finish cooking, while the burners are still on, then turn off the BBQ. That empties the gas from the hose.

## Taking it to be swapped or filled

NSW says you can carry **up to two cylinders totalling 9 kg or less** in an enclosed vehicle, upright and secured, ideally in the boot, with a plug in the valve. Don't carry a bottle bigger than 9 kg inside the car.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long is a BBQ gas bottle valid for in Australia?**
10 years from the test date stamped on its collar or base.

**Can I use an out-of-date gas bottle?**
Queensland and NSW both say not to, and retailers won't refill one. Swap it, have it retested, or dispose of it.

**Where do I get rid of an old gas bottle?**
Approved gas cylinder test stations, some council recycling centres and some gas suppliers.

**Can I keep my gas bottle in the car?**
In NSW, up to two cylinders totalling 9 kg or less can travel upright and secured in an enclosed vehicle, preferably the boot, with the valve plugged. Don't leave them in a hot car.
