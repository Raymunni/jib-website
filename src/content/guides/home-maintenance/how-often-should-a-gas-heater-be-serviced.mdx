The heater has sat quiet since September. The first cold night comes, you flick it on, and it seems fine. That's the problem with gas heaters: a faulty one can look and feel perfectly normal while it leaks carbon monoxide into the room. A service every two years is how you find out.

## The rule: at least every two years

Energy Safe Victoria recommends that **all gas heaters are serviced at least every two years by a qualified gasfitter**. That covers wall furnaces, space heaters, central heating units and decorative log fires, whether the heater is new or old.

Service it sooner if:

- the flame has changed from its usual colour
- there's soot or staining around the heater or the flue
- it smells, pops or makes new noises
- it hasn't been serviced for several years. ESV says to consider replacing an old heater that hasn't been looked after.

The best time to book is late summer or autumn, before the cold arrives and before every gasfitter in town is booked out.

## Why it matters: carbon monoxide

Carbon monoxide (CO) is a poisonous gas you can't see, smell or taste. ESV warns that any gas heater can spill it, and that it can make your family very sick or kill them.

Consumer Affairs Victoria lists the symptoms of CO poisoning as:

- tiredness
- shortness of breath
- headaches
- nausea and vomiting
- dizziness

If people feel this way while the heater is on and better when they go outside, turn the heater off, open windows and doors, get everyone out into fresh air, and get medical help. Don't use the heater again until a gasfitter has checked it.

## What a service covers

A licensed gasfitter will inspect the heater and check how it's installed, including **testing for carbon monoxide leakage**. For an open-flued heater, ESV says the service should also include the flue and a check for **negative pressure** in the house.

Negative pressure happens when exhaust fans pull air out of a well-sealed home. The air has to come from somewhere, and it can be drawn down the heater's flue, bringing CO with it. That's why ESV says not to run the rangehood or bathroom fan at the same time as an open-flued heater.

Ask the gasfitter whether your heater is **open-flued** or **room-sealed**. Open-flued heaters use air from inside the room and spill CO more easily. Room-sealed heaters draw their air from outside.

## Between services: what you can do

- **Keep it ventilated.** Check permanent vents aren't blocked, and leave a window open a little if your heater is open-flued.
- **Don't run exhaust fans** while an open-flued heater is on.
- **Don't leave it on overnight** or for long stretches. You won't notice CO while you sleep.
- **Keep the outside end of the flue clear** of plants and nests.
- **Keep a metre of space** around the heater. ACCC Product Safety recommends at least one metre from clothes, bedding, furniture and curtains.
- **Fit a carbon monoxide alarm** as a back-up. ESV says to choose one that meets UL2034 or EN50291, and that it's never a substitute for servicing.
- **Check for safety alerts.** ESV has issued alerts for some older models (including certain Vulcan, Pyrox, Cannon, Regency, Nectre and Real Flame heaters). If you have one, contact the supplier.

Never bring an outdoor gas heater or barbecue inside, and never use the cooktop or oven to heat the room.

## Can I service it myself?

No. ESV is clear that gas work is not a DIY project. Servicing, repairs and adjustments must be done by a licensed gasfitter, and in Victoria that means one licensed for Type A appliance servicing. Ask to see their licence card. Our [gas appliance guide](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-install-a-gas-appliance-myself/) covers what the rules allow in each state.

## Renting? The rules in Victoria

In Victoria, rental providers **must** have a gas safety check done **every two years** by a licensed or registered gasfitter. They must tell a new renter the date of the last check before the lease is signed, and give a renter a copy of the latest check within 7 days of a written request.

A gas leak is an urgent repair. If the rental provider can't be reached, renters can arrange an urgent repair of up to $2,500 and be paid back within 7 days.

Other states handle rentals differently, so check with your state's tenancy authority.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should a gas heater be serviced in Australia?**
At least every two years by a licensed gasfitter, as Energy Safe Victoria recommends for all gas heaters. Sooner if the flame changes colour, you see soot or anyone feels unwell while it's on.

**Do new gas heaters need servicing?**
Yes. ESV says any gas heater, old or new, can spill carbon monoxide.

**What are the signs a gas heater is leaking carbon monoxide?**
Soot or staining, a flame that has changed colour, and people feeling tired, headachy, dizzy or sick while it's running. You can't see or smell CO itself, which is why servicing and a CO alarm matter.

**Is a gas safety check required for rentals?**
In Victoria, yes: every two years, by law. Other states have their own rules.
