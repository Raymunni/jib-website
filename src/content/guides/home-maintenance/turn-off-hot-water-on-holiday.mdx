A storage hot water tank keeps a full load of water hot around the clock, whether anyone's home or not. Two weeks away over Christmas is two weeks of heating water nobody uses. Switching it off is worth it, as long as you know what to do when you get back.

## Is it worth turning off?

The Australian Government's energy advice says **hot water is about 25% of household energy use**, and **if you're away for more than a week, turning off your storage hot water system saves money and energy**.

It depends on the type:

| System | Turn off for a trip? |
| --- | --- |
| Electric storage tank | Worth it for a week or more |
| Gas storage tank | Worth it for a week or more; many have a holiday setting |
| Heat pump | Worth it for a week or more |
| Solar with an electric or gas booster | Turn the booster off |
| Instantaneous (continuous flow) | Little to save: it only heats when a tap runs |

## Why the restart matters

Storage systems are kept hot for a reason. The Australian Building Codes Board says **Legionella bacteria grow in water between 20°C and 45°C**, and growth drops sharply in water **stored at 60°C and above**. A tank that sits cold for two weeks spends its time in that range.

So energy.gov.au says that when you turn it back on, **the water must stay above 60°C for at least 35 minutes before you use it**, and that **it could take several hours** to get there.

Queensland's public housing advice goes further: it tells tenants **not to turn the system off unless they're away for a long time, such as 3 months**, and to let it reach full temperature before using hot water again. If you'd rather not manage the restart, leaving it on is the simple choice.

## How to switch off

- **Electric:** turn off the hot water circuit at the switchboard (usually labelled "HWS" or "hot water"). Some tanks also have an isolator switch beside them.
- **Gas:** use the holiday or vacation setting on the control if it has one, or follow the manual. Don't fiddle with the gas valve or burner beyond what the manual says.
- **Heat pump or solar booster:** use the isolator or switchboard circuit, as the manual describes.

Don't turn down the thermostat instead. The ABCB warns that storing hot water below 60°C to save money can encourage Legionella to grow.

## When you get home

1. **Turn it back on first**, before you unpack.
2. **Wait several hours** without using hot water. Overnight is easiest.
3. **Run the hot taps** for a short time once it's fully hot, to flush water that sat in the pipes. The Queensland guidance also says to run every tap and outlet, inside and out, at least every 2 months.

If the water isn't hot after a day, check the switchboard and the pilot or error light, then call a licensed plumber (or gasfitter for gas).

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I turn off an electric hot water system in Australia?**
At the switchboard in your meter box, flick down the switch labelled hot water or HWS. Some tanks also have an isolator switch on the wall beside them. Leave the cold water valve open.

**How do I turn off a gas hot water system?**
Use the holiday or vacation setting on the control if it has one. If not, follow the steps in the manual or on the label inside the cover, and leave the gas valve and burner alone beyond that.

**How do I turn off a solar hot water system?**
Turn off the electric or gas booster, using its switchboard circuit or isolator. The panels can keep heating the water on sunny days.

**Does turning off the hot water system save money?**
For a storage system, yes, if you're away for more than a week, energy.gov.au says. For a night or a weekend it isn't worth it, and an instantaneous system only heats when a tap is on, so there's little to save.

**What if the hot water system is leaking?**
Turn off its power at the switchboard (or the gas at the unit), close the cold water valve on the pipe into the tank, and call a licensed plumber. Don't touch the relief valve pipe, which can run very hot.

**Does turning the hot water off and on damage it?**
Switching a storage system off for a trip is normal. If it won't restart, that's a job for a licensed plumber or gasfitter.

**Can I just turn the temperature down while I'm away?**
The ABCB says storing water below 60°C can encourage Legionella. Turn it off and follow the 60°C restart, or leave it at its normal setting.

**How long does it take to heat back up?**
energy.gov.au says it could take several hours, and the water needs to stay above 60°C for 35 minutes before use.
