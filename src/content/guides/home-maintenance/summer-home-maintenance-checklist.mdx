An Australian summer is hard on a house: heatwaves push the aircon flat out, storms test the gutters and roof, bushfire season puts every dry leaf to work, and the mozzies arrive with the first humid week. Most of the work is cheap and quick if you do it in spring, before you need it.

## Before summer (October and November)

| Job | Time | DIY? |
|---|---|---|
| Clean the aircon filters | 15 min | Yes |
| Clear around the outdoor aircon unit | 15 min | Yes |
| Switch ceiling fans to summer mode | 5 min | Yes |
| Clear gutters and downpipes | 1 to 2 hrs | Yes, from a ladder |
| Trim branches over the roof | 1 hr | Small ones only |
| Check the pool fence and gate | 10 min | Yes |
| Patch or replace flyscreens | 30 min | Yes |
| Test smoke alarms | 5 min | Yes |
| Book an aircon service | 5 min to book | Pro |

### Aircon: filters inside, airflow outside

**Inside:** clean the filters on your split system. It's a 10-minute job: turn it off, lift the front panel, slide out the mesh filters, vacuum them, wash them in warm soapy water and let them dry in the shade. Here's [the full method](/guides/home-maintenance/how-to-clean-a-split-system-air-conditioner/).

**Outside:** the outdoor unit needs air flowing through it. Clear leaves, weeds and plants from around it and pull out anything caught in the fins. If the fins are dusty, **turn the power off at the outdoor isolator switch first**, then rinse them gently with a hose on a soft spray from the top down. Never use a pressure washer, which bends the thin fins.

Why bother: in government bench tests reported by energy.gov.au, common faults such as a blocked condenser added 14% to 20% to energy use. energy.gov.au also recommends a service by a licensed technician before peak season, which means booking now, before the first heatwave fills their calendar. Anything involving the refrigerant is a job for an ARC-licensed technician.

### Ceiling fans to summer mode

Most fans should spin **anticlockwise** in summer, pushing air down onto you. Stand under the fan on high: if you don't feel a strong breeze, it's on winter mode. Turn it off, wait until it stops, and flick the switch on the motor housing or press reverse on the remote. Full details: [which way should a ceiling fan spin in summer?](/guides/how-to/ceiling-fan-direction-summer/)

### Gutters, branches and the roof

Summer storms and bushfire season both start with your gutters. Get Ready Queensland says to clear gutters and downpipes so water doesn't pool in the eaves or roof cavity, and the NSW RFS says leaves in gutters are a major ember risk in bushfire season. Trim branches that hang over the roof. Anything near power lines is a job for your electricity distributor. Our [storm season checklist](/guides/home-maintenance/storm-season-checklist/) has the full list.

### Pool fence and gate

Before the first swim, walk the fence. Move anything climbable (pot plants, chairs, the BBQ) away from it, check the gate swings shut and latches from every position, and make sure no gap under the fence has grown past 100mm. In Queensland, the QBCC says climbable objects must be at least 900mm from the outside of the fence. Here's [the 10-minute pool fence check](/guides/home-maintenance/pool-fence-rules-qld/).

### Flyscreens

Check every flyscreen for tears and gaps around the frame before mozzie season. Small holes can be patched with a flyscreen repair patch. Re-meshing a whole screen with a spline roller is a satisfying DIY job, and so is replacing a worn door closer.

### Smoke alarms

Summer is when you're most likely to be cooking outside, running fans and lighting candles for the mozzies. Test every alarm with its test button. If you're in Queensland, check you're on track for interconnected photoelectric alarms by 2027: see [Queensland's smoke alarm laws](/guides/home-maintenance/queensland-smoke-alarm-laws-2027/).

## Through summer

- **Tip out standing water weekly.** Mosquitoes breed in water in pot plant saucers, buckets, toys, blocked gutters and old tyres. Empty, cover or throw them out.
- **Clean the aircon filters every few weeks** while it's running hard, more often in humid weather.
- **Water early in the morning,** so less evaporates and leaves dry before evening.
- **Keep fans and aircon working together.** energy.gov.au says each degree of extra cooling adds 5% to 10% to your aircon's energy use, and a fan can make it feel about 3°C cooler for around 2 cents an hour.
- **Close blinds on the sunny side** in the morning before the heat builds.
- **Check the BBQ gas hose** for cracks before the first cook-up. A gas leak smells like rotten eggs: turn the bottle off and don't light it.

## Before you go away

- **Set up a watering timer** on the garden tap, or ask a neighbour to water.
- **Clear the gutters** if you'll be away in bushfire or storm season.
- **Unplug what you don't need** to protect it from storm surges.
- **Check the fire danger rating** for where you're going, and for home if you're in a bushfire-prone area.

## DIY or a pro?

- **DIY:** filters, clearing around the outdoor unit, rinsing fins with the power off, fans, gutters from a ladder, flyscreens, pool fence checks, smoke alarm tests, mozzie control.
- **Pro:** aircon servicing and anything involving refrigerant (ARC-licensed technician), electrical faults or anything wired in (licensed electrician), gas appliances and leaks (licensed gasfitter), roof work (roofer), trees near power lines (your distributor).

## Frequently asked questions

**What should I do to my house before summer in Australia?**
Clean the aircon filters, clear around the outdoor unit, switch fans to summer mode, clear gutters, trim branches, check the pool fence, fix flyscreens and test smoke alarms.

**When should I service my air conditioner?**
Before peak season, so late spring, according to energy.gov.au. Clean the filters yourself every few weeks while it's running a lot.

**Can I hose my aircon outdoor unit?**
Yes, gently. Turn it off at the isolator switch first, use a soft spray from the top down, and never use a pressure washer.

**How do I stop mosquitoes breeding around my house?**
Tip out standing water every week: pot plant saucers, buckets, toys, birdbaths and blocked gutters are the usual spots.
