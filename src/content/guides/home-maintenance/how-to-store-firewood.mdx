A trailer load of firewood gets tipped down the side of the house every autumn in thousands of Australian backyards. It's handy there. It's also exactly where termites would put it, if they could choose. Where and how you stack it decides whether it burns well and whether it brings termites to your walls.

## Why a woodpile near the house is a problem

Termites eat timber and like damp. A woodpile on the ground gives them both, and a stack against a wall gives them a hidden way into the house. Victoria's building regulator says termite damage costs Australian homes more than fire, floods and storms combined, and that it may not be covered by insurance.

Its termite tips include:

- **Don't stack materials against walls.** It lets termites get in without being seen.
- **Don't store anything made of wood or paper under the house.**
- **Keep subfloor vents clear.**

The aim, the regulator says, is to keep timber away from soil contact and to make termites build their mud tubes out in the open, where you can see them.

## Where to stack it

- **Away from the house.** Not against a wall, under the eaves, beside a subfloor vent or under the house.
- **Away from timber structures,** like sheds, decks, fences and retaining walls, for the same reason.
- **Off the ground,** on a steel rack, bricks or pavers, so the bottom layer isn't sitting on soil.
- **Under cover,** with the sides open so air moves through. The NSW EPA says to store firewood under cover in a dry, ventilated area.
- **Not in the garage.** Wood stored inside brings anything living in it inside too.

Keep a small day's supply near the back door in a metal tub or log holder, and refill it from the main stack.

## Dry wood burns cleaner

The NSW EPA says green or unseasoned wood can be **up to 50% water**, which makes the fire smoke. Freshly cut wood needs to be stored for **8 to 12 months** before you burn it.

To tell if a piece is dry, the EPA suggests:

- **Bang two pieces together.** Dry wood makes a loud, hollow crack.
- **Tap it with a key or coin.** Dry wood sounds sharp. Wet wood sounds dull.

If you're buying wood to burn straight away, ask the seller to confirm it's aged. Wet wood isn't just smoky. The CFA says burning green wood or treated timber makes creosote build up faster in the chimney, and creosote is highly flammable. Our [chimney and flue guide](/guides/home-maintenance/how-often-to-clean-a-chimney-or-flue/) covers that side.

**Never burn** treated, painted or old building timber, driftwood or rubbish. The EPA says they pollute the air and can give off poisonous gases.

## Use it up, oldest first

Stack new wood behind or beside the old, and burn the oldest first. A pile that sits untouched for years is the one most likely to have something living in it. Only buy what you'll burn in a season or two.

When you take the last of the stack, look at the ground and the rack underneath. If you see **mud tubes**, or timber that breaks into thin layers, don't disturb it. Call a licensed pest controller. Victoria's regulator suggests looking at trees, woodpiles and garden beds up to 50 m around the house for signs of a colony.

## The other autumn jobs that go with it

- **Book the termite inspection.** QBCC says to have a licensed pest controller inspect and report every year, or more often in high-risk areas. Our [termite inspection guide](/guides/home-maintenance/termite-inspection-how-often/) has the details.
- **Get the chimney or flue cleaned** before the first fire. The CFA says to do it every year.
- **Clear leaves from the gutters** so autumn rain runs away from the house, not into the walls.

## Frequently asked questions

**How far from the house should firewood be stored?**
Not against the house or under it. Victoria's building regulator says not to stack materials against walls, because termites can get in unseen. Keep it well away, off the ground and under cover.

**Does firewood attract termites?**
Wood sitting on the ground, especially damp wood, is food and shelter for termites. Stored against a house, it can give them a hidden way in.

**How long does firewood need to dry?**
The NSW EPA says freshly cut wood needs 8 to 12 months, stored under cover in a dry, ventilated area.

**Can I store firewood in the garage?**
It's best not to. Anything living in the wood comes inside with it.
