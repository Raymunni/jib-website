Twenty people, three generations, one backyard and a pool. Hosting Christmas is mostly about food, but a handful of jobs around the house decide whether the day runs smoothly. Here they are in the order to do them.

## Two weeks out: the safety checks

### Test the smoke alarms

Press the test button on every alarm. ACCC Product Safety says to **test smoke alarms every month**, replace the battery every year (unless it's a 10-year sealed alarm), and **replace the alarm itself every 10 years**. The date of manufacture is printed on the alarm. With a house full of guests and candles, this one matters. If one keeps chirping, our [smoke alarm beeping guide](/guides/how-to/why-does-my-smoke-alarm-keep-beeping/) has the fixes.

### Check the pool gate and fence

In NSW, the law requires a child-resistant barrier around a pool and a **CPR sign** near it that can be read from 3 m away. Everywhere, the gate should **close and latch by itself** from any position. Walk the fence for anything kids could climb: chairs, eskies, pot plants and the new trampoline. See our guides for [NSW](/guides/home-maintenance/pool-fence-rules-nsw/) and [Queensland](/guides/home-maintenance/pool-fence-rules-qld/), and the [CPR sign rules](/guides/home-maintenance/pool-cpr-sign-requirements/).

### Check the gas bottle and clean the BBQ

Find the test stamp on the bottle's collar. Queensland says it's **valid for 10 years**, and an out-of-date bottle won't be refilled. Check for rust, a damaged seal or a rotten egg smell. Then give the grill and the fat tray a proper clean. Our [gas bottle guide](/guides/home-maintenance/gas-bottle-expiry-date/) shows how to read the stamp.

### Check the Christmas lights before they go up

Queensland's Electrical Safety Office says to check old lights for damage, use only outdoor-rated lights outside, and test your safety switch first. Our [Christmas lights guide](/guides/how-to/how-to-put-up-christmas-lights-safely/) covers getting them up safely.

## The week before: the house

- **Button battery sweep.** If young children are coming, gather remotes, light-up toys, musical cards and scales and check every battery compartment is secure. Put spare and used batteries up high. See our [button battery guide](/guides/home-maintenance/button-batteries-christmas-safety/).
- **Spare room.** Make the bed, clear a drawer, check the bedside lamp works and the window opens.
- **Bathroom.** Fix the dripping tap or running toilet now, not on Boxing Day. Replace the shower silicone if it's gone black. Stock up on toilet paper.
- **Paths and driveway.** Clear trip hazards, trim plants over paths, and make sure there's a light by the front door for evening arrivals.
- **Outdoor area.** Clean the outdoor furniture, put up shade, and check the deck for loose boards or nails.
- **Fridge space.** Clean out the fridge and, if you have one, the second fridge in the garage.

## The day before and on the day

- **Pool gate shut,** and an adult whose only job is watching the kids in or near the water. Swap the job every half hour.
- **Candles** away from curtains and the tree, and out before everyone leaves the room.
- **Lights off** before bed and when the house is empty, as the ESO advises.
- **Gas off at the bottle** when the BBQ is done.
- **First aid kit** where adults can find it.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should I do before hosting Christmas?**
Test the smoke alarms, check the pool gate latches by itself, check the gas bottle date and clean the BBQ, check your Christmas lights, and do a button battery sweep if small children are coming.

**How often should smoke alarms be tested?**
Every month, according to ACCC Product Safety, with the battery replaced every year (unless it's a 10-year sealed alarm) and the alarm replaced every 10 years.

**How far ahead should I start?**
Two weeks gives you time to book a plumber, electrician or pool inspector if a check turns something up.
