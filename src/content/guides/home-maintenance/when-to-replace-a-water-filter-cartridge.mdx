A water filter doesn't tell you when it's finished. The water keeps coming, it just stops being filtered as well, and an overdue cartridge is easy to forget because it lives in a dark cupboard. Here's how long each type usually lasts, and how to work out yours.

**Want a date for your own household?** Use our free [water filter replacement calculator](/tools/water-filter-replacement-calculator/). It takes your filter's rating and how many people drink from it, and gives you the month to change it.

## How long each type of cartridge lasts

| Filter type | Typical life | What sets the limit |
|---|---|---|
| Fridge filter | About 6 months | Time, usually with an indicator light |
| Sediment cartridge | About 3 to 6 months | How dirty the incoming water is |
| Carbon block cartridge | About 6 to 12 months | Litres used and time |
| Reverse osmosis membrane | About 2 to 3 years | Water quality and the filters before it |
| Benchtop or jug filter | Check the pack | Litres used |

Those are general ranges from Australian manufacturers and retailers, and they vary a lot by product. For example, Puretec's QT12 guide says to change the cartridge at least every 11,355 litres or 6 months, whichever comes first. Other cartridges are rated for tens of thousands of litres. Samsung and Fisher & Paykel both work on about six months for their fridge filters. Go by the number for your model.

## Litres or months: which one counts?

Whichever comes first. Most cartridges are rated both ways, and the two limits do different jobs:

- **Litres** cover how much the filter has already caught. A big family can reach this long before the time limit.
- **Months** cover what happens in the cartridge over time, even when it isn't used much. A filter in a holiday house, or for one person who drinks little, still has a time limit.

To work out litres, a household that uses the filtered tap for drinking and cooking might use around 3 to 5 litres per person a day. That's our rule of thumb, not a standard, so count your own bottles and kettles if you want it closer. The maths is simple: **rated litres ÷ litres a day = days until it's due.** Then compare that with the time limit.

A family of four using 16 litres a day on a 11,355 litre cartridge would take about 710 days to hit the litres, but the 6-month limit comes first. That's why time is the limit for most homes, and litres matter most for big households and high-capacity use.

## Signs a cartridge needs changing early

- **The flow has dropped.** A noticeable fall in pressure at the filtered tap is the most common sign the cartridge is clogged.
- **The taste or smell has changed.** Chlorine taste coming back usually means the carbon is used up.
- **The water looks cloudy or has specks.** Run it for a while, and if it doesn't clear, change the cartridge.
- **The indicator light is on.** Fridge indicators generally run on a timer of about six months.

Bore water, tank water and supplies with a lot of sediment can use a cartridge up sooner than the rating suggests, especially the first-stage sediment filter. If you're on town water and suddenly get dirty water after mains work, expect the cartridge to clog early too.

**Rainwater and bore water:** a cartridge that's rated to improve taste isn't necessarily rated to make untreated water safe to drink. Check what your system is certified to do before relying on it.

## How to change an under-sink cartridge

Steps differ by model, so follow your manual. The usual routine is:

1. **Check you have the right cartridge.** Match the part number on the old one, not just the shape.
2. **Turn off the filter's isolation valve** and open the filtered tap to release the pressure.
3. **Put a towel and a small bucket under it.** There will be some water.
4. **Twist out the old cartridge** and fit the new one as the manual shows.
5. **Turn the water back on slowly** and check for leaks.
6. **Flush it.** Run the tap for the time your manual says, as new carbon can release fine black dust. It's normal, and it clears.
7. **Write the date on the cartridge** with a marker, or set a reminder.

If you're on a fridge filter, buy approved filters for your model. Samsung warns that unapproved filters can leak and damage the fridge.

## What's DIY and what isn't

Swapping a cartridge in an existing system is normally DIY, because you're only replacing a consumable part. **Installing a new under-sink filter is different.** Connecting anything new to the drinking water supply is plumbing work in Australia, and a filter installed by a licensed plumber also protects you if it later leaks into your cabinets. See [Can I replace a tap myself?](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-replace-a-tap-myself/) for how the rules work. Fridge filters that twist in, and benchtop and jug filters, are all fine to do yourself.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should I change my under-sink water filter?**
Most last about 6 to 12 months, or their rated litres, whichever comes first. Check your cartridge's rating, and use our [calculator](/tools/water-filter-replacement-calculator/) if you'd like a date for your household.

**What happens if I don't change my water filter?**
The flow usually falls and the taste or smell of the water can return, because the filter is clogged or can't remove as much. Old cartridges are also not doing the job you bought them for.

**How often should a fridge water filter be replaced?**
About every six months for the Samsung and Fisher & Paykel models we checked, and the fridge usually has an indicator. Check your manual for the exact part number.

**Can I clean and reuse a water filter cartridge?**
Most household cartridges aren't designed for it. Follow the manufacturer's instructions, and replace it unless they specifically say it can be cleaned.

**Do I need a plumber to change a water filter cartridge?**
Normally not, if you're swapping a cartridge in a system that's already installed. Installing a new system connected to the water supply is plumbing work.
