The CPR sign is the cheapest part of pool safety, and the one people forget until an inspector, a buyer's conveyancer or a sun-faded fence reminds them. If you own a pool in NSW or Queensland, here's what the law asks for.

## NSW

The NSW Government says the law requires a **CPR sign displayed near your pool**, and this includes **portable pools**. The sign must be:

- in **good condition**
- **easily readable from 3 m away**

**New CPR signs came into effect on 1 September 2019**, and all new pools must use the updated signage. Owners of existing pools **don't have to update** their sign unless the pool barrier is substantially altered or rebuilt. A faded, cracked or unreadable sign still needs replacing, because it must be in good condition.

In NSW, portable pools that can hold **more than 30 cm** of water have the same fencing and safety requirements as permanent pools. Our [NSW pool fence guide](/guides/home-maintenance/pool-fence-rules-nsw/) covers the barrier and the pool register.

## Queensland

The Queensland Government says if you have a pool, **you must display a CPR sign prominently**. When you replace one, make sure it is:

- **attached to the pool's safety barrier**, or displayed near the pool so someone at the pool can see it easily
- **at least 300 mm x 300 mm**
- made of **durable, weatherproof** material
- **clear and legible**

It must show **how to perform CPR** in line with **ANZCOR Guideline 8** (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation), and include a prominent statement on **how to act in an emergency**, such as calling Triple Zero (000), staying with the person and giving first aid. The QBCC notes the technique starts with **30 chest compressions followed by 2 breaths**.

Building a new pool in Queensland? You also need a **warning sign** during construction: within 1.5 m of the road frontage, visible from the road, with the bottom at least 300 mm above the ground and bold text at least 50 mm high.

Our [Queensland pool fence guide](/guides/home-maintenance/pool-fence-rules-qld/) covers the rest of the pool safety standard.

## Other states

Rules differ. Check with your local council or your state's building regulator before you assume you don't need one. A clear CPR sign at the pool is worth having either way: in an emergency, nobody remembers the steps perfectly.

## Before summer: the five-minute pool check

1. **The sign:** readable from 3 m, not faded or cracked, and fixed where people at the pool can see it.
2. **The gate:** closes and latches by itself from any position.
3. **The fence:** no gaps, loose panels or damage.
4. **The fence line:** nothing kids could climb, like chairs, pot plants, eskies or the BBQ.
5. **The rules:** an adult always watching children in and around the water.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a CPR sign for my pool in NSW?**
Yes. NSW law requires one near the pool, including portable pools, in good condition and readable from 3 m away.

**What size does a pool CPR sign have to be in Queensland?**
At least 300 mm by 300 mm, durable, weatherproof, clear and legible.

**Do I have to replace my old CPR sign?**
In NSW, existing pools only need the updated sign if the barrier is substantially altered or rebuilt, but any sign must stay in good condition and readable. In Queensland, a replacement sign must meet the current requirements.

**Where should a pool CPR sign go?**
In Queensland, on the pool barrier or near the pool where it's easy to see. In NSW, near the pool and readable from 3 m.
