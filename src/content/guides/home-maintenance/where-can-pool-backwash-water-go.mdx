Backwashing the filter sends a rush of dirty, chlorinated water out of the waste line. Where that water is allowed to go isn't the same in every city, and getting it wrong can mean a fine or a flooded neighbour.

## Sewer or stormwater: the difference

**Stormwater** drains (the grates in your yard, the driveway and the street gutter) usually flow **untreated** into creeks, rivers and the sea. The **sewer** (wastewater) goes to a treatment plant. Chlorine, salt and the gunk from a filter aren't things you want in a creek, which is why most rules push pool water toward the sewer or the lawn.

## Sydney (Sydney Water)

Sydney Water says:

- **Do not connect pool or spa drainage to the stormwater system.**
- **Chlorinated pool and spa water must discharge to the wastewater system** so it can be treated.
- It approves backwash and overflow to the wastewater system if the discharge goes **through the pool backwash pump system**, the flow **doesn't exceed 2 litres per second**, and the connection is **through an overflow gully** installed to AS/NZS 3500.
- **No direct gravity overflow** from a pool or spa into the wastewater system.

A new pool should have a compliant connection for backwashing. If the backwash line on yours ends at a stormwater grate, talk to a licensed plumber. Our [overflow relief gully guide](/guides/how-to/overflow-relief-gully-and-blocked-sewers/) explains the gully.

## Brisbane and Ipswich (Urban Utilities)

Urban Utilities says:

- You can send **filter backwash to the sewer** only with **written approval** before you start. Applying is **free**.
- If approved, the discharge must go **through your property's private plumbing**, not a direct connection, at **no more than 1 litre a second**.
- **Pool overflows** from rain or overfilling **must never go to the sewer**.
- Draining a whole pool should be kept to major maintenance. Reuse the water where practical, or spread it over a **grassed area** or an infiltration trench on your property, without it running onto neighbours. If that isn't possible, a **controlled discharge to stormwater** with a garden hose may be an option, but **your council** decides on stormwater.
- For **saltwater pools**, emptying **more than 5%** of the volume to the sewer needs written approval.

Ipswich City Council also says backwash must go to a **lawful discharge point**, not onto the ground where it causes a nuisance.

## South Australia (EPA)

The SA EPA lists **pool backwash water and pool chemicals** among the pollutants that **must not be placed where they can enter the stormwater system**. Breaking the rules can mean a **$300 fine**, an Environment Protection Order or prosecution. Check with your plumber or SA Water about connecting to the sewer.

## Everywhere else

Rules vary by water utility and council. Before you backwash onto the lawn or drain a pool, ask your **water utility** about the sewer and your **council** about stormwater.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should I backwash a pool?**
When the pressure gauge on the filter reads well above its clean reading, or after a big storm or a busy weekend in the pool. The filter's manual gives the number for your model. Each time, the water still has to go where your water utility allows.

**Can I backwash onto my lawn?**
In Brisbane, Urban Utilities suggests the lawn or an infiltration trench for draining pool water if it stays on your property. In Sydney, chlorinated pool water must go to the sewer. Check your local rules.

**Can I drain my pool into the street gutter?**
In Sydney, no: pool drainage can't go to stormwater. In SA, backwash water and pool chemicals are banned from stormwater. In Brisbane, it's a last resort and up to your council.

**Do I need a plumber?**
For a new connection to the sewer, yes. Connecting to the wastewater system is licensed plumbing work.
