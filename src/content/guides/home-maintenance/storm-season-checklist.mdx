Most storm damage to Queensland homes doesn't come from the storm being too big. It comes from a gutter full of leaves that sends water into the ceiling, a branch that was always going to land on the roof, or a trampoline that ends up three houses down. Every one of those is a job you can do on a dry weekend in spring.

Here's the full list, in the order that matters, with what's DIY and what isn't.

## The checklist

| Job | DIY? | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Clear gutters and downpipes | Yes, from a ladder | Blocked gutters overflow into the eaves and roof cavity |
| Trim branches over the roof | Small ones yes. Near power lines, no | Falling branches and leaf build-up |
| Check the roof from the ground | Yes, look only | Cracked tiles, lifted sheets and loose ridge capping let water in |
| Tie down or pack away loose items | Yes | Trampolines, furniture and bins become missiles |
| Check door and window seals | Yes | Wind-driven rain gets through broken seals |
| Find your shut-offs | Yes | You need them fast if a pipe bursts or a ceiling floods |
| Sandbags ready | Yes | Stops sewage backflow through floor drains in a flood |
| Emergency kit and plan | Yes | Power and phones can be out for days |
| Insurance check | Yes | Know what's covered before you need to claim |

## 1. Clear the gutters and downpipes

Get Ready Queensland's first home maintenance tip is to clear gutters and downpipes so water doesn't pool in your eaves or roof cavity. When a gutter overflows in a downpour, the water doesn't just spill over the front. It can run back over the fascia into the ceiling space, which is how a storm turns into a stained, sagging ceiling.

Do it from a ladder on flat ground, with someone holding it or the ladder secured, and never lean out. Our [gutter cleaning guide](/guides/home-maintenance/how-to-clean-gutters/) has the full method. If you'd have to walk on the roof, pay a professional.

## 2. Trim branches hanging over the roof

Cut back branches that overhang the roof or could reach it in a big wind. Small branches you can reach safely from the ground with a pole saw are DIY. Anything big, high or that needs climbing is a job for an arborist.

**If a tree is anywhere near power lines, don't touch it.** Get Ready Queensland says to notify your electricity distributor (Energex or Ergon Energy).

## 3. Check the roof (from the ground)

You don't need to climb up. Stand back on the lawn or across the street, and use your phone's zoom:

- **Cracked or slipped tiles**, or metal sheets with lifted edges or missing screws.
- **Ridge capping on a tile roof.** These are the curved tiles along the peaks, held on with mortar or flexible pointing. If you can see cracks, gaps or crumbling bits, wind-driven rain can get under them. This is the one most people have never looked at.
- **Flashing** around chimneys, skylights and where the roof meets a wall.

Repairs are a job for a roofer. Your job is noticing before the storm finds it.

## 4. Tie down or pack away anything that can fly

Walk the yard and ask: "would this move in a 100 km/h gust?"

- **Trampolines:** strap the frame to ground anchors with a tie-down kit, or turn it upside down if a storm is forecast.
- **Outdoor furniture, umbrellas, kids' toys and pot plants:** bring them in or put them in the shed.
- **Wheelie bins:** put them in a sheltered spot, not out on the kerb.
- **Shade sails:** take them down if a severe storm is coming. They catch wind like a sail on a boat and can pull their posts or fixings out.

## 5. Check door and window seals

Get Ready Queensland recommends checking doors and windows for broken seals, and fixing loose or broken fittings, corrosion and termite damage. Run your hand along the seals and look for daylight around closed doors. Weather strips are a cheap DIY fix from the hardware store.

## 6. Find your shut-offs before you need them

If a pipe bursts or water comes through the ceiling near a light fitting, you don't want to be searching with a torch. Find them now, and show everyone in the house:

- **Water:** the main stop valve, usually at the water meter near the front boundary.
- **Power:** the main switch at your switchboard. If water is getting near anything electrical, turn the power off at the main switch only if you can do it safely without standing in water.
- **Gas:** the valve at the gas meter or bottle.

## 7. Keep sandbags ready

Get Ready Queensland suggests keeping hessian bags and sand on hand to block internal drains and prevent sewage backflow during flooding. A sandbag on the shower and laundry floor drains stops water coming up from the sewer when the system is overwhelmed.

## 8. Pack a kit and make a plan

The Queensland SES suggests making an emergency plan and checking with your council whether storms, cyclones, floods or storm surge are likely in your area. A basic kit:

- Torch and spare batteries, and a battery or wind-up radio
- Power banks for phones
- First aid kit and any medications
- Important documents in a waterproof bag
- Water and food that doesn't need cooking for a few days
- Pet food and supplies

For storm or flood help that isn't life-threatening, call the **SES on 132 500**. In an emergency, call **000**.

## 9. Check your insurance

Queensland is the most disaster-affected state in Australia, and Get Ready Queensland recommends checking your home and contents cover. While you're reading the policy, look for what it says about **maintenance**. Many policies don't cover damage caused by wear and tear or lack of maintenance, and a blocked gutter is a classic example. Check your own policy's wording.

## DIY or a pro?

- **DIY:** gutters from a ladder on a single-storey home, small branches from the ground, checking the roof from the ground, tying things down, seals, finding shut-offs, the kit.
- **Pro:** anything on the roof, roof and ridge capping repairs (roofer), big trees and anything near power lines (arborist, or your electricity distributor), anything electrical that got wet (licensed electrician), and plumbing repairs (licensed plumber).

## Frequently asked questions

**When is storm season in Queensland?**
Severe storms can happen any time, but the main season runs roughly from October to March. Spring is the time to get the jobs done.

**What should I do with a trampoline in a storm?**
Strap it to ground anchors with a tie-down kit, or turn it upside down and weigh it down if a severe storm is forecast. Take the net off if you can.

**Will insurance pay for storm damage from blocked gutters?**
It depends on your policy. Many policies exclude damage caused by lack of maintenance, so keep the gutters clear and keep photos of the work.

**Who do I call if a tree falls on my house?**
For non-life-threatening help, call the SES on 132 500. If anyone is hurt or there are fallen power lines, call 000 and stay well away from the lines.
