The backyard cricket pitch is turning brown, and Christmas is three weeks away. Before you leave the sprinkler on all afternoon, check the rules. Most Australian cities have permanent watering rules that apply even when there are no water restrictions.

## The rules at a glance

| Where | Sprinklers and watering systems | Hand watering |
|---|---|---|
| Sydney, Blue Mountains, Illawarra | Before 10 am or after 4 pm. Drip and smart systems any time | Before 10 am or after 4 pm, hose with a trigger nozzle |
| Victoria (including Melbourne) | Between 6 pm and 10 am only | Any time, with a trigger nozzle hose, watering can or bucket |
| Perth and much of WA | Once, before 9 am or after 6 pm, on your rostered days only. Off 1 June to 31 August | Check Water Corporation's rules for your area |

These are the permanent rules. If your area moves into water restrictions during a drought, tighter rules apply. Brisbane, Adelaide, Hobart, Canberra and Darwin have their own rules: check your water utility's website.

## Sydney: Water Wise Guidelines

Sydney Water's permanent Water Wise Guidelines apply to drinking water in Greater Sydney, the Blue Mountains and the Illawarra. You **can**:

- water gardens and lawns **before 10 am or after 4 pm** with a hose with a trigger nozzle, a sprinkler or a watering system
- use **drip irrigation or smart watering systems at any time**
- fill a new or existing pool

You **can't**:

- **hose hard surfaces** like paths and driveways, except for safety, health or emergency reasons
- **leave a hose or tap running unattended**

New lawns and gardens have some extra flexibility under the guidelines. The rules don't apply to rainwater from a tank, greywater, recycled water from purple pipes, or bore water (which has its own government rules).

## Victoria: permanent water saving rules

Victoria's rules apply statewide:

- **Every hose must have a trigger nozzle** and be leak free.
- **Watering systems** (sprinklers, irrigation) can only run **between 6 pm and 10 am**.
- You can **hand water any time** with a trigger nozzle hose, a watering can or a bucket.
- Don't use water to **clean hard surfaces** like driveways, except in limited cases.

## Perth and WA: watering days

Water Corporation sets **rostered watering days** by address. On your days, water **once, before 9 am or after 6 pm**. Enter your street number and postcode on Water Corporation's website to find your days.

From **1 June to 31 August** the **Sprinkler Switch-off** applies in Perth, the South West and parts of the Great Southern. That won't affect your summer, but it's worth a reminder for autumn.

## How to water so the lawn actually survives

- **Water early in the morning.** It's cooler and less is lost to evaporation, and all three sets of rules allow it.
- **Water deeply, less often,** so roots grow down rather than staying near the surface.
- **Raise the mower.** Longer grass shades its own roots in summer.
- **Use a tank.** Rainwater isn't covered by Sydney Water's guidelines. Our [rainwater tank guide](/guides/council-approval/rainwater-tank-without-council-approval/) covers when a tank needs approval.
- **Check your sprinkler run times** to avoid overwatering. Water Corporation publishes run times for different sprinkler types.

## Frequently asked questions

**What time can I water my lawn in Sydney?**
Before 10 am or after 4 pm, with a trigger hose, sprinkler or watering system. Drip and smart systems can run any time.

**Can I water my garden during the day in Melbourne?**
Yes, by hand with a trigger nozzle hose, watering can or bucket. Sprinklers and other watering systems can only run between 6 pm and 10 am.

**How do I find my watering days in Perth?**
Enter your street number and postcode on Water Corporation's "Check your watering days" page.

**Do watering rules apply to rainwater tanks?**
Sydney Water's guidelines don't apply to rainwater, as long as the tank doesn't hold drinking water from the mains. Check your own utility's rules elsewhere.
