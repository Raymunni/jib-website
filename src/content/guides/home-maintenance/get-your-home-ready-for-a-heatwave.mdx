Get Ready Queensland says **heatwaves have taken more lives than any other natural hazard in Australia**. The Bureau of Meteorology defines one as **three or more days** of high maximum and minimum temperatures that are unusual for the area. With a few days' warning, you can get the house ready.

## A few days out: check the gear

Get Ready Queensland says to **check that fridges, freezers, fans and air conditioners work well** before the heat arrives.

- **Air con:** run it on cool for 15 minutes and check it blows cold. Clean the filters. A blocked filter makes it work harder when you need it most. Our [split system cleaning guide](/guides/home-maintenance/how-to-clean-a-split-system-air-conditioner/) shows how, and if it's running but not cooling, see our [not cooling guide](/guides/how-to/split-system-not-cooling/).
- **Ceiling fans:** check they spin and switch them to summer direction. See our [fan direction guide](/guides/how-to/ceiling-fan-direction-summer/).
- **Fridge and freezer:** check the seals close properly and nothing's iced up.
- **Pedestal fans:** dust them and test them.

## Set up the house

- **Make ice and store cool packs** in the freezer.
- **Fill spray bottles** with cool water.
- **Stock up on food and drinking water,** so you don't need to go out in the worst of it.
- **Block the sun:** close blinds or curtains on windows that get direct sun. Outside shading works even better. Our [keep cool without air con guide](/guides/how-to/how-to-keep-your-house-cool-without-air-con/) covers it.
- **Pick a cool room,** usually the one with air con, or the shadiest room on the lowest floor, and plan to spend the hottest hours there.
- **Plan cross breezes** for when it cools down in the evening.

## The people and pets

Get Ready Queensland says some people need **special care** in a heatwave:

- babies and young children
- older people
- pregnant and breastfeeding people
- people with medical conditions, such as asthma, or on certain medications
- people who work outdoors

If that's someone in your house, it suggests asking their doctor how the heat affects their condition and medication, and how much they should drink. Keep phone numbers for family, carers and local services handy.

Pets need shade and water too. Bring them inside if you can.

## During the heatwave

- Run the air con at a sensible setting rather than as cold as it goes. Our [air con temperature guide](/guides/how-to/best-temperature-for-air-conditioner-in-summer/) explains why.
- Keep blinds and windows shut on the sunny side during the day.
- Check on older neighbours and family.

## Frequently asked questions

**What counts as a heatwave?**
The Bureau of Meteorology defines it as three or more days of high maximum and minimum temperatures that are unusual for that location.

**When should I clean the air con filters?**
Before the hot weather starts, and before a forecast heatwave if you haven't already.

**What's the best room to shelter in?**
The room with air con, or the shadiest, lowest room in the house with the blinds down.

**How do I prepare my house for a heatwave?**
Check the air con, fans, fridge and freezer work, clean the air con filters, make ice and store cool packs, stock up on food and drinking water, block the sun with blinds or curtains, and pick a cool room to spend the hottest hours in.

**Who is most at risk in a heatwave?**
Get Ready Queensland lists babies and young children, older people, pregnant and breastfeeding people, people with medical conditions such as asthma or on certain medications, and people who work outdoors.

**What should I stock up on before a heatwave?**
Food and drinking water, so you don't need to go out in the worst of the heat, plus ice and cool packs in the freezer and spray bottles of cool water.

**How do I keep pets safe in a heatwave?**
Give them shade and plenty of water, and bring them inside if you can. Walk dogs early or late, when the ground is cooler.
