Most expensive home repairs start as a five-minute job nobody did: a gutter that overflowed into the eaves, a smoke alarm with a dead battery, a relief valve that seized, termites nobody looked for. Here's the full list of what to check around an Australian home, how often, and whether it's a DIY job or one for a licensed pro. Every interval comes from the relevant regulator or manufacturer where one exists.

<MaintenancePlanner />

## The five that matter most

If you only do five things, do these. They're the ones that protect your family or stop a small problem becoming a five-figure one.

1. **Test smoke alarms monthly, replace them every 10 years.** Fire and Rescue NSW recommends a monthly test with the test button, vacuuming every six months, and replacing every alarm at least every 10 years. *In Queensland, every home must have interconnected photoelectric smoke alarms by 1 January 2027.* See [Queensland's smoke alarm laws](/guides/home-maintenance/queensland-smoke-alarm-laws-2027/).
2. **Test safety switches every 3 months.** Press the "T" button on each RCD in your switchboard. If it doesn't trip, call a licensed electrician. It's the device that stops an electric shock from killing someone.
3. **Clear the gutters at least twice a year**, and before bushfire season if you're in a bushfire-prone area. Ember attack is the main way houses are lost in bushfires, and leaves in gutters are fuel. See [how to clean gutters safely](/guides/home-maintenance/how-to-clean-gutters/).
4. **Book a yearly termite inspection.** Termite damage usually isn't covered by home insurance, and it's invisible until it's expensive.
5. **Service gas heaters at least every 2 years.** Energy Safe Victoria recommends a service by a licensed gasfitter, including a carbon monoxide test. CO is invisible, odourless and can kill.

## Spring checklist (September–November)

Spring is when Australian homes get ready for storms, heat and bushfire season:

- **Clear gutters and downpipes** before the Fire Danger Period is declared, and trim branches overhanging the roof.
- **Service the air-conditioner** before the first heatwave, and clean the filters.
- **Check the roof from the ground** for tiles or sheets the winter weather moved.
- **Check the pool fence and gate** before swimming season.
- **Check for termite activity.** Termites are most active in warm, humid months. Look for mud tubes on foundations and hollow-sounding timber.
- **Oil the deck** once the weather is dry and warm.
- **Change smoke alarm batteries when daylight saving starts** (in states that have it), if your alarms have replaceable batteries.

## Autumn checklist (March–May)

- **Clear gutters again** after leaf fall, before winter rain.
- **Service gas heaters** before you turn them on for winter, and check flues are clear.
- **Test smoke alarms** before heater season. Home fires peak in winter.
- **Check weather seals** on doors and windows to keep heat in.
- **Clean rainwater tank screens** so autumn leaves don't wash into the tank.

## DIY or a licensed pro?

Most of this list is DIY: testing, cleaning, clearing and checking. The rule of thumb is simple: **if it involves fixed wiring, gas or plumbing connections, it's licensed work.** Pressing the RCD test button is DIY; replacing a faulty RCD needs an electrician. Easing the hot water relief valve is DIY; replacing it needs a plumber. Not sure about a specific job? The [DIY legality checker](/tools/diy-legality-checker/) covers the common ones by state, and [what you can legally DIY](/guides/diy-legal/) explains the rules.

**Working at heights is the real danger.** Ladder and roof falls are one of the most common ways people get badly hurt doing jobs around the home. Use a stable ladder on firm ground, have someone hold it, and never work on a roof alone. If you're not comfortable, gutter cleaning is cheap to outsource.

## Why a schedule beats memory

The problem with home maintenance isn't knowing what to do; it's remembering. Six-monthly and yearly jobs are easy to forget, and a smoke alarm that stopped working in March won't tell you. Pick a system that reminds you: a calendar with repeating events, a note on the fridge, or an app.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should you clean gutters in Australia?** At least twice a year, in late autumn and early spring. Do it more often if you have big trees nearby, and before bushfire season in bushfire-prone areas.

**How often should smoke alarms be replaced?** At least every 10 years. Check the manufacture or expiry date on the back. Test them monthly and vacuum them every six months.

**How often should you test a safety switch?** Every three months, by pressing the test button on each RCD, or more often if the manufacturer says so.

**How often should you get a termite inspection?** At least once a year by a licensed timber pest inspector, and every three to six months in high-risk areas, as recommended by Australian Standard AS 3660.2.

**How often should a gas heater be serviced?** At least every two years by a licensed gasfitter, including a carbon monoxide spillage test.
