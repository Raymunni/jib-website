Dusty panels make less power, so it's tempting to grab a ladder and a bucket. Don't. The cleaning is easy. Getting to the panels is what hurts people. The good news is that most Australian homes don't need to clean their panels often, and when they do, it can usually be done from the ground or by someone with the right gear.

## Is it safe to clean solar panels yourself?

Yes, if you stay on the ground. No, if it means getting on the roof. Solar Victoria's advice for single-storey homes is to brush panels with a long-handled, soft-bristled broom while keeping your feet on the ground, or hose them with a light spray from the ground. Anything that needs you on the roof is a job for a professional with fall protection.

Here's why the roof is a hard no:

- **Ladders put a lot of Australians in hospital.** The Queensland Government says more than 1,000 Queenslanders are hospitalised each year after falling off ladders, mostly around the home, and more than 30 Australians die from ladder falls each year on average. Most are men, many at or near retirement age, doing maintenance at home.
- **Not every part of a roof can hold you.** SafeWork NSW warns that not all areas of a roof are safe to walk on. It describes a worker cleaning gutters who stepped onto polycarbonate sheeting, fell more than three metres and died. Skylights, plastic sheeting and old roofing can give way without warning.
- **Panels can't hold you either.** REC says standing on a panel puts far more force through it than it's designed for. Never stand on, walk on, lean on or press on panels, as this can cause damage you can't see.
- **Wet roofs and panels are slippery,** and you'd be working with a hose in your hand.

## Do solar panels even need cleaning in Australia?

Often not. Rain cleans angled panels for you. REC says its panels are made so that normal, regular rainfall cleans them naturally if they're installed at a sufficient angle. Solar Victoria says dirt you can't reach should wash off when it rains if the panels are on an angle.

Cleaning is worth doing when you can actually see dirt on the glass, because dust and debris block light and reduce output. It's more likely to be needed if:

- the panels are flat or nearly flat. Solar Victoria says rain can pool on flat panels and leave muddy marks, so they may need hosing.
- the air is dusty where you live. REC says how dirty panels get depends on local dust, how much it rains and the angle of the panels.
- birds or overhanging trees leave droppings or debris on the panels.
- there hasn't been decent rain for a long time.

**Check before you clean.** If your inverter has an app or a display, compare output against a clear day from a similar time of year. If output is fine and the glass looks clean from the ground, leave it.

## How to clean solar panels from the ground

Work early in the morning, with water at air temperature, and use nothing harder than a soft brush. This method is for panels you can reach from the ground, on a single-storey home.

1. **Look first, from the ground.** REC warns that damaged panels can be a lethal shock hazard, and the risk is higher when they're wet. If you see cracks, chips, scorch marks or loose cables, don't wet them. Call a licensed electrician.
2. **Pick a cool time.** REC says panels run around 20 degrees above the air temperature as they work, and cool water on hot glass can crack it. Clean early in the morning, before they heat up.
3. **Check the weather.** Skip it if it is windy or raining. A long pole is hard to control in wind, and the Queensland Government advises against using ladders in wind or rain for the same reason.
4. **Hose with a gentle spray.** Use an ordinary garden hose at normal mains pressure and let the water run down the panels from the top. REC says this is fine for its panels.
5. **Brush only if the dirt doesn't rinse off.** Use a soft, non-abrasive brush or sponge on an extension pole, and lightly wipe. REC says to take care not to scratch the glass.
6. **For stubborn marks**, REC allows a mild, biodegradable washing-up liquid, rinsed off straight away with plenty of water. Solar Victoria says water is usually enough and to avoid harsh soaps or chemicals.
7. **Let them air dry.** REC says that's the best way. If you must, a rubber squeegee on a pole, wiping from the top down, gently.

Watch for power lines when you're lifting a long pole near the roof, and keep it well clear of them.

## What never to use on solar panels

- **Pressure washers and steam cleaners.** REC says high-pressure hoses and pressure or steam cleaners aren't permitted on its panels and will void the warranty. Solar Victoria says a pressure hose could damage panels.
- **Hot or cold water on hot panels.** Use water at air temperature, early in the day.
- **Scourers, blades, knives or metal sponges.**
- **Harsh chemicals, acid or alkali detergents.** REC says these must not be used.
- **Your feet or a ladder leaned on the panels.**

Check your own panel maker's manual too. Cleaning that doesn't follow it can void the warranty.

## When to call a professional

Call a professional whenever you'd have to leave the ground: two-storey homes, steep roofs, panels you can't reach with a pole, and any roof you're unsure of. Also call one for:

- **Panels that are very dirty or hard to reach.** Solar Victoria says to get a professional in.
- **Cracks, chips, discolouring or a drop in output.** These need a licensed electrician to check, not a cleaner.
- **Inverter error lights or warning codes.** Solar Victoria says to talk to your installer.
- **Damage after hail or a storm.** Look from the ground only. See the [storm season checklist](/guides/home-maintenance/storm-season-checklist/).

When you book a cleaner, Solar Victoria suggests checking their work won't void your warranty, and says professionals should wear full safety gear and must not stand on or place anything on the panels. You can also ask your installer whether cleaning can be included in your system service.

## Solar maintenance beyond cleaning

Cleaning isn't the same as servicing. Solar Victoria recommends a service at least every 2 years by a licensed electrician, ideally an A-grade electrician accredited by Solar Accreditation Australia. It includes electrical testing and a check for cracks, chips and discolouring. Solar Accreditation Australia took over installer accreditation from the Clean Energy Council in February 2024, so older paperwork may mention the Clean Energy Council instead.

Between services, Solar Victoria suggests a few checks you can do safely:

- Make sure the inverter is running normally with no error lights.
- Keep the inverter clean and free of dust, spider webs and pests, so its vents don't block and overheat it in hot weather.
- Ask your electricity distributor whether it needs a yearly inverter (anti-islanding) test. Solar Victoria says some do.

Don't open the inverter, isolators or any wiring yourself. That's licensed electrical work.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should solar panels be cleaned in Australia?**
There's no set schedule. Clean them when you can see dirt on the glass or output has dropped. On angled panels, rain often does it for you. Flat panels and dusty or coastal areas may need it more often.

**Can I use a pressure washer on solar panels?**
No. REC says pressure and steam cleaners aren't permitted and void its warranty, and Solar Victoria says a pressure hose could damage the panels. A garden hose at normal pressure is enough.

**Can I walk on solar panels?**
Never. Panels aren't designed to take a person's weight in one spot, and the damage isn't always visible.

**Is it worth paying for solar panel cleaning?**
If you can't reach them from the ground and you can see dirt on them, yes. It's much cheaper than a fall. If rain is keeping them clean and output is normal, you probably don't need it.

**What time of day should I clean solar panels?**
Early morning, before the panels heat up. Cool water on hot glass can crack it.

**Can I clean solar panels with tap water?**
Yes. REC prefers deionised water but says rainwater or tap water is fine if there's no deionised water. Make sure it's free of grit.
