A pool fence doesn't stop complying all at once. A pot plant gets parked against it, mulch washes out from under the bottom rail, a kid's bike leans on it, or the gate starts closing slowly. NSW law says owners must keep the barrier compliant **at all times**, not just on the day it was certified.

Most of the checks below take 10 minutes and a tape measure. Do them before the first swim of summer and again after any work near the fence.

## Do the NSW pool laws apply to you?

The Swimming Pools Act 1992 covers any pool or spa at a home that can be filled to **more than 30cm** and is used for swimming, wading or paddling. That includes **portable and inflatable pools**. If you've bought a blow-up pool that holds 30cm of water, it needs a compliant barrier and a CPR sign, just like an in-ground pool.

In a strata or community scheme, the owners corporation is responsible for pools on common property.

## The rules at a glance

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Fence height | At least **1.2m** from the finished ground level |
| Gap under the fence | No more than **10cm** |
| Gaps between vertical bars | No more than **10cm** |
| Horizontal climbable rails | At least **90cm** apart |
| Mesh or perforated panels (1.2m fence) | Holes no bigger than **13mm** |
| Boundary fence used as the pool fence | At least **1.8m** high |
| Non-climbable zone | **90cm** clear on the outside, plus **30cm** inside |
| Gate | Swings **away from the pool**, self-closes from any position, self-latches |
| CPR sign | In good condition, readable from **3m** |
| Register | Every pool and spa on the NSW Swimming Pool Register |

All of these come from the NSW Government's guide for pool owners.

## The 10-minute check

**You need:** a tape measure and a few minutes on a dry day.

### 1. Height, all the way round

Measure from the ground to the top of the fence at several points, especially where the ground rises: beside garden beds, retaining walls, steps and paths. It must be at least 1.2m everywhere. If a garden bed has built up against the fence, the height counts from the new ground level.

### 2. Gaps, including underneath

Nothing bigger than 10cm under the fence or between vertical bars. Pavers sink and mulch and sand wash away, so check the bottom rail all the way along. A gap that was 8cm when the fence went in can be 12cm a few summers later.

### 3. The non-climbable zone

This is where most fences fail. The 90cm zone is measured as an arc from the top of the fence down towards the ground, and there's another 30cm inside the pool area. Keep it clear of:

- BBQs, outdoor chairs and tables
- Pot plants, eskies and toys
- Ladders and bins
- Trees and shrubs, including branches that have grown into the zone
- Anything that gives a small foot or hand a hold

Any horizontal rails a child could climb must be at least 90cm apart.

### 4. The gate, from every position

Open the gate a little, then halfway, then all the way, letting go each time. It must swing shut and latch **without a push**, every time. If it swings in towards the pool, it has to be re-hung to swing outwards.

Then pull, push and jiggle the closed gate. It shouldn't open. Spray the self-closing hinges, lock and latch with lubricating oil or silicone, and tighten any loose bolts and screws. The NSW Government lists all of these as owner maintenance.

**Never prop the gate open**, even for a minute while you carry things in. Keeping pool gates and doors closed is a legal duty in NSW.

### 5. Doors and windows

Some older pools use a house door or window as part of the barrier. The NSW Government says this is no longer allowed. If your pool area is reached straight from the house, ask your council or a registered pool certifier to inspect it.

### 6. The CPR sign

You need a CPR sign near the pool, including for portable pools. It must be in good condition and easy to read from 3m. New signs came in on 1 September 2019 and all new pools must use them. Existing pools only need the new sign if the barrier is substantially altered or rebuilt, but a faded sign you can't read from 3m needs replacing either way.

## Register the pool (it's free online)

Every pool and spa in NSW must be on the **NSW Swimming Pool Register**. Registering online yourself costs nothing. If your council does it for you, it can charge up to $10. Pools are registered against the address, so if you've just bought a place with a pool, search the register first: it may already be there, along with any certificate of compliance.

Registering isn't the same as complying. Compliance is checked separately by an inspection from your council or a registered certifier. The register also has a self-assessment checklist you can work through first.

## Selling or renting a house with a pool

**Selling.** The contract for sale must include a registration certificate from the register, plus one of:

- an occupation certificate issued in the last 3 years
- a valid certificate of compliance
- a certificate of non-compliance

If none of these is attached, the buyer can rescind the contract within 14 days of exchange (unless it has already settled). With a certificate of non-compliance, the buyer takes on the job of fixing the listed defects and has 90 days from settlement to get a certificate of compliance.

**Renting.** The landlord or agent must give the tenant a copy of the certificate of compliance or occupation certificate. A certificate of non-compliance **can't** be used to rent a property.

These rules don't apply to a lot in a strata or community scheme with more than two lots. A certificate of compliance lasts **3 years**, so book an inspection well before you list.

## DIY or a pro?

- **DIY:** moving climbable things out of the zone, trimming plants, topping up the ground under the fence, tightening screws and bolts, oiling hinges and latches, replacing the CPR sign, registering the pool.
- **Pro:** a council or registered pool certifier for a certificate of compliance. A licensed builder, pool technician or fencing contractor for re-hanging a gate, new panels or a new fence. Some certifiers can do minor repairs (up to $1,000) so they can issue a certificate, but you're free to hire someone else.

## Frequently asked questions

**How high does a pool fence have to be in NSW?**
At least 1.2m from the finished ground level. If a boundary fence forms part of the pool barrier, it must be 1.8m.

**How far do pot plants have to be from a pool fence in NSW?**
Outside the 90cm non-climbable zone, and out of the 30cm space inside the pool area.

**Does an inflatable pool need a fence in NSW?**
Yes, if it can hold more than 30cm of water. It needs a compliant barrier and a CPR sign, and it must be registered.

**Do I need a certificate of compliance if I'm not selling or renting?**
Not for the sale or lease paperwork, but your pool must be registered and the barrier must comply at all times.
