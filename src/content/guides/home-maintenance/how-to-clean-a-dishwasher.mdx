A dishwasher washes everything except itself. Food scraps collect in the filter, grease coats the seals, and one day you open the door and the smell hits you. It's one of the easiest jobs in the house to fix, and it usually means cleaner dishes too.

## What you'll need

- A soft brush, such as an old toothbrush or a plastic dish brush
- A toothpick
- A cloth and warm soapy water
- A dishwasher cleaner, or a bowl of white vinegar if your manual allows it

## Step 1: Clean the filter

This is the part that matters most, and most people have never looked at it.

1. **Pull out the bottom rack.**
2. **Twist out the filter.** CHOICE says it usually twists out of the lowest part of the tub without tools. Some dishwashers have a coarse filter and a fine one, so take out both.
3. **Rinse them under hot running water**, and scrub off the grease gently with a soft brush.
4. **Check the hole underneath** and take out anything you can see by hand, such as broken glass or seeds. Watch your fingers.
5. **Refit the filters properly.** Twist until they lock. A loose filter can let food through to the pump.

Some DishDrawer-style models have their own layout, and a filter indicator that flashes when it needs cleaning, so check your manual.

## Step 2: Clear the spray arms

Blocked spray arms are a common cause of dishes coming out dirty.

1. **Remove the spray arms.** Most clip or unscrew, so check your manual if yours doesn't lift out.
2. **Poke a toothpick into the holes** to clear any blockage. CHOICE warns against using sharp metal objects.
3. **Run water through the inlet of each arm.** Miele's check is simple: if water flows freely out of the holes, the arm is clear.
4. **Refit them and spin them by hand** to check they turn freely.

## Step 3: Wipe the bits the water misses

Use a cloth and warm soapy water on the door seals, the bottom edge of the door, and around the detergent dispenser. Grease and bits of food hide in these places, and the wash water doesn't reach them.

## Step 4: Run an empty hot cycle

CHOICE suggests running the hottest cycle with the dishwasher empty, with a commercial cleaner or a bowl of vinegar on the top rack, to help flush out grease, limescale and smells. Follow the directions on any cleaner, and check your manual, as some manufacturers are wary of vinegar.

**If it still smells,** CHOICE suggests sprinkling a cup of baking soda over the bottom of the tub, leaving it overnight, and then running a hot cycle.

## How often to do each job

| Job | How often |
|---|---|
| Clean the filter | About monthly, sooner if you can see food in it. Miele says weekly |
| Clear the spray arms | About monthly, or when dishes come out dirty |
| Wipe the seals and door edges | Monthly |
| Run an empty cleaning cycle | Every 1 to 3 months, as your manual suggests |
| Top up rinse aid and dishwasher salt | When the indicator shows |

Fisher & Paykel's user guides say about monthly for the filter and spray arms. Miele's advice is weekly for the filter. Your own manual is the final word, and if you scrape less off your plates, go shorter.

## What not to use

- **Bleach.** Miele warns it can be corrosive to stainless steel.
- **Scourers and abrasive cleaners.** Fisher & Paykel lists plastic and stainless steel scouring pads among the products it doesn't recommend.
- **Sharp metal tools** in the spray arm holes.
- **Dishwashing liquid in the dispenser.** It makes a huge amount of foam. If it happens, stop the cycle and wait for the foam to settle.

## Common problems

**Water left in the bottom.** Fisher & Paykel lists blocked filters and a crimped, bent or blocked drain hose as likely causes. Clean the filters first, then check that the hose behind the kickboard or under the sink isn't squashed. If the sink itself is draining slowly, see [how to unblock a sink](/guides/how-to/how-to-unblock-a-drain/).

**Dishes come out dirty.** Check for blocked spray arms, an overfilled rack blocking the arms, and a dirty filter.

**White film on glasses.** It's often hard water or too little rinse aid. Check the salt and rinse aid levels if your model uses them.

**It leaks at the door.** Check that the seal is clean and undamaged, and that you've used the right detergent.

If it still won't drain, trips the [safety switch](/guides/how-to/why-does-my-safety-switch-keep-tripping/), or leaks from underneath, turn it off and call a repairer. Replacing a dishwasher can involve plumbing and electrical work, so check [what you can connect yourself](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-connect-a-dishwasher-or-rangehood-myself/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my dishwasher smell?**
Usually food and grease caught in the filter and around the seals. Clean the filter, wipe the seals, and run an empty hot cycle. Leaving the door slightly ajar after a cycle can also help it dry out.

**How often should a dishwasher be cleaned?**
Clean the filter about monthly, and run an empty cleaning cycle every month or few. Check your manual for your model.

**Can I put vinegar in my dishwasher?**
CHOICE suggests a bowl of vinegar on an empty cycle, but it's worth checking your manual, as some brands recommend their own cleaner instead. Don't pour it into the detergent dispenser.

**Where is the filter on my dishwasher?**
Usually in the bottom of the tub, under the lower spray arm. It normally twists out by hand. DishDrawers are a little different, so check the manual.

**Why is there water in the bottom of my dishwasher?**
A little around the filter is normal in many models. Standing water above the filter usually points to a blocked filter or a kinked drain hose.
