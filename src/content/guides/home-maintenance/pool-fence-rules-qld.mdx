A pool fence that passed inspection years ago can quietly stop complying. A pot plant gets moved next to it, a garden bed creeps higher, mulch washes out from under it, or a kicked ball knocks the gate latch out of line. The QBCC is clear that the fence has to comply **at all times**, not just when it was certified.

The good news: most of the checks take 10 minutes and a tape measure. Here's what the rules are and how to check each one.

## The rules at a glance

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Fence height | At least **1200mm** from the finished ground level to the top |
| Gaps | No gap bigger than **100mm** in any direction, including under the fence |
| Outside non-climbable zone | Nothing climbable within **900mm** of the outside |
| Inside clear zone | Nothing climbable within **300mm** of the inside (fences under 1800mm) |
| Gate | Opens **away from the pool**, self-closes and self-latches from **any** open position |
| Outside latch | Release at least **1500mm** above the ground |
| Inside latch | At least **150mm** below the top of the gate, with a **450mm** radius shield |
| Hinges | At least **900mm** apart, or a 60 degree safety cap on the lower hinge |
| CPR sign | At least **300mm by 300mm**, weatherproof, visible from the pool |

All of these come from the QBCC's guidance on the Queensland pool safety standard.

## The 10-minute check

**You need:** a tape measure.

### 1. Height, all the way round

Measure from the ground to the top of the fence at several points, especially where the ground rises: next to garden beds, retaining walls and paths. It must be at least 1200mm everywhere. If a garden bed has crept up, the QBCC says you may need to lower the soil, remove the bed, or raise the fence.

### 2. Gaps, including underneath

Nothing bigger than 100mm in any direction. Check under the fence too. **Sand and mulch move.** The QBCC warns that young children can dig them away to make a gap they can crawl under, so the ground under the fence should be solid.

### 3. Climbable objects

This is where most fences fail. Look for anything within **900mm of the outside** or **300mm of the inside** that a small child could use as a step. The QBCC's list includes:

- BBQs and outdoor furniture
- Pot plants, rockeries, raised garden beds and garden edging
- Trees, shrubs and overhanging branches
- Toys, including trampolines
- Air-conditioning units, taps and pipework
- Steps, ledges and window sills

Move them, or trim them back. Branches grow back, so the QBCC suggests removing a plant entirely if it keeps coming back into the zone.

### 4. The gate, from every position

Open the gate to a few different positions and let go each time. It must swing shut and latch securely **without being pushed**, every time. Then try to pull, push and jiggle it open from closed. It shouldn't budge. Check there's no gap bigger than 10mm around the latch handle.

The QBCC notes that a kicked ball or someone leaning on the gate can knock the latch out of line over time. Oil the hinges as the manufacturer recommends.

**Never prop the gate open.** Propping a pool gate open is an offence in Queensland and can be fined.

### 5. Broken or loose parts

Every part should be rigid and held in with screws or permanent fixings. No broken rails, no wobbly bars or palings, no loose or cracked glass panels, and nothing that can be bent or moved.

### 6. The CPR sign

It must show how to do CPR, starting with **30 chest compressions followed by 2 breaths**, include what to do in an emergency (call 000), be at least 300mm by 300mm, be weatherproof, and be easy to see from near the pool. The QBCC suggests putting it near the shallow end or the steps.

## Boundary fences

If your pool fence is also the boundary fence, you can't control what the neighbours put against their side. The QBCC's option is to build that section **at least 1800mm high** with the 900mm non-climbable zone on the **inside**, measured down from the top. Also check corners where your pool fence meets a paling fence: horizontal rails that line up can make a ladder.

## Selling or leasing

If you sell or lease a property with a pool in Queensland, you need a **pool safety certificate** from a licensed pool safety inspector, unless there's already a current one. You can check the QBCC pool register for an existing certificate. If you sell without one, a **Form 36 (notice of no pool safety certificate)** must be given to the buyer and lodged with the QBCC.

A certificate isn't a pass for the future. Your council can still fine you if the fence stops complying.

## DIY or a pro?

- **DIY:** moving climbable objects, trimming plants, fixing the ground under the fence, tightening screws, oiling hinges, adding a hinge safety cap, replacing the CPR sign.
- **Pro:** a licensed **pool safety inspector** for a certificate or if you're unsure whether it complies, and a fencing contractor for new fences. Fences over 2m, or fences on shared pools, can need building approval.

## Frequently asked questions

**How high does a pool fence have to be in Queensland?**
At least 1200mm from the finished ground level, measured on the outside. A boundary fence relying on an inside non-climbable zone must be at least 1800mm.

**How far do pot plants have to be from a pool fence in Queensland?**
At least 900mm from the outside of the fence and 300mm from the inside.

**Can the pool gate open into the pool area?**
No. In Queensland, pool gates must open away from the pool, and self-close and self-latch from any open position.

**Do I need a pool safety certificate if I'm not selling?**
You only need one when you sell or lease, but your fence has to meet the standard at all times either way.
