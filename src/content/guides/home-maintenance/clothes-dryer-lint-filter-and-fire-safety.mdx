A clothes dryer is a hot box full of tumbling fluff, and on a wet week it might run every day. Australian fire services warn about dryers each winter, and most of what they say comes down to a few small habits. Here's what to do, and how often.

## The dryer checklist

| Job | How often | DIY? |
|---|---|---|
| Clean the lint filter | Every load | Yes |
| Empty the water tank (condenser and heat pump) | Every load, or as your dryer shows | Yes |
| Wash the lint filter | When lint builds up, if your manual allows it | Yes |
| Clean the condenser filter (heat pump and some condenser dryers) | When the light flashes, or as your manual says | Yes |
| Move the dryer and vacuum behind and under it | At least once a year | Yes, with help |
| Inspect and clean the exhaust duct (vented dryers) | Periodically | Yes, if reachable |
| Fix a damaged cord, or a dryer that smells of burning | Straight away | Licensed repairer |

## How to clean the lint filter

1. **Open the door** and pull the filter out. It's usually in the door opening or the bottom of the drum.
2. **Peel the lint off** with your fingers. It usually comes away in one sheet.
3. **Put the filter straight back in.** The CFA warns against running a dryer without a filter, or with one that's loose or damaged.

Every so often, lint leaves a film that lint-peeling alone won't shift. Queensland's fire service suggests cleaning the filter in soapy water from time to time. **Check your manual first:** some filters shouldn't get wet. Fisher & Paykel warns that one of its filter types can block if vacuumed or wetted. If you do wash it, dry it completely before refitting.

## Fire habits that matter

These come straight from Australia's fire services:

- **Let it finish.** Dryers are designed to cool the clothes down at the end of a cycle. South Australia's Metropolitan Fire Service says fires have started from hot clothes left bunched up inside after a cycle was stopped early. If you have to stop it, Fisher & Paykel says to take everything out and spread it to let the heat escape.
- **Don't run it unattended.** The CFA says never to go out or go to bed with the dryer running.
- **Keep oily items out.** Don't dry rags or clothes that have soaked up flammable liquids or oils. Wash clothes worn with oils or sprays in hot water and detergent first, and keep cleaning rags out altogether.
- **Leave room around it.** Dryers need ventilation, so don't let laundry baskets and piles crowd it.

If your dryer smells of burning, feels unusually hot, or keeps stopping, turn it off at the wall and stop using it until it's checked.

## The once-a-year clean

Lint doesn't only collect on the filter. The MFS recommends moving the dryer at least once a year and vacuuming the lint behind and under it.

1. **Turn it off at the wall and unplug it.**
2. **Move it carefully.** Wall-mounted dryers and heavy ones are a two-person lift. If yours is mounted high up and you can't safely reach behind it, get help.
3. **Vacuum** behind, under and around it, plus the vents.
4. **Check the cord and plug** for damage, without opening anything. Damaged cords are a job for a licensed repairer.
5. **Vented dryers:** if yours has a duct, check it's connected and not squashed. Fisher & Paykel says exhaust ducting should be inspected and cleaned periodically.
6. **Push it back** with room left around it, and plug it in.

## Vented, condenser or heat pump?

Each one has its own extra job:

| Dryer type | How it works | Extra maintenance |
|---|---|---|
| Vented | Blows moist air out, often into the room or a duct | Keep the duct and laundry well aired |
| Condenser | Collects the water in a tank or sends it down a drain | Empty the tank, clean any condenser filter |
| Heat pump | Reuses the heat and collects water | Empty the tank, clean the condenser filter |

Fisher & Paykel's heat pump guide says to pull out the condenser filter and clear lint from its mesh with your hand, a cloth or a vacuum crevice nozzle, and its warning lights flash if a filter is blocked. If your dryer starts taking far longer than it used to, a blocked filter is the first thing to check.

## Signs something's wrong

- **Clothes take longer to dry.** Check the filters and the vents first, and don't overload it.
- **The laundry gets steamy or damp.** A vented dryer needs fresh air, and damp air can lead to mould. Open a window or door while it runs.
- **A burning smell, or very hot clothes.** Turn it off and have it checked.
- **It keeps tripping the safety switch.** See [why a safety switch keeps tripping](/guides/how-to/why-does-my-safety-switch-keep-tripping/).

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should you clean a dryer lint filter?**
After every load. It takes seconds, and fire services single it out for good reason.

**Can you leave a clothes dryer running overnight?**
Fire services say not to. The CFA advises never going to bed or going out while it's running.

**Can a dryer catch fire with the filter clean?**
Yes. Lint can build up behind and under it, and hot clothes left bunched together or items with oil on them can also start fires. That's why the other habits matter too.

**Why is my dryer taking so long to dry clothes?**
Common reasons are a blocked filter, an overloaded drum, a full water tank, or poor airflow around the dryer. Clean the filters first and check your manual.

**Can I repair my own dryer?**
Cleaning is DIY. Opening it up, or repairing electrical parts and cords, is a job for a qualified repairer.
