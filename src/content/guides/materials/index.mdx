The most common DIY mistake isn't a bad cut — it's a second trip to the hardware store. These guides give you the full list for common jobs, in the sizes Australian stores actually sell.

## The three calculations behind every list

### 1. Length — timber, edging, rails

Work out the total length you need, then **plan it around the lengths the store sells** (for example 1.8, 2.4, 3.0 and 3.6 m for sleepers). A bed designed around a 2.4 m sleeper wastes almost nothing; a 2.5 m bed wastes a lot.

### 2. Area — pavers, tiles, membranes, paint

**Area = length × width** (split odd shapes into rectangles). Then add an allowance for cuts and breakages: less for a simple straight-laid rectangle, more for diagonal patterns, curves and lots of edges. Check coverage rates on the product itself — adhesives, membranes and paints all state how many square metres a bag or tin covers.

### 3. Volume — soil, sand, road base, concrete

**Volume (m³) = area (m²) × depth (m)**. Loose materials like road base shrink when compacted, so order extra. Landscape suppliers usually sell by the tonne, so multiply by the supplier's density figure.

## Before you buy

- **Check what needs a licence or approval first.** Materials for a job you can't legally do yourself are wasted money — see [what you can legally DIY](/guides/diy-legal/) and [council approval rules](/guides/council-approval/).
- **Ask about delivery minimums** for bulk materials — it's often cheaper to order a little extra than pay a second delivery.
- **Keep receipts and offcuts** until the job's finished.
