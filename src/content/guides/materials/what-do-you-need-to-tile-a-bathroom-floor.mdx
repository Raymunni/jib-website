Tiling a bathroom floor is one of the most satisfying DIY jobs, and one of the easiest to under-buy for. Running out of adhesive halfway through, or finding the second batch of tiles is a slightly different shade, is how a weekend turns into a fortnight. Here's the complete list.

**Want exact numbers for your room?** The [bathroom floor calculator](/tools/bathroom-floor-calculator/) works out tiles, adhesive, grout, membrane and primer in one go.

## Materials

| Material | How much | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Floor tiles** | Floor area + 10% (15% for diagonal or herringbone) | Check the slip rating: P3 or higher is a common choice for bathroom floors. Buy one batch. |
| **Tile adhesive** | ~3.5 kg/m² (6 mm notch) to ~7 kg/m² (12 mm notch) | A flexible, cement-based adhesive rated for wet areas. Bigger tiles need a bigger notch. |
| **Grout** | Depends on tile size and joint width: ~0.15 kg/m² for 300 × 600 mm tiles with 2 mm joints | Cement grout for most floors; epoxy grout is harder-wearing and more stain-resistant but trickier to use. |
| **Waterproofing membrane** | ~1.4 L/m² for two coats | Shower area floor and walls to 1800 mm, plus the rest of the floor and wall upturns. [Check who can apply it](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-waterproof-my-own-bathroom/). |
| **Membrane primer** | ~0.15 L/m² | Must be compatible with your membrane. |
| **Bond breaker / sealant tape** | Perimeter of the room + shower corners | Lets the membrane flex at wall–floor and wall–wall junctions. |
| **Wet-area silicone** | One 300 mL cartridge per ~8 m of joint | For the wall–floor junction and around fixtures. Never grout movement joints. |
| **Sand and cement screed** | Depends on the fall needed | Only if the floor needs re-grading to the waste. |
| **Grout sealer** | Optional | Helps cement grout resist stains. |

## Tools

- **Notched trowel** sized to your tiles (6, 10 or 12 mm)
- **Tile cutter** (manual rail cutter for straight cuts) and an **angle grinder with a diamond blade** or **tile nippers** for cut-outs around the waste
- **Tile spacers** or a **levelling clip system** (worth it for tiles 600 mm and over)
- **Spirit level** and a **straight edge**
- **Rubber grout float**, **buckets** and **sponges**
- **Mixing paddle** for a drill, for adhesive and grout
- **Tape measure**, **pencil** and **chalk line**
- **Knee pads**, **safety glasses**, **gloves** and a **P2 mask** for cutting and mixing

## Five mistakes that cost the most

1. **Tiling over an uncured membrane.** Every membrane has a minimum cure time before tiling. Read the data sheet and wait.
2. **No fall to the waste.** Water should run to the floor waste, not sit in the corners. Check the fall before you tile, not after.
3. **Grout in movement joints.** The joints where the floor meets the walls, and at changes of plane, get silicone. Grout there cracks.
4. **Too little adhesive coverage.** Floor tiles need close to full coverage, especially in wet areas. Lift a tile early on and check the back.
5. **Mixed batches.** Tiles from different batches can differ slightly in shade and size. Buy all you need, plus spares, at once.

## What isn't DIY

Laying tiles, grouting and siliconing are yours to do in every state. **Moving or connecting the floor waste, shower waste or any pipes is licensed plumbing**, and **anything electrical** (heated floors, exhaust fans, new lights) needs a licensed electrician. Waterproofing depends on your state and the size of the job: see [can I waterproof my own bathroom?](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-waterproof-my-own-bathroom/)

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I tile over existing floor tiles?** Sometimes, if the old tiles are sound, well-bonded and flat, and the extra height works with doors and the floor waste. You'll need a primer suited to tile-on-tile. In a wet area, it also means relying on the old waterproofing underneath.

**How long before I can walk on new floor tiles?** Typically 24 hours for standard adhesive, and longer before grouting or heavy use in cold weather. Rapid-set adhesives are faster. Follow the bag.

**What size notched trowel for 600 × 600 tiles?** Usually a 12 mm square notch, plus back-buttering each tile for full coverage. For 300 × 600 mm tiles, a 10 mm notch is typical.
