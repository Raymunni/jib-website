Most paving problems — sinking, rocking pavers, puddles — come from the base, not the pavers. Getting the depths right, and buying enough material, is most of the job.

## How deep to dig

A typical pedestrian path or patio build-up, from the bottom:

| Layer | Depth |
|---|---|
| Compacted road base (crushed rock) | 50–100 mm |
| Bedding sand | 20–30 mm |
| Pavers | Usually 30–60 mm, depending on the paver |

Bunnings' guide uses 50 mm of compacted road base plus 20–30 mm of bedding sand, so for 30 mm pavers you'd dig about 100–110 mm below the finished surface. A deeper base (around 100 mm) is sensible on soft or clay soils. Council specifications for footpaths use around 25 mm of compacted bedding sand.

**Driveways are different.** Cars need a much deeper, engineered base, and a driveway crossover to the street has to meet your council's specification. Check with them before you start.

## Working out quantities

**Volume (m³) = area (m²) × depth (m)**

Road base shrinks when it's compacted, so order extra — roughly **25% more** than the compacted volume. Then convert to tonnes, because landscape suppliers usually sell by weight.

Approximate densities (they vary by supplier — ask for their figure):

- Road base: about **1.8–2.0 tonnes per m³**
- Bedding sand: about **1.6 tonnes per m³**

## Worked example: a 10 m² patio

**Road base (100 mm compacted):**
10 m² × 0.10 m = 1.0 m³ compacted
× 1.25 for compaction = **1.25 m³ loose**
× 1.8–2.0 t/m³ ≈ **2.3–2.5 tonnes**

**Bedding sand (30 mm):**
10 m² × 0.03 m = **0.3 m³**
× 1.6 t/m³ ≈ **0.5 tonnes**

**Pavers:** 10 m² plus an allowance for cuts and breakages. How much depends on the pattern and how many cuts your edges need — straight lay needs the least; herringbone and curves need more. Check with your supplier.

## Build steps in brief

1. **Mark out and excavate** to the total depth, sloping the base away from the house so water drains off (a gentle fall is enough).
2. **Lay and compact the road base** in layers, with a plate compactor — several passes each layer.
3. **Screed the bedding sand** to an even depth using rails and a straight edge. Don't walk on it.
4. **Lay the pavers**, working from a straight edge, and cut the edges.
5. **Compact the pavers** into the sand and sweep jointing sand into the gaps.
6. **Edge restraint:** make sure the edges are held — by a wall, a concrete haunch or an edge strip — or the pavers will creep over time.
