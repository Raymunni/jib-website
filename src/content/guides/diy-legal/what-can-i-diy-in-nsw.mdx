New South Wales requires a licence for plumbing and electrical work “regardless of the cost”, with a small set of exceptions for owner-occupiers doing repairs. Here’s where the line sits for the jobs people ask about most.

We checked 23 of the most common home jobs against New South Wales’s regulators. Each row says whether you can do it yourself, and why.

## The quick list

**You can DIY:** change a light globe; replace a battery-only smoke alarm; plug an appliance into an existing power point; change a tap washer or jumper valve; clear a blocked sink or drain with a plunger; patch a hole in plasterboard; regrout tiles or replace shower silicone.

**Needs a licensed tradie:** replace or add a power point; replace a light switch; replace a light fitting or downlight; install a ceiling fan; install a hardwired smoke alarm; connect a hardwired oven, cooktop or rangehood; replace a whole tap or mixer; replace a toilet suite; connect a dishwasher to water and drain; replace a hot water system; install or move a gas appliance or bayonet.

**Check with the regulator first:** replace the plug on an appliance or extension lead; replace a screw-on showerhead; replace washers inside a toilet cistern; plug a portable gas heater into an existing bayonet; waterproof your own bathroom (membrane).

## Rules specific to New South Wales

These are the jobs where New South Wales’s regulator has said something specific:

- **Replace or add a power point:** In NSW a licence is needed for any electrical wiring work, regardless of cost or type. Your electrician lodges a CCEW when finished.
- **Change a tap washer or jumper valve:** An owner-occupier may repair a tap in their home (not thermostatic mixing or tempering valves).
- **Replace a screw-on showerhead:** NSW lets an owner-occupier repair a showerhead. Replacing one outright isn't clearly covered, check with NSW Fair Trading.
- **Replace a toilet suite:** NSW requires a licence for any plumbing work regardless of cost; individuals face fines of up to $22,000.
- **Waterproof your own bathroom (membrane):** Contractor licences apply to waterproofing work for others over $5,000. On your own home, an owner-builder permit is needed over $10,000 if the work needs a DA or CDC. Must meet AS 3740.
