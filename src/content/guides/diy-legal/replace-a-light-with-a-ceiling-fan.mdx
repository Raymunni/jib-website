The bedroom light is already in the middle of the ceiling, the wiring's right there, and summer's coming. Swapping it for a fan feels like it should be simple. It mostly is, for a licensed electrician. Here's what to know before you call one.

## Can I do it myself?

No, not the wiring. Connecting a fan to the fixed wiring in your house is electrical work, and in Australia that needs a licence.

- **Victoria:** Energy Safe Victoria says doing your own electrical work is illegal and can be deadly, and that you should use a registered electrical contractor.
- **WA:** Building and Energy says it's illegal for an unlicensed person to do electrical work, including changes to fixed wiring, and warns against replacing fittings and switches yourself.

The other states work the same way. Our [ceiling fan and light fitting guide](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-install-a-ceiling-fan-or-light-fitting-myself/) covers each state.

What you **can** do: pick the fan, measure the room and ceiling, move furniture, and have the box ready when the electrician arrives.

## What it costs

Beacon Lighting publishes its installation prices, which make a useful benchmark:

| Job | From |
|---|---|
| Replacing a fan on existing wiring | $285 for the first fan, $160 for each extra |
| New fan install, with the switches in the box | $350 for the first fan, $280 for each extra |

Those are for ceilings up to 3 m. Beacon's service isn't offered in NSW, and any electrician will quote their own price. Doing two or three rooms in one visit is the easiest way to bring the price per fan down.

### What adds to the price

- **A separate switch for the fan.** Swapping a light usually means the fan needs its own control. Beacon notes remote-controlled fans still need an isolation switch nearby, and wall controls need extra wiring.
- **No existing light point** where you want the fan.
- **High or raked ceilings.** Over 3 m usually needs a longer downrod and more time. Angled ceilings need a canopy rated for the slope.
- **Hard roof access,** such as a low roof space or a two-storey house.

## Ceiling height and blade height

Beacon's guide says the blades must sit **at least 2.1 m above the floor**. As a rough guide from the same source:

| Ceiling height | Fan type |
|---|---|
| Under 2.4 m | Low-profile (hugger) fan, no rod |
| 2.4 m | Low-profile or standard |
| 2.7 m | Standard |
| 3 m and up | Standard with an extension rod |

Measure your ceiling before you buy. Standard Australian ceilings are often 2.4 m, and a standard fan on a downrod can bring the blades too low.

## What size fan for the room?

Blade span is the width from tip to tip. Manufacturers' guides differ a little. Beacon's is:

| Room size | Blade span |
|---|---|
| Up to 5 m² | Up to 107 cm |
| 5 to 7 m² | 107 to 122 cm |
| 7 to 13 m² (most bedrooms) | 122 to 132 cm |
| 13 to 20 m² (living rooms) | 132 to 142 cm |
| 20 to 36 m² | 142 cm and up |

For long or very large rooms, two smaller fans usually work better than one big one.

## What to ask your electrician

1. Can the fan go on the existing light point, and is there a joist or brace to fix it to?
2. What control will it have: wall control, remote, or both, and where will the isolation switch go?
3. Is the price for supply and install, or install only? Does it include taking the old light away?
4. Will I get a certificate for the work? (In Victoria that's a Certificate of Electrical Safety.)

Then set the fan to spin the right way for summer: our [fan direction guide](/guides/how-to/ceiling-fan-direction-summer/) explains how.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I replace a light with a ceiling fan myself in Australia?**
No. It's fixed wiring work, which must be done by a licensed electrician in every state.

**How much does it cost to replace a light with a ceiling fan?**
From around $285 on existing wiring and around $350 for a new install with switches, based on Beacon Lighting's prices. High ceilings, new wiring and separate wall controls cost more.

**How high do ceiling fan blades need to be?**
At least 2.1 m above the floor.

**What size ceiling fan for a bedroom?**
For most bedrooms (7 to 13 m²), a blade span of about 122 to 132 cm.
