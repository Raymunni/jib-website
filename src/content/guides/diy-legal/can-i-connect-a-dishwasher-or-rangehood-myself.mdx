Kitchen appliances sit at the meeting point of three trades — electrical, plumbing and gas — so "can I install it myself?" usually has a mixed answer. The deciding question is: **does it plug in, or is it wired in?**

## At a glance

| Appliance | Plug-in to an existing power point | Hardwired / new connection |
|---|---|---|
| Dishwasher (power) | <span class="v ok">DIY OK</span> | <span class="v no">Licensed electrician</span> |
| Dishwasher (water and drain) | — | <span class="v no">Licensed plumber</span> in most states |
| Wall oven | <span class="v ok">DIY OK</span> if it has a plug | <span class="v no">Licensed electrician</span> |
| Electric cooktop | Rarely plug-in | <span class="v no">Licensed electrician</span> |
| Rangehood | <span class="v ok">DIY OK</span> to mount and plug in | <span class="v no">Licensed electrician</span> |
| Gas cooktop or oven | — | <span class="v no">Licensed gasfitter</span> |

## Electrical: plug-in yes, hardwired no

Queensland's Electrical Safety Office puts it clearly: hardwired appliances "must be connected by a licensed electrician". Its own example is useful — fitting an electric wall oven into a cabinet, without connecting it, is not electrical work. So you can often do the heavy lifting and fitting, then have an electrician make the connection.

If your appliance has a plug and there's already a suitable power point in the right place, plugging it in is yours to do. If there isn't a power point, or it's in the wrong spot, adding or moving one is licensed work.

## Plumbing: the dishwasher connection

Connecting a dishwasher's inlet to the water supply and its outlet to the drain isn't on Queensland's or Western Australia's lists of work homeowners can do, and NSW requires a licence for any plumbing work regardless of cost. Treat it as a job for a licensed plumber unless your state's regulator confirms otherwise.

Washing machines are the common exception people point to: screwing the hoses onto existing washing-machine taps is generally treated as normal appliance use. A dishwasher, which usually needs a connection under the sink, is different.

## Gas: always a gasfitter

Installing, moving or replacing a gas cooktop, oven or any fixed gas appliance is licensed gasfitting in every state. In Victoria, installing a gas appliance always needs a compliance certificate, whatever the cost.

## What you can do to save money

- Measure the cavity and check clearances before you buy.
- Remove the old appliance once it's been safely disconnected by the right tradie.
- Slide the new unit in, level it, and fit any trims or kickboards.
- Book one visit where the electrician (and plumber) connect everything at once.
