It's one of the most-searched DIY questions in Australia, and the answer is the same in every state: **no**. A power point looks simple — three wires, two screws — but it's connected to fixed wiring, and working on fixed wiring is licensed electrical work everywhere in the country.

## What the regulators actually say

- **Victoria:** Energy Safe Victoria says electrical work "isn't a DIY job, even for small jobs such as changing power points or light switches."
- **Queensland:** the Electrical Safety Office lists installing a power point and replacing a switch as electrical work for a licensed electrician. It says fines for individuals can reach $40,000.
- **New South Wales:** a licence is needed for "any electrical wiring work", regardless of cost or type.
- **Western Australia:** "never replace fittings and switches yourself."
- **SA, Tasmania, ACT and NT:** the same general rule applies — use a licensed electrician.

"Like-for-like" doesn't change the answer. Swapping a cracked power point for an identical new one is still electrical work.

## Why a "simple" swap is licensed

Most of the risk isn't in the power point itself — it's in what you can't see. Old wiring can be brittle or wrongly connected, the circuit may not be the one you switched off, and a loose connection can overheat inside the wall long after the job looks finished. A licensed electrician also tests the circuit, including the earth, before signing off.

## What you can do instead

- **Plug-in upgrades:** smart plugs, plug-in USB chargers and good-quality powerboards with overload protection don't touch fixed wiring.
- **Plan the job:** mark exactly where you want new or moved points, how many outlets, and whether you want USB or double points. Clear decisions keep the call-out short.
- **Batch it:** most of an electrician's cost is getting to you. Save up the power points, switches, light fittings and fans into one visit.
- **Prep and finish:** clear furniture, patch and paint afterwards. Don't cut holes in walls for new points yourself — leave that to the electrician, who needs to check what's behind the plasterboard.

## Ask for the certificate

When the work is finished, your electrician should give you a compliance certificate: a Certificate of Compliance for Electrical Work (CCEW) in NSW, a Certificate of Electrical Safety in Victoria, an Electrical Safety Certificate in WA, or an electronic Certificate of Compliance in SA. Keep it — it's proof the work was done lawfully if you ever sell or make an insurance claim.
