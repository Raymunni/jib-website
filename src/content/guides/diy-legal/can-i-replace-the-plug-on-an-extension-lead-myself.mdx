Rewiring a plug used to be something every household did. In Australia, it's now treated as electrical work in at least one state, and it isn't clearly allowed anywhere else.

## The rules by state

| State | Replace a plug on a lead or appliance |
|---|---|
| Queensland | <span class="v no">Licensed electrician</span> |
| Other states and territories | <span class="v maybe">Check with the regulator first</span> |

- **Queensland:** the Electrical Safety Office lists replacing a plug on the end of an extension lead, and constructing an extension lead, as licensed electrical work.
- **Everywhere else:** we couldn't confirm the position from the regulators. Don't assume it's allowed; ask your state's electrical safety regulator, or have an electrician do it.

## Why it matters

A plug that's wired wrongly can make the appliance's metal case live, or leave the earth disconnected so a fault can't trip the safety switch. Cords with a loose or cracked plug are one of the most common causes of electrical shocks and fires at home.

## What to do with a damaged plug or cord

- **Stop using it straight away** if the plug is cracked, scorched, loose in its casing, or the cord is cut or crushed.
- **Replace cheap leads outright.** A new extension lead usually costs less than a repair.
- **For appliances worth fixing,** take them to an appliance repairer or electrician.
- **Never tape up a damaged cord** as a fix.

## What you can do yourself

- Plug appliances into existing power points
- Use powerboards and extension leads that are in good condition, without piggybacking them
- Check leads and plugs for damage before using them outdoors or with power tools
