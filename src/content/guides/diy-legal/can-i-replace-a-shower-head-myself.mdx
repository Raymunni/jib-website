A new shower head is one of the cheapest bathroom upgrades there is, and a water-efficient one can cut your water and hot water bills. Whether you can fit it yourself depends on your state.

## The rules by state

| State | Replace a screw-on shower head |
|---|---|
| Queensland | <span class="v ok">DIY OK</span> |
| Victoria | <span class="v ok">DIY OK</span> (water-efficient shower head) |
| Western Australia | <span class="v ok">DIY OK</span> |
| Tasmania | <span class="v ok">DIY OK</span> like-for-like, including the hose |
| New South Wales | <span class="v maybe">Repair only, check before replacing</span> |
| SA, ACT, NT | <span class="v maybe">Check with the regulator first</span> |

What the regulators say:

- **Queensland:** replacing a shower head is on the QBCC's list of unregulated plumbing work.
- **Victoria:** changing over to a water-efficient shower head is exempt from the plumbing licensing rules.
- **Western Australia:** owners can maintain, repair or replace a shower head.
- **Tasmania:** owners can replace shower heads and shower hoses like-for-like on their own property.
- **New South Wales:** an owner-occupier can repair a shower head. Replacing one outright isn't clearly covered, so check with NSW Fair Trading.

## How to replace a screw-on shower head

This covers a head that screws onto an existing shower arm, where you're allowed to do it.

1. **Buy the right head.** Look for a good WELS water-efficiency star rating. Most Australian shower arms take a standard ½-inch thread.
2. **Unscrew the old head** anticlockwise. Use a cloth around the nut if you need a wrench, so you don't scratch the finish.
3. **Clean the thread** on the shower arm and remove old tape.
4. **Wrap the thread** with two or three turns of thread seal tape, clockwise.
5. **Screw on the new head** by hand, then snug it a quarter turn. Don't overtighten.
6. **Run the water** and check for drips at the joint. A drip usually means a little more tape.

If the shower arm itself is loose, cracked or leaking at the wall, stop there: that's pipework, and it's a plumber's job.

## When to call a plumber

- Leaks behind the wall or at the wall plate
- Replacing the mixer, taps or shower arm
- Converting to a rail shower that needs new pipework
- Water pressure problems that a new head doesn't fix
