Storm season shows up every weakness in a tiled roof, and the ridge is usually first. The curved caps along the top of the roof sit on a bed of mortar, sealed with pointing. When that pointing cracks, water gets in under the caps, washes out the bedding, and the caps start to work loose.

Repointing sounds like a weekend job. Sometimes it is. But it's worth being honest about what it involves before you go up.

## Repointing or rebedding?

- **Pointing** is the outer layer you can see along the edges of each cap. On most roofs today it's a flexible pointing compound. When it's cracked but the caps underneath are solid, the fix is to **repoint**: clean it back and apply new pointing over the top.
- **Bedding** is the mortar the caps actually sit on. If the caps rock when pressed, have slipped out of line, or the bedding crumbles away, they need to be **rebedded**: lifted off, re-laid on new bedding, then pointed.

Repointing over loose caps only hides the problem. If the caps move, it's a rebed, and that's a roofer's job.

## Is it legal to do yourself?

Ridge capping isn't electrical or plumbing work, so the licensed-trade rules that stop most DIY don't apply. Building licences are mostly about who you **pay** to do the work.

- **Queensland:** the QBCC says an owner doing work on their own property doesn't need a licence where the work is valued at no more than **$11,000**. Above that, you need an owner-builder permit or a licensed contractor.
- **Other states:** check your state's building regulator before starting, and make sure any roofer you hire holds the licence or registration your state requires.

Legal isn't the same as safe, though.

## The honest part: roofs are where people fall

In 2024 SafeWork NSW investigated a gutter cleaner, a professional, who slid down a house roof and fell about 5 metres. WorkSafe Queensland was notified of **173 falls from a roof or through a ceiling** in just over five years. For construction work, NSW requires a written safe work method statement wherever someone could fall more than 2 metres, and SafeWork says guardrails or a work platform should come before a harness.

Most homeowners have none of that. The hazards SafeWork NSW lists for residential roofs are the same ones you'd face:

- Unprotected edges
- Wet or windy weather, and slippery surfaces like glazed tiles
- Losing balance on steep or sloping roofs
- Skylights, vents and fragile surfaces
- Live power lines near the roof

## Check it from the ground first

You can learn most of what you need without climbing anything.

**From the yard,** using your phone's zoom or binoculars, look for:

- Cracked or missing chunks of pointing along the ridge
- Caps that sit out of line, or gaps between a cap and the tiles
- Broken or slipped caps after a storm
- Bits of mortar or pointing in the gutters or on the ground below

**From inside the roof space,** with a torch, on a sunny day and after rain:

- Daylight showing along the ridge
- Water stains or damp timber under the ridge
- Stains on the ceiling below the ridge line

Take photos of everything. They're what a roofer needs to quote, and what you'll compare against next year.

## When it can be DIY

A careful DIY repoint is reasonable when **all** of these are true:

- It's a single-storey roof with a low pitch
- The pointing is cracked in a few spots, but the caps don't move when pressed
- The tiles aren't glazed, mossy or wet
- You have safe access (a ladder set up properly on firm, level ground) and someone with you
- It's a dry, calm day with no rain due while the pointing cures

Walk as little as possible and step on the lower part of each tile, where it overlaps the one below, to avoid cracking tiles. Follow the pointing compound's instructions for cleaning back, application and curing time.

## When to call a roofer

- The caps are loose, slipped, cracked or missing
- The roof is two-storey, steep, or has glazed or mossy tiles
- Water is already coming in, or you can see daylight in the roof space
- The whole ridge (and the hips) need doing, not a few spots
- You're not comfortable on a ladder or a roof. That's reason enough.

Ask the roofer whether they're repointing or rebedding, how the caps will be fixed down, and for before and after photos of the ridge.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I know if my ridge capping needs repointing?**
Cracked or missing pointing, gaps between the caps and the tiles, mortar in the gutters, or water stains in the roof space under the ridge.

**Do I need a licence to repoint my own roof?**
Usually not for an owner doing minor work on their own home. In Queensland, owners can do up to $11,000 of work on their own property without a QBCC licence. Any roofer you pay must hold the licence your state requires.

**Can I just repoint over loose ridge caps?**
No. If the caps move, the bedding underneath has failed and they need to be rebedded, not just repointed.

**Is it safe to walk on a tiled roof?**
It's risky. Tiles crack, glazed and wet tiles are slippery, and falls from house roofs injure and kill people every year. Check from the ground and the roof space first, and use a roofer for anything beyond a small repair on a low roof.
