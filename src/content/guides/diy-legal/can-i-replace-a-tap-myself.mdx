A dripping tap is usually a worn washer or jumper valve, a cheap part and a genuinely DIY-friendly fix in most of Australia. Replacing the whole tap or mixer is a different story, and that's where people get caught out.

## How to change a tap washer

| Time | Cost | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| About 20 minutes | Under $15 in parts | Easy, with a spanner |

**You'll need:** an adjustable spanner, a flat screwdriver, a new washer and jumper valve (a mixed pack covers most taps), and a cloth to protect the chrome.

1. **Turn off the water** at the isolation valve under the sink, or at the mains if there isn't one.
2. **Open the tap fully** to drain the line and release the pressure.
3. **Take off the handle and cover:** prise off the cap, undo the screw, then unscrew the headgear with the spanner (wrap the cloth around it first).
4. **Swap the washer or jumper valve** for one the same size. Take the old one to the hardware store to match it.
5. **Reassemble**, turn the water back on slowly and check for drips.

Still dripping after a new washer? The seat inside the tap body is probably worn, and reseating it is usually a job for a plumber. For ceramic disc taps and mixer cartridges, see [how to fix a dripping tap](/guides/how-to/how-to-fix-a-dripping-tap/).

## Is changing a tap washer legal? The rules by state

| State | Change a washer or jumper valve | Replace the whole tap or mixer |
|---|---|---|
| Queensland | <span class="v ok">DIY OK</span> | <span class="v no">Licensed plumber</span> |
| Western Australia | <span class="v ok">DIY OK</span> (repairing a tap) | <span class="v no">Licensed plumber</span> |
| Victoria | <span class="v ok">DIY OK</span> (minor tap repairs) | <span class="v no">Licensed plumber</span> |
| New South Wales | <span class="v ok">DIY OK</span> (repairing a tap) | <span class="v no">Licensed plumber</span> |
| Tasmania | <span class="v ok">DIY OK</span> | <span class="v ok">DIY OK</span> like-for-like |
| SA, ACT, NT | <span class="v maybe">Check with regulator</span> | <span class="v no">Licensed plumber</span> |

What the regulators say:

- **Queensland:** the QBCC's list of unregulated work includes replacing a jumper valve or washer in a tap.
- **Western Australia:** since March 2024, owners (or someone helping them unpaid) can maintain or repair a tap, but not a thermostatic mixing tap.
- **Victoria:** tap washers and other minor tap repairs in a home are exempt from the plumbing licensing rules.
- **New South Wales:** plumbing needs a licence "regardless of the cost", but the regulations let an owner-occupier repair a tap or showerhead (not thermostatic mixing or tempering valves).
- **Tasmania:** owners can repair or replace tapware like-for-like as low-risk work on their own property.

## Why "just swapping the tap" is licensed

Replacing a tap or mixer means disconnecting and reconnecting the water supply, often with new flexible hoses, isolation valves and seals under the sink or in the wall. Get it wrong and you get a slow leak inside a cabinet or wall cavity, the kind of damage that isn't obvious until it's expensive. That's why most states draw the line at repair.

## When to call a plumber straight away

- Mixers and quarter-turn ceramic cartridges you're not sure how to service
- Any leak inside a wall, or water where it shouldn't be
- Thermostatic mixing valves and tempering valves (hot water safety devices)
- Anything involving gas hot water

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I change a tap washer in Australia?**
Turn off the water, open the tap, remove the handle and headgear, swap the washer or jumper valve for the same size, and reassemble. It's about a 20-minute job.

**Do I need a plumber to change a tap washer?**
No, in most states. Changing a washer or jumper valve is legal DIY in Queensland, WA, Victoria and Tasmania, and owner-occupiers can repair a tap in NSW. Replacing the whole tap or mixer usually needs a licensed plumber.

**Do I need to turn the water off at the mains?**
Only if the tap has no isolation valve. Most kitchen and basin taps have one under the sink; showers and older baths often don't.

**Why is my tap still dripping after changing the washer?**
The tap seat may be worn or pitted, or it may be a ceramic disc tap that needs a new cartridge rather than a washer.
