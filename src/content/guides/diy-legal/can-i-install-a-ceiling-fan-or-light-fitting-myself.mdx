Ceiling fans and new light fittings are two of the most popular weekend upgrades — and two of the jobs people most often assume they can do themselves. In Australia, you can't.

## The rule

A ceiling fan, pendant, oyster light or downlight is wired into the lighting circuit. Connecting, disconnecting or replacing it is electrical installation work, which needs a licensed electrician in every state and territory.

Queensland's Electrical Safety Office names these jobs specifically: replacing a batten holder with a new light fitting, and replacing a light fitting with a ceiling fan, are both listed as licensed electrical work. Western Australia's advice is "never replace fittings and switches yourself", and Victoria's regulator says electrical work isn't a DIY job "even for small jobs".

## What you can do yourself

- **Change globes and tubes** — that's normal use of the fitting. Switch off first and let halogen or incandescent globes cool.
- **Choose and buy the fittings.** Check the fan suits the room size and ceiling height, and that the fitting is rated for where it's going (bathrooms and outdoor areas need suitable IP ratings). Having everything on site before the electrician arrives saves a return trip.
- **Assemble fans before the visit** if the instructions allow it — blades, light kit and brackets — so the electrician only has to mount, wire and test.
- **Plan switch positions.** Decide whether you want wall controls, remote or smart control, and where switches go, before the visit.
- **Patch and paint** around old fittings afterwards.

## What about plug-in lights?

Anything that plugs into an existing power point — lamps, plug-in LED strips, plug-pack garden lighting — isn't fixed wiring, so it's fine to install yourself. The moment a light is hardwired or needs a new power point, it's back to a licensed electrician.

## After the job

Ask for the compliance certificate for your state (for example a CCEW in NSW or a Certificate of Electrical Safety in Victoria) and keep it with your home records.
