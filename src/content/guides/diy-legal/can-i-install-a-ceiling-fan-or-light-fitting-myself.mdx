Ceiling fans and new light fittings are two of the most popular weekend upgrades, and two of the jobs people most often assume they can do themselves. In Australia, you can't.

## The rule

A ceiling fan, pendant, oyster light or downlight is wired into the lighting circuit. Connecting, disconnecting or replacing it is electrical installation work, which needs a licensed electrician in every state and territory.

Queensland's Electrical Safety Office names these jobs specifically: replacing a batten holder with a new light fitting, and replacing a light fitting with a ceiling fan, are both listed as licensed electrical work. Western Australia's advice is "never replace fittings and switches yourself", and Victoria's regulator says electrical work isn't a DIY job "even for small jobs".

## What you can do yourself

- **Change globes and tubes**: that's normal use of the fitting. Switch off first and let halogen or incandescent globes cool.
- **Choose and buy the fittings.** Check the fan suits the room size and ceiling height, and that the fitting is rated for where it's going (bathrooms and outdoor areas need suitable IP ratings). Having everything on site before the electrician arrives saves a return trip.
- **Assemble fans before the visit** if the instructions allow it, blades, light kit and brackets, so the electrician only has to mount, wire and test.
- **Plan switch positions.** Decide whether you want wall controls, remote or smart control, and where switches go, before the visit.
- **Patch and paint** around old fittings afterwards.

## What about plug-in lights?

Anything that plugs into an existing power point, lamps, plug-in LED strips, plug-pack garden lighting, isn't fixed wiring, so it's fine to install yourself. The moment a light is hardwired or needs a new power point, it's back to a licensed electrician.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I install a ceiling fan myself in Australia?**
No. A ceiling fan connects to the fixed wiring, so installing one is licensed electrical work in every state and territory, even where it replaces an old fan or light in the same spot.

**Do I need an electrician to replace a ceiling fan?**
Yes. Disconnecting the old fan and connecting the new one is electrical installation work. Queensland's Electrical Safety Office lists replacing a light fitting with a ceiling fan as licensed work.

**Can I change a light fitting myself?**
No. Swapping a pendant, oyster light, batten holder or downlight is licensed electrical work in every state. Changing the globe or tube is yours to do.

**How does an electrician install a ceiling fan?**
They check the ceiling can carry the fan, fix the mounting bracket to the framing, connect the fan to the lighting circuit with your chosen wall, remote or smart control, test it and give you a compliance certificate.

**What can I do myself to save on a ceiling fan install?**
Choose a fan that suits the room size and ceiling height, have it on site, assemble the blades, light kit and brackets beforehand if the instructions allow, decide where the switches go, and patch and paint afterwards.

**Can I put a ceiling fan in a bathroom or outdoors?**
Only one rated for that location, with a suitable IP rating, installed by a licensed electrician. Check the rating before you buy.

**Can I install plug-in lights myself?**
Yes. Lamps, plug-in LED strips and plug-pack garden lights that plug into an existing power point aren't fixed wiring, so they're fine to install yourself.

**How much does it cost to have a ceiling fan installed?**
Beacon Lighting's published prices start at about $285 to fit a fan on existing wiring and $350 for a new install with its switch, more for high ceilings or new wiring. See [replacing a light with a ceiling fan](/guides/diy-legal/replace-a-light-with-a-ceiling-fan/) for what adds to the price.
