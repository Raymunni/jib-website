Black grout and mouldy silicone make a clean bathroom look dirty. Replacing them is one of the most satisfying weekend jobs there is, and it's legal DIY everywhere.

## Regrout or waterproofing problem?

| What you see | Likely problem | Who fixes it |
|---|---|---|
| Stained or mouldy grout, cracked silicone | Worn grout and sealant | <span class="v ok">DIY</span> |
| Crumbling or missing grout | Worn grout | <span class="v ok">DIY</span> |
| Loose or hollow-sounding (drummy) tiles | Water behind the tiles | <span class="v maybe">Get it checked</span> |
| Stains, peeling paint or swollen skirting on the other side of the wall | Failed waterproofing | <span class="v no">Waterproofer or builder</span> |

If you're seeing the last two, see [can I waterproof my own bathroom?](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-waterproof-my-own-bathroom/) before spending a weekend on grout.

## Replacing shower silicone

1. **Cut out the old silicone** with a sharp blade or silicone removal tool, then pick out the rest.
2. **Clean off mould and residue.** Mild detergent or diluted white vinegar works for most mould. Let it dry completely, ideally 24 hours with the fan running.
3. **Mask both sides** of the joint with painter's tape for a clean line.
4. **Apply a wet-area (bathroom) silicone** in one steady bead.
5. **Smooth it** with a wet finger or a profile tool, then remove the tape straight away.
6. **Leave it to cure** before using the shower, usually 24 hours (check the tube).

## Regrouting tiles

1. **Rake out the old grout** with a grout saw or multi-tool, taking care not to chip the tiles.
2. **Vacuum and clean** the joints, and let them dry.
3. **Mix and press in new grout** with a rubber float, working diagonally across the joints.
4. **Wipe off the excess** with a damp sponge before it hardens, then polish off the haze once it's dry.
5. **Seal the grout** once cured if the product recommends it.

Don't grout the corners and the joint where the wall meets the floor or bath: those move, so they get silicone, not grout.

## Keeping mould away

Run the exhaust fan during and after every shower, squeegee the walls, and fix leaks quickly. For stubborn mould, see [how to get rid of mould in the bathroom](/guides/how-to/how-to-get-rid-of-mould-in-the-bathroom/).
