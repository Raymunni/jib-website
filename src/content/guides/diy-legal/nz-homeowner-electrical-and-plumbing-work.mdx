New Zealand has a genuine homeowner exemption for electrical work — something no Australian state has. But it comes with conditions, and plumbing and gas are much tighter.

## Electrical: the homeowner exemption

**Who can use it:** you must own the home and live in it, the work must be on a low-voltage installation, and you can't be paid for it. Landlords, tenants and employees can't use the exemption.

**What you can do without an inspection** (per WorkSafe NZ):

- remove and replace switches, socket outlets, permanent connection units, light fittings, batten holders, cord-grip lampholders and ceiling roses
- replace water-heater switches and thermostats
- remove and replace fuse links
- connect and disconnect fixed-wired appliances
- move existing switches, sockets and lighting outlets wired with TPS cable

**What must be inspected:** if you install, extend or alter sub-circuits, you can't connect the work to the supply yourself. A licensed electrical inspector must test and connect it — WorkSafe recommends talking to the inspector before you start.

**Never:** work on the switchboard, other than replacing fuse wire or cartridges.

## Plumbing, gas and drains

The Plumbers, Gasfitters and Drainlayers Board sets out what homeowners can do:

| Work | Homeowner? |
|---|---|
| Install appliances such as dishwashers and washing machines | <span class="v ok">Yes</span> |
| Replace or repair taps, ball cocks and plugs (including tap washers) | <span class="v ok">Yes</span> |
| Fixing or unfixing pipes, fixtures, traps, waste, soil, vent or water supply pipes | <span class="v no">Licensed plumber</span> |
| Portable gas appliances with the bottle attached (BBQ, patio heater) | <span class="v ok">Yes</span> |
| Fixing or unfixing gas pipes or flues, or commissioning | <span class="v no">Licensed gasfitter</span> |
| Clearing a blocked drain (without altering the inspection or vent pipe) | <span class="v ok">Yes</span> |
| Installing, altering or repairing a drain | <span class="v no">Licensed drainlayer</span> |

## Building consent is a separate question

Schedule 1 of the Building Act lets some plumbing and drainage repairs and replacements happen **without a building consent** — but only when an authorised (licensed) person does the work. It removes the need for consent, not the need for a licence.

## Compared with Australia

Across the Tasman, replacing a switch or socket is licensed electrical work in every state, and the plumbing exemptions are narrower. See [what you can legally DIY in Australia](/guides/diy-legal/).
