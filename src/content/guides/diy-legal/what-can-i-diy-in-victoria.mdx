Victoria has some of the clearest guidance in the country: Energy Safe Victoria publishes a “don’t do it yourself” list for electrical work, and the plumbing regulations spell out the minor jobs owners can do.

We checked 23 of the most common home jobs against Victoria’s regulators. Each row says whether you can do it yourself, and why.

## The quick list

**You can DIY:** change a light globe; replace a battery-only smoke alarm; plug an appliance into an existing power point; change a tap washer or jumper valve; replace a screw-on showerhead; clear a blocked sink or drain with a plunger; patch a hole in plasterboard; regrout tiles or replace shower silicone.

**Needs a licensed tradie:** replace or add a power point; replace a light switch; replace a light fitting or downlight; install a ceiling fan; install a hardwired smoke alarm; connect a hardwired oven, cooktop or rangehood; replace a whole tap or mixer; replace a toilet suite; connect a dishwasher to water and drain; replace a hot water system; install or move a gas appliance or bayonet.

**Check with the regulator first:** replace the plug on an appliance or extension lead; replace washers inside a toilet cistern; plug a portable gas heater into an existing bayonet; waterproof your own bathroom (membrane).

| Replace a whole tap or mixer ([guide](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-replace-a-tap-myself/)) | <span class="v no">Licensed tradie</span> | Replacing a whole tap or mixer means disconnecting and reconnecting the water supply, licensed plumbing work. The homeowner exemptions cover repair only. |
| Replace a screw-on showerhead ([guide](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-replace-a-tap-myself/)) | <span class="v ok">DIY OK</span> | Changing over a water-efficient showerhead is exempt from Victoria's plumbing licensing rules. |
| Replace washers inside a toilet cistern ([guide](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-replace-a-toilet-myself/)) | <span class="v maybe">Check first</span> | We couldn't confirm a homeowner exemption from your regulator, treat it as licensed work unless they confirm otherwise. |
| Replace a toilet suite ([guide](/guides/diy-legal/can-i-replace-a-toilet-myself/)) | <span class="v no">Licensed tradie</span> | Replacing a toilet suite involves the water supply and the sewer connection, licensed plumbing work in every state. |

## Gas

Gasfitting is licensed everywhere. Using an existing bayonet is what it’s designed for; installing one isn’t DIY.

| Job | In VIC | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Install or move a gas appliance or bayonet ([guide](/guides/diy-legal/)) | <span class="v no">Licensed tradie</span> | In Victoria, installing, moving or replacing a gas appliance needs a licensed gasfitter and always needs a compliance certificate. |
| Plug a portable gas heater into an existing bayonet ([guide](/guides/diy-legal/)) | <span class="v maybe">Check first</span> | Plugging a portable appliance into an existing bayonet is what bayonets are designed for. Installing the bayonet is licensed gasfitting. Some states restrict unflued gas heaters, check the heater is legal to use where you live. |

Finishing work is generally yours to do, as long as it doesn’t touch the plumbing, wiring or waterproofing.

## Rules specific to Victoria

These are the jobs where Victoria’s regulator has said something specific:

- **Replace or add a power point:** Energy Safe Victoria: electrical work isn't a DIY job, even for small jobs such as changing power points or light switches.
- **Replace a light switch:** Energy Safe Victoria names changing light switches as work that isn't DIY.
- **Change a tap washer or jumper valve:** Tap washers and other minor tap repairs in a home are exempt from Victoria's plumbing licensing rules.
- **Replace a screw-on showerhead:** Changing over a water-efficient showerhead is exempt from Victoria's plumbing licensing rules.
- **Install or move a gas appliance or bayonet:** In Victoria, installing, moving or replacing a gas appliance needs a licensed gasfitter and always needs a compliance certificate.
- **Waterproof your own bathroom (membrane):** Domestic building work on your own home over $20,000 needs an owner-builder certificate of consent. Must meet the building code.
