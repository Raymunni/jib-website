Queensland is one of the more DIY-friendly states for small plumbing repairs, thanks to the QBCC’s list of “unregulated” work. Electrical work is a different story: the Electrical Safety Office is clear that almost anything beyond changing a globe is for a licensed electrician.

We checked 23 of the most common home jobs against Queensland’s regulators. Each row says whether you can do it yourself, and why.

## The quick list

**You can DIY:** change a light globe; replace a battery-only smoke alarm; plug an appliance into an existing power point; change a tap washer or jumper valve; replace a screw-on showerhead; replace washers inside a toilet cistern; clear a blocked sink or drain with a plunger; patch a hole in plasterboard; regrout tiles or replace shower silicone.

**Needs a licensed tradie:** replace or add a power point; replace a light switch; replace a light fitting or downlight; install a ceiling fan; install a hardwired smoke alarm; connect a hardwired oven, cooktop or rangehood; replace the plug on an appliance or extension lead; replace a whole tap or mixer; replace a toilet suite; connect a dishwasher to water and drain; replace a hot water system; install or move a gas appliance or bayonet.

**Check with the regulator first:** plug a portable gas heater into an existing bayonet; waterproof your own bathroom (membrane).

## Rules specific to Queensland

These are the jobs where Queensland’s regulator has said something specific:

- **Replace a battery-only smoke alarm:** Photoelectric alarms powered by a 10-year non-removable battery can be installed yourself. From 1 January 2027 every Queensland home needs interconnected photoelectric alarms in each bedroom, connecting hallways and every storey.
- **Replace or add a power point:** Queensland's Electrical Safety Office lists installing a power point as licensed electrical work. Fines for individuals can reach $40,000.
- **Replace a light switch:** Queensland's Electrical Safety Office lists replacing a switch as licensed electrical work.
- **Replace a light fitting or downlight:** Queensland's Electrical Safety Office lists replacing a batten holder with a light fitting as licensed electrical work.
- **Install a ceiling fan:** Queensland's Electrical Safety Office lists replacing a light fitting with a ceiling fan as licensed electrical work.
- **Connect a hardwired oven, cooktop or rangehood:** Queensland's Electrical Safety Office: hardwired appliances must be connected by a licensed electrician. Fitting a wall oven into its cabinet without connecting it is not electrical work.
- **Replace the plug on an appliance or extension lead:** Queensland's Electrical Safety Office lists replacing a plug on the end of an extension lead, and constructing an extension lead, as licensed electrical work.
- **Change a tap washer or jumper valve:** Replacing a jumper valve or washer in a tap is on the QBCC's list of unregulated plumbing work.
- **Replace a screw-on showerhead:** Replacing a shower head is on the QBCC's unregulated plumbing work list.
- **Replace washers inside a toilet cistern:** Repairing or replacing the drop-valve washer, float-valve washer or suction-cup rubber in a toilet cistern is unregulated work.
- **Clear a blocked sink or drain with a plunger:** Plunging is normal maintenance, and cleaning a ground-level trap grate is on the QBCC's unregulated list. Anything beyond the grate needs a plumber.
- **Waterproof your own bathroom (membrane):** Owners can do work on their own property valued up to $11,000 without a licence; above that needs an owner-builder permit. Must meet the building code.
